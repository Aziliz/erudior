## Colorado's brokerage relationships

| Relationship | Represents | Created by |
|---|---|---|
| Seller's (landlord's) agent | The seller only, as an advocate | Written agreement |
| Buyer's (tenant's) agent | The buyer only, as an advocate | Written agreement |
| Transaction-broker | Nobody; assists one or both parties | **Presumed** unless there's a written single-agency agreement |
| Customer | Not a relationship: a party who hasn't engaged the broker | |
| Dual agent | **Not allowed in Colorado**, even with consent | |

## Disclosure timing

- A written disclosure of the relationship is required **before the broker elicits or discusses confidential information**. Small talk about price range, area and style of home isn't confidential.
- Single agency needs a written agreement **before** the broker acts as that agent.
- The Commission's Definitions of Working Relationships form is a separate rule: it's required when a party asks about a relationship the firm doesn't offer.
- **Change of status** (for example, a buyer's agent who will now work with their own seller-client on the same deal): written disclosure no later than when the party signs the contract.

## Designated brokerage

The employing broker can designate, in writing, specific brokers to work with a party. The relationship is with those brokers only, not the whole firm, so two designated brokers in one firm can represent opposite sides without either being a dual agent.

## Duties side by side

| Duty | Single agent | Transaction-broker |
|---|---|---|
| Reasonable skill and care | Yes | Yes |
| Present offers and counteroffers in a timely way | Yes | Yes |
| Account for money and property | Yes | Yes |
| Counsel the party on material benefits and risks of the transaction | Yes | No |
| Keep the parties fully informed about the transaction | Not a separately listed duty | Yes |
| Disclose adverse material facts actually known | Yes | Yes |
| Advocate for the party, promote their interests | **Yes** | No |
| Negotiate for the best terms for one side | **Yes** | No |
| Keep confidential a party's motivation and willingness to take less or pay more | Yes, unless the client gives informed consent | Yes, unless **all** parties give informed consent |
| Independently inspect or verify | Not required | Not required |
| Duties that survive after the relationship ends | Accounting for money and property, and confidentiality | Accounting for money and property only |

> **Exam tip:** A seller's agent need not disclose to a buyer that the property was the site of a homicide, felony or suicide, or that an occupant had HIV/AIDS. Colorado law says these aren't material facts (C.R.S. 38-35.5-101).

## Compensation

- Commissions aren't set by law and are fully negotiable.
- Payment alone doesn't create an agency relationship. Compensation can come from either party, a third party, or be split.
- Parties must be told about compensation arrangements before signing the brokerage agreement or the contract.
- A buyer's agent needs the buyer's written approval before asking to be paid from the seller's side.
