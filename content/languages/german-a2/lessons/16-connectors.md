## What this lesson is for

Your German sentences are getting longer. You can join ideas with *und, aber, denn* (A1) and with *weil, dass, wenn, ob* (lessons 3–4). This lesson adds a third group: **linking adverbs** such as *deshalb* (that's why), *trotzdem* (anyway, nevertheless), *außerdem* (besides) and *sonst* (otherwise). They have their own word order, and mixing up the three groups is one of the most common mistakes at A2. So we'll put all three side by side, add *sondern* after a negative, and finish with the order of time, manner and place in the middle of a sentence.

The theme is the weather and the environment, which gives plenty to argue about: *Es regnet, trotzdem fahre ich mit dem Fahrrad.*

## Linking adverbs: verb straight after

A linking adverb is not a conjunction. It is a normal part of the sentence, like *heute* or *dann*, so when it stands in position 1 the verb comes next, in position 2, and the subject follows the verb. You saw this with *deshalb* at the end of A1.

| Position 1 | Verb (position 2) | Subject | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Es ist sehr kalt.* *Deshalb* | *bleiben* | *wir* | *zu Hause.* |
| *Es regnet.* *Trotzdem* | *gehe* | *ich* | *spazieren.* |
| *Das alte Auto verbraucht viel Energie.* *Außerdem* | *ist* | *es* | *zu laut.* |
| *Zieh eine Jacke an,* *sonst* | *frierst* | *du.* | |

What each one means:

- *deshalb* — that's why, so, therefore (a result): *Ich spare Strom, deshalb zahle ich weniger.* — I save electricity, so I pay less.
- *trotzdem* — anyway, all the same, nevertheless (something happens although you wouldn't expect it): *Es war neblig. Trotzdem sind wir losgefahren.* — It was foggy. We set off anyway.
- *außerdem* — besides, in addition, also (one more point): *Fahrradfahren ist gesund. Außerdem schützt es die Umwelt.* — Cycling is healthy. Besides, it protects the environment.
- *sonst* — otherwise, or else (what happens if not): *Wir müssen Energie sparen, sonst wird es teuer.* — We have to save energy, otherwise it'll be expensive.
- *dann* and *danach* — then, afterwards (sequence), which you know from A1: *Zuerst trennen wir den Müll, danach bringen wir ihn raus.*

The adverb doesn't have to come first. It can also stand after the verb, in the middle: *Wir bleiben deshalb zu Hause. Ich gehe trotzdem spazieren.* The meaning is the same; position 1 just gives it more weight.

### A memory hook

Think of the sentence as a train with the verb as the engine always in carriage 2. Conjunctions like *und* and *aber* are the coupling between two trains: they don't take a seat. Linking adverbs are passengers: if they sit in carriage 1, the subject has to move behind the engine.

## sondern: not this, but that

You met *sondern* in A1. It means "but" in the sense of "but rather" and comes only after a negative (*nicht, kein, nie*), when the second part corrects the first. *aber* means "but" in the sense of "however".

- *Morgen wird es nicht sonnig, sondern bewölkt.* — Tomorrow it won't be sunny but cloudy.
- *Das ist kein Nebel, sondern Rauch.* — That's not fog, it's smoke. (*der Rauch* = smoke)
- *Es ist nicht warm, aber es ist sonnig.* — It isn't warm, but it is sunny. (both true: *aber*)

Test: if you can say "not X, but rather Y", use *sondern*. Like *aber*, *sondern* doesn't change the word order.

## Three ways to join sentences

Here are the three groups together. Learn which word belongs to which group; the word order follows automatically.

| Group | Words | Word order | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **coordinating conjunctions** (position 0) | *und, aber, oder, denn, sondern* | normal: verb in position 2 | *Ich bleibe zu Hause, denn es regnet.* |
| **subordinating conjunctions** | *weil, dass, wenn, ob, als* | verb at the end | *Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil es regnet.* |
| linking adverbs (position 1) | *deshalb, trotzdem, außerdem, sonst, dann, danach* | verb straight after, then subject | *Es regnet, deshalb bleibe ich zu Hause.* |

The same idea with a conjunction and with an adverb:

- *Es ist heiß, aber ich trinke einen heißen Tee.* — It's hot, but I'm drinking a hot tea.
- *Es ist heiß. Trotzdem trinke ich einen heißen Tee.* — It's hot. I'm drinking a hot tea anyway.

And a reason versus a result:

- *Ich fahre mit dem Rad, weil ich die Umwelt schützen will.* — reason (*weil*, verb at the end)
- *Ich will die Umwelt schützen, deshalb fahre ich mit dem Rad.* — result (*deshalb*, verb straight after)

Notice that *weil* gives the reason and *deshalb* introduces the result. They look at the same facts from opposite ends.

## Time, manner, place

When a sentence has several pieces of information in the middle, German likes this order: when (time) → how (manner) → where (place). German teachers call the full version *te-ka-mo-lo*: *temporal, kausal, modal, lokal* (when, why, how, where).

- *Ich fahre morgen mit dem Zug nach München.* — I'm going to Munich by train tomorrow.
- *Wir sind am Sonntag mit Freunden im Wald gewandert.* — We hiked in the forest with friends on Sunday.
- *Sie geht jeden Tag zu Fuß zur Arbeit.* — She walks to work every day.

English usually does it the other way round ("to Munich by train tomorrow"), so this needs practice.

## Weather and environment words

You know *das Wetter, die Sonne, der Regen, der Schnee, der Wind, warm, kalt, heiß, kühl* from A1. Now you can be more precise.

| Noun | Adjective | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *die Sonne* | *sonnig* — sunny | *Am Wochenende wird es sonnig.* |
| *die Wolke* — cloud | *bewölkt* — cloudy | *Der Himmel ist bewölkt.* |
| *der Wind* | *windig* — windy | *An der Küste ist es oft windig.* |
| *der Nebel* — fog | *neblig* — foggy | *Morgens ist es neblig.* |
| *der Regen* | *nass* — wet | *Meine Schuhe sind ganz nass.* |
| — | *trocken* — dry | *Der Sommer war sehr trocken.* |

More: *das Gewitter* (thunderstorm), *der Sturm* (storm, gale), *die Hitze* (heat), *das Eis* (ice), *frieren* (to be cold, to freeze), *der Himmel* (sky), *das Klima* (climate). Temperatures use *der Grad* (degree), which doesn't change after a number: *Es sind heute 30 Grad.* — It's 30 degrees today. *In der Nacht hatten wir minus fünf Grad.* — In the night it was minus five. *Die Temperatur* is the temperature.

For the environment: *die Umwelt* (environment), *der Umweltschutz* (environmental protection), *schützen* (to protect), *die Energie* and *der Strom* (energy, electricity), *sparen* (to save), *verbrauchen* (to consume, use up), *den Müll trennen* (to separate the rubbish) and *recyceln* (to recycle). In Germany people sort their rubbish into several bins: paper, plastic packaging, glass, and the rest.

## Text: A weather forecast and a reply

> Das Wetter für morgen: Im Norden ist es bewölkt und windig, an der Küste gibt es am Nachmittag Regen. Die Temperaturen liegen bei 12 bis 15 Grad. Im Süden scheint am Vormittag die Sonne, danach kommen von Westen Wolken und am Abend sind Gewitter möglich. In den Alpen kann es in der Nacht unter null Grad gehen, dort gibt es Eis auf den Straßen.

> Paula: Morgen soll es in München Gewitter geben. Wollen wir trotzdem wandern gehen?
>
> Felix: Nur am Vormittag. Wir starten früh, sonst kommen wir in den Regen.
>
> Paula: Gute Idee. Und wir fahren nicht mit dem Auto, sondern mit dem Zug. Das ist billiger, außerdem ist es besser für die Umwelt.
>
> Felix: Einverstanden. Ich nehme eine Jacke mit, denn in den Bergen ist es immer kälter.
>
> Paula: Und ich packe Brötchen ein. Dann müssen wir unterwegs nichts kaufen.

In English:

> The weather for tomorrow: In the north it'll be cloudy and windy, with rain on the coast in the afternoon. Temperatures will be around 12 to 15 degrees. In the south the sun will shine in the morning; after that, clouds will come in from the west and thunderstorms are possible in the evening. In the Alps it may drop below zero overnight, and there will be ice on the roads.

> Paula: There are supposed to be thunderstorms in Munich tomorrow. Shall we still go hiking?
>
> Felix: Only in the morning. We'll start early, otherwise we'll get caught in the rain.
>
> Paula: Good idea. And we won't go by car but by train. That's cheaper, and besides it's better for the environment.
>
> Felix: Agreed. I'll take a jacket, because it's always colder in the mountains.
>
> Paula: And I'll pack some rolls. Then we won't have to buy anything on the way.

(*Es soll … geben* = there is supposed to be, according to the forecast; *einverstanden* = agreed; *einpacken* = to pack.) Notice *trotzdem*, *sonst*, *außerdem* and *dann* each followed directly by the verb, while *sondern* and *denn* keep the normal order.

## Pronunciation

- *trotzdem*: stress on the first syllable, TROTZ-dem; the *tz* is a sharp "ts".
- *außerdem*: AU-ßer-dem, stress at the start; *ß* is a sharp *s*.
- *deshalb*: DES-halb, and the final *b* sounds like *p*.
- *Gewitter*: ge-WIT-ter, stress on the middle.
- *recyceln*: pronounced as in English, "ree-SAI-keln"; *ich recycle* sounds like "ree-SAIK-le".
- *Umwelt*: UM-welt, the *w* is an English *v*.

## Common mistakes

- *Deshalb ich bleibe zu Hause.* — After a linking adverb in position 1, the verb comes next: *Deshalb bleibe ich zu Hause.*
- *Trotzdem ich gehe spazieren.* — Same rule: *Trotzdem gehe ich spazieren.*
- *…, weil es regnet nicht.* / *…, deshalb ich bleibe …* — mixing the groups. *weil* sends the verb to the end; *deshalb* pulls it forward.
- *Das ist nicht kalt, aber warm.* — After a negative that is corrected, use *sondern*: *nicht kalt, sondern warm*.
- *Ich fahre nach München morgen mit dem Zug.* — English order. Time first: *morgen mit dem Zug nach München*.
- *30 Grade* — after a number, *Grad* has no ending: *30 Grad*.
- Confusing *trotzdem* and *deshalb*: *deshalb* = as expected; *trotzdem* = against expectation.

## Key points

- Linking adverbs (*deshalb, trotzdem, außerdem, sonst, dann, danach*) are part of the sentence: in position 1 they're followed directly by the verb, then the subject.
- *deshalb* = result, *trotzdem* = against expectation, *außerdem* = an extra point, *sonst* = otherwise.
- *und, aber, oder, denn, sondern* don't change the order; *weil, dass, wenn, ob, als* send the verb to the end.
- *sondern* corrects a negative: *nicht …, sondern …*
- In the middle field: time → manner → place.
- Temperatures: *Es sind 20 Grad*; weather adjectives *sonnig, bewölkt, windig, neblig, nass, trocken*.
