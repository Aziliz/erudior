## What this lesson is for

Module 1 was about your own story. This module turns to people: how they get ready, how they feel, how they get on with each other, what they give each other and what they are like. Almost every one of those sentences needs a small word you have already met in chunks like *Ich freue mich*: the pronoun *sich* and its relatives.

In this lesson you learn how **reflexive verbs** work: verbs whose object is the same person as the subject, like English "I hurt myself" or "enjoy yourself". German has many more of them than English, so learn each verb together with its *sich*. By the end you can describe your morning routine and say how you feel, in the present, the perfect and with modal verbs.

## Doing something to yourself

Compare these two sentences:

- *Ich wasche das Auto.* — I'm washing the car.
- *Ich wasche mich.* — I'm washing (myself).

In the second sentence, the person washing and the person being washed are the same: the object points back at the subject. The word that points back is a **reflexive pronoun**, here *mich*. English often leaves "myself" out ("I'm washing", "I'm getting dressed"), but German almost never can: *Ich wasche* on its own sounds unfinished, because *waschen* needs an object.

### The accusative reflexive pronouns

| subject | reflexive (accusative) | example |
|---|---|---|
| *ich* | *mich* | *Ich ziehe mich an.* — I get dressed. |
| *du* | *dich* | *Du ziehst dich an.* |
| *er / sie / es* | *sich* | *Er zieht sich an.* |
| *wir* | *uns* | *Wir ziehen uns an.* |
| *ihr* | *euch* | *Ihr zieht euch an.* |
| *sie / Sie* | *sich* | *Sie ziehen sich an.* |

Only one form is new. *mich, dich, uns, euch* are the accusative pronouns from A1. The new word *sich* covers every third person (he, she, it, they) and formal *Sie*, and it is never capitalised: *Setzen Sie sich bitte* (please sit down). A memory hook: *sich* is for everyone you talk *about*, and for *Sie*.

### Where does the reflexive pronoun go?

In a normal statement the reflexive pronoun comes straight after the conjugated verb, as early as possible:

- *Ich rasiere mich jeden Morgen.* — I shave every morning.
- *Morgens rasiere ich mich.* — In the mornings I shave.
- *Rasierst du dich jeden Tag?* — Do you shave every day?

If a pronoun subject follows the verb, it comes before *sich*: *Heute kämme ich mich nicht.* A noun subject usually comes after *sich*: *Morgen ruht sich mein Vater aus.* — Tomorrow my father is having a rest.

In a subordinate clause (lesson 3), the conjugated verb goes to the end, but *sich* stays near the front, right after the subject: *Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil ich mich nicht wohlfühle.* — I'm staying at home because I don't feel well.

## Getting ready: verbs of daily care

Here are the verbs you need for the bathroom and the bedroom. Several are separable, so the prefix goes to the end as usual and *sich* stays near the verb.

| German | English |
|---|---|
| *sich waschen* | to wash (yourself), have a wash |
| *sich duschen* | to have a shower (also simply *duschen*) |
| *sich anziehen* | to get dressed |
| *sich ausziehen* | to get undressed |
| *sich umziehen* | to get changed |
| *sich rasieren* | to shave |
| *sich kämmen* | to comb your hair |
| *sich schminken* | to put on make-up |
| *sich beeilen* | to hurry |

- *Ich stehe auf, ich dusche, dann ziehe ich mich an.* — I get up, have a shower, then I get dressed.
- *Nach der Arbeit ziehe ich mich um.* — After work I get changed.
- *Die Kinder ziehen sich schnell aus und gehen ins Bett.* — The children get undressed quickly and go to bed.
- *Beeil dich! Der Bus kommt.* — Hurry up! The bus is coming.

Careful with *umziehen*. In A1 it meant to move house: *Wir ziehen nach Hamburg um.* With *sich* it means to change your clothes: *Ich ziehe mich um.* The pronoun changes the meaning, which is why it matters so much.

The things you use: *der Spiegel* (mirror), *die Zahnbürste* (toothbrush), *die Zahnpasta* (toothpaste), *die Seife* (soap), *das Shampoo*, *das Handtuch* (towel), *der Kamm* (comb) and *die Creme* (cream) for *die Haut* (skin).

## The dative reflexive: washing your hands

What happens when you wash not yourself but your hands? German says "I wash to myself the hands":

- *Ich wasche mir die Hände.* — I wash my hands.
- *Ich putze mir die Zähne.* — I brush my teeth.
- *Er kämmt sich die Haare.* — He combs his hair.

Now there are two objects. *die Hände* is the thing being washed (accusative), so the pronoun moves to the dative: *mir* instead of *mich*. Only *ich* and *du* look different in the dative; all the others are the same as the accusative forms:

| subject | accusative | dative |
|---|---|---|
| *ich* | *mich* | *mir* |
| *du* | *dich* | *dir* |
| *er / sie / es* | *sich* | *sich* |
| *wir* | *uns* | *uns* |
| *ihr* | *euch* | *euch* |
| *sie / Sie* | *sich* | *sich* |

The rule is simple: if the sentence already has an accusative object (the hands, the teeth, a jacket), the reflexive pronoun is dative. If not, it is accusative.

- *Ich ziehe mich an.* — I get dressed. (no other object: accusative)
- *Ich ziehe mir eine Jacke an.* — I put on a jacket. (*eine Jacke* is the object: dative)
- *Du wäschst dich.* / *Du wäschst dir das Gesicht.* — You have a wash. / You wash your face.

Notice that German does not say "my hands" or "my teeth" here. The dative pronoun already tells you whose hands they are, so the article is enough: *Ich putze mir die Zähne*, not ~~Ich putze meine Zähne~~ (which is understandable but sounds like you are cleaning someone else's set of teeth).

## Feelings and states

Many verbs about feelings and health are reflexive in German, even when English uses "to be" + adjective or "to get". This is the group you simply have to learn with *sich*:

| German | English |
|---|---|
| *sich fühlen* | to feel |
| *sich wohlfühlen* | to feel well, feel comfortable, feel at home |
| *sich freuen* | to be pleased, be glad |
| *sich ärgern* | to be annoyed, get angry |
| *sich aufregen* | to get worked up, get upset |
| *sich langweilen* | to be bored |
| *sich ausruhen* | to have a rest |
| *sich entspannen* | to relax |
| *sich erholen* | to recover, get some rest |
| *sich konzentrieren* | to concentrate |
| *sich verletzen* | to hurt yourself, get injured |
| *sich erkälten* | to catch a cold |

- *Wie fühlst du dich heute?* — How are you feeling today?
- *Ich fühle mich in meiner Wohnung sehr wohl.* — I feel very at home in my flat.
- *Ich freue mich!* — I'm so pleased!
- *Mein Chef ärgert sich, weil der Drucker schon wieder kaputt ist.* — My boss is annoyed because the printer is broken again.
- *Reg dich nicht auf!* — Don't get worked up!
- *Im Urlaub erholen wir uns am Meer.* — On holiday we get some rest by the sea.
- *Die Kinder langweilen sich.* — The children are bored.

Two pairs are easy to confuse. *langweilig* (A1) describes the boring thing; *sich langweilen* is what you do when something is boring: *Der Film ist langweilig. Ich langweile mich.* And *sich ausruhen* is a short rest (on the sofa, for an hour), while *sich erholen* is getting your strength back over days or weeks, after an illness or a hard year.

*sich wohlfühlen* is a separable verb: *Ich fühle mich wohl*, perfect *Ich habe mich wohlgefühlt*. You will also see it written as two words, *wohl fühlen*; both spellings are correct.

## Reflexive verbs in the perfect and with modals

Reflexive verbs form the perfect with *haben*, without exception, even when the action is a movement or a change of state. The reflexive pronoun stays after *haben*, and the participle goes to the end:

- *Ich habe mich beeilt.* — I hurried.
- *Hast du dich erkältet?* — Have you caught a cold?
- *Er hat sich beim Fußball verletzt.* — He hurt himself playing football.
- *Wir haben uns im Urlaub gut erholt.* — We had a good rest on holiday.
- *Sie hat sich schnell umgezogen.* — She got changed quickly.
- *Ich habe mich sehr über dein Geschenk gefreut.* — I was very pleased about your present.

Look at the participles. *anziehen* → *angezogen*, *umziehen* → *umgezogen* (separable, so *ge* goes in the middle). *rasieren* and *konzentrieren* end in *-ieren*, so no *ge-*: *rasiert*, *konzentriert*. *verletzen*, *erkälten* and *erholen* have inseparable prefixes, so no *ge-* either: *verletzt*, *erkältet*, *erholt*.

With a modal verb, the modal is in position 2, the reflexive pronoun follows it, and the infinitive goes to the end:

- *Du musst dich beeilen.* — You have to hurry.
- *Ich will mich heute nur ausruhen.* — Today I just want to rest.
- *Kannst du dich bitte konzentrieren?* — Can you concentrate, please?
- *Ich muss mir noch die Zähne putzen.* — I still have to brush my teeth.

## Reading: Ein ganz normaler Montag

> Montags ist bei uns immer Chaos. Mein Wecker klingelt um halb sieben, aber ich bleibe noch zehn Minuten liegen. Dann muss ich mich natürlich beeilen. Ich dusche schnell, putze mir die Zähne und kämme mich vor dem Spiegel. Rasieren? Keine Zeit, das mache ich am Abend.
>
> Meine Frau Julia ist schon fertig. Sie steht um sechs auf, macht Yoga und schminkt sich in aller Ruhe. „Wie machst du das?", frage ich sie jeden Montag. „Ich ärgere mich nicht über den Montag", sagt sie. „Ich freue mich einfach auf den Kaffee."
>
> Unser Sohn Leon ist zwölf. Er zieht sich am Morgen an wie ein Bär im Winter: langsam, langsam. Heute hat er sich zweimal umgezogen, weil ihm die Hose nicht gefallen hat. Und letzte Woche hat er sich beim Basketball verletzt, deshalb muss er sich jetzt in der Pause ausruhen. Er sagt, er langweilt sich.
>
> Um halb acht sitzen wir endlich alle im Auto. Ich fühle mich wie nach einem Marathon. Aber am Abend, wenn alle zu Hause sind, entspannen wir uns zusammen auf dem Sofa, und dann fühle ich mich wieder wohl.

In English:

> On Mondays it's always chaos at our place. My alarm clock rings at half past six, but I stay in bed for ten more minutes. Then of course I have to hurry. I have a quick shower, brush my teeth and comb my hair in front of the mirror. Shaving? No time, I'll do that in the evening.
>
> My wife Julia is already ready. She gets up at six, does yoga and puts on her make-up in peace and quiet. "How do you do it?" I ask her every Monday. "I don't get annoyed about Monday," she says. "I just look forward to the coffee."
>
> Our son Leon is twelve. In the morning he gets dressed like a bear in winter: slowly, slowly. Today he got changed twice because he didn't like his trousers. And last week he hurt himself playing basketball, so now he has to rest at break time. He says he gets bored.
>
> At half past seven we're finally all sitting in the car. I feel as if I've run a marathon. But in the evening, when everyone is at home, we relax together on the sofa, and then I feel good again.

Notes: *der Wecker* is an alarm clock; *in aller Ruhe* means calmly, without hurrying; *weil ihm die Hose nicht gefallen hat* uses *gefallen* with the dative, which lesson 7 explains. *deshalb* means "so, that's why" (lesson 16). Look at *ich putze mir die Zähne* (dative, because *die Zähne* is the object) next to *ich kämme mich* (accusative, no other object).

## Each other: sich, uns, euch

In the plural, the same pronouns can mean "each other". This is called the **reciprocal** use:

- *Wir sehen uns morgen.* — We'll see each other tomorrow. (See you tomorrow.)
- *Anna und Tom kennen sich seit der Schule.* — Anna and Tom have known each other since school.
- *Ihr trefft euch am Bahnhof, oder?* — You're meeting (each other) at the station, right?

## Pronunciation

- *sich*, *mich*, *dich*: the soft *ch* of *ich*, never a "k". *mir*, *dir*: long *i*, like "meer", "deer".
- *sich beeilen*: *be* and *ei* are separate syllables, be-EI-len, with the stress on *ei*.
- *sich rasieren*, *sich konzentrieren*: stress on *-ie-*: ra-SIE-ren, kon-zen-TRIE-ren.
- *sich erkälten*: er-KÄL-ten; *sich erholen*: er-HO-len, with a long *o*.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving out the pronoun: ~~Ich freue.~~ → *Ich freue mich.* ~~Beeil!~~ → *Beeil dich!*
- Using *sich* for *ich* or *du*: ~~Ich wasche sich.~~ → *Ich wasche mich.* *sich* is only for third person and *Sie*.
- Accusative when there's already an object: ~~Ich putze mich die Zähne.~~ → *Ich putze mir die Zähne.*
- Using a possessive with body parts: *Ich wasche meine Hände* is understood but unidiomatic; say *Ich wasche mir die Hände.*
- The perfect with *sein*: ~~Ich bin mich erkältet.~~ → *Ich habe mich erkältet.* Reflexive verbs always take *haben*.
- Copying English: "I'm bored" is not ~~Ich bin langweilig~~ (that means "I'm boring"!) but *Ich langweile mich*.
- Putting *sich* at the end: ~~Er hat beim Fußball verletzt sich.~~ → *Er hat sich beim Fußball verletzt.*

## Key points

- A reflexive pronoun points back to the subject: *mich, dich, sich, uns, euch, sich*. Only *sich* is new.
- Learn reflexive verbs with *sich*: *sich anziehen, sich beeilen, sich freuen, sich erkälten* …
- If the sentence has another accusative object, the pronoun is dative: *mir, dir*, the rest unchanged. *Ich putze mir die Zähne.*
- Body parts take *der/die/das*, not *mein*: *Ich wasche mir die Hände.*
- The pronoun stays near the conjugated verb; in the perfect *Ich habe mich …*, always with *haben*.
- In the plural, *uns, euch, sich* can mean "each other".
