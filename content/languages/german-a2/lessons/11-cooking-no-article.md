## What this lesson is for

You can now put an adjective after *ein* (lesson 9) and after *der* (lesson 10). But a lot of the time there's no article at all. You say "fresh bread", "hot water", "with cold milk", not "a fresh bread". In German that's when the adjective has the most work to do: with no article to show gender and case, the adjective shows it alone.

This lesson teaches that third and last table, shows how *viele, einige* and *ein paar* fit in, and then puts all three tables side by side. The theme is food: cooking at home, following a recipe and eating out.

## In the kitchen

| German | English |
|---|---|
| das Rezept | recipe |
| die Zutat | ingredient |
| der Topf | pot, saucepan |
| die Pfanne | frying pan |
| der Teller | plate |
| die Tasse | cup |
| der Löffel | spoon |
| die Gabel | fork |
| das Messer | knife |
| das Salz | salt |
| der Pfeffer | pepper |
| der Zucker | sugar |
| das Öl | oil |

The verbs of cooking are mostly regular, but three change their vowel or their perfect:

| | present (du / er) | perfect |
|---|---|---|
| braten — to fry, to roast | du brätst, er brät | hat gebraten |
| backen — to bake | du backst, er backt (also bäckst, bäckt) | hat gebacken |
| schneiden — to cut | du schneidest, er schneidet | hat geschnitten |
| schälen — to peel | regular | hat geschält |
| probieren — to taste, to try | regular | hat probiert |

*probieren* is "try" in the sense of tasting: *Probier mal die Suppe!* (Try the soup!). For clothes you use *anprobieren* from lesson 9. Two more cooking verbs are worth recognising: *mischen* (to mix) and *rühren* (to stir).

Food is *süß* (sweet), *sauer* (sour), *salzig* (salty) or *scharf* (spicy, hot). *scharf* also means "sharp", so *ein scharfes Messer* is a sharp knife and *eine scharfe Soße* a spicy sauce. *heiß* is only about temperature. *frisch* means fresh.

## Endings with no article

When there's no article, the adjective takes the ending that *der, die, das* would have had. It's the signal rule from lesson 9 at full strength: nothing else is there to show the case, so the adjective shows it.

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nominative | frischer Fisch | frische Milch | frisches Brot | frische Eier |
| accusative | frischen Fisch | frische Milch | frisches Brot | frische Eier |
| dative | frischem Fisch | frischer Milch | frischem Brot | frischen Eiern |

Compare each box with the definite article:

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nominative | der → -er | die → -e | das → -es | die → -e |
| accusative | den → -en | die → -e | das → -es | die → -e |
| dative | dem → -em | der → -er | dem → -em | den → -en |

So you don't need to learn a new table: you need the last letters of the article you already know. A memory hook: *der* gives *-er*, *das* gives *-es*, *dem* gives *-em*, *die* gives *-e*. The one place where English speakers slip is the plural: after *die* it's *-en* (*die frischen Eier*), but with no article it's *-e* (*frische Eier*).

Examples:

- *Frischer Fisch schmeckt am besten.* — Fresh fish tastes best.
- *Ich trinke gern kaltes Wasser.* — I like drinking cold water.
- *Wir brauchen braunen Zucker.* — We need brown sugar.
- *Sie trinkt Kaffee mit warmer Milch.* — She drinks coffee with warm milk.
- *Brot mit frischem Käse ist lecker.* — Bread with fresh cheese is delicious.
- *Kleine Kinder essen nicht gern scharfe Sachen.* — Small children don't like eating spicy things.

This happens most with mass nouns (*Wasser, Zucker, Milch*), with plurals, after *mit* and *aus*, and in signs and menus: *Frische Brötchen! Heißer Kaffee!*

## Numbers, viele, einige and ein paar

Numbers have no endings and don't count as an article, so the adjective after them takes the no-article ending:

- *zwei große Töpfe* — two big pots
- *mit drei kleinen Löffeln* — with three small spoons (dative plural, *-en*)

The same goes for *viele* (many), *einige* (some, a few) and *wenige* (few). They take the same endings as the adjective, and so does every adjective after them:

- *viele frische Zutaten* — lots of fresh ingredients
- *Einige alte Rezepte sind sehr einfach.* — Some old recipes are very simple.
- *mit vielen guten Freunden* — with lots of good friends

*ein paar* (a few, a couple of) never changes, and the adjective after it takes the no-article ending: *ein paar frische Eier*, *mit ein paar guten Ideen*.

Be careful with *alle*: it's a der-word, so the adjective after it ends in *-en*: *alle frischen Eier*. And *etwas* (some) and *viel* (a lot of) before a singular mass noun don't change either, so the adjective shows the ending: *mit etwas warmer Butter*, *viel kaltes Wasser*.

## A recipe: Grandma's pancakes

German recipes use the polite imperative (*Schneiden Sie …*) or, more often, just the infinitive at the end (*Die Eier aufschlagen*). Here is a family recipe written as a note.

> Omas Pfannkuchen (für 4 Personen)
>
> Zutaten: 250 Gramm Mehl, 3 frische Eier, ein halber Liter kalte Milch, eine Prise Salz, ein Löffel Zucker, etwas Öl für die Pfanne.
>
> Mehl, Salz und Zucker in eine große Schüssel geben. Die Eier und die Milch dazugeben und alles gut mischen. Den Teig zehn Minuten stehen lassen.
>
> Etwas Öl in einer schweren Pfanne heiß machen. Eine kleine Kelle Teig in die Pfanne geben und den Pfannkuchen auf beiden Seiten goldbraun braten.
>
> Mit frischem Obst oder mit Zucker und Zimt servieren. Mein Opa isst sie lieber salzig, mit gebratenem Speck. Probieren Sie beides!

In English:

> Grandma's pancakes (serves 4)
>
> Ingredients: 250 grams of flour, 3 fresh eggs, half a litre of cold milk, a pinch of salt, a spoonful of sugar, a little oil for the pan.
>
> Put the flour, salt and sugar in a large bowl. Add the eggs and the milk and mix everything well. Leave the batter to stand for ten minutes.
>
> Heat a little oil in a heavy frying pan. Put a small ladle of batter in the pan and fry the pancake on both sides until golden brown.
>
> Serve with fresh fruit or with sugar and cinnamon. My grandad prefers them savoury, with fried bacon. Try both!

Find the no-article endings: *frische Eier* (plural), *kalte Milch* (feminine), *mit frischem Obst* and *mit gebratenem Speck* (dative, *-em*). Compare *in einer schweren Pfanne* (lesson 9) and *eine große Schüssel*. *ein halber Liter* shows that *halb* is an adjective too. (*das Mehl* = flour, *die Schüssel* = bowl, *der Teig* = batter, dough, *die Kelle* = ladle, *der Zimt* = cinnamon, *der Speck* = bacon, *eine Prise* = a pinch.)

## In a restaurant

| German | English |
|---|---|
| die Speisekarte | menu |
| die Vorspeise | starter |
| die Hauptspeise | main course |
| der Nachtisch | dessert (also *die Nachspeise*) |
| bestellen | to order |
| die Rechnung | bill |
| das Trinkgeld | tip |

*bestellen* is inseparable: *Ich habe eine Suppe bestellt.* When you want to pay, say *Die Rechnung, bitte* or *Wir möchten bitte zahlen*. The waiter often asks *Zusammen oder getrennt?* (Together or separately?). In Germany you tip about five to ten per cent, and you say the total out loud as you pay: if the bill is 27 Euro, you give 30 and say *Dreißig, bitte* or *Stimmt so* (keep the change).

> Kellner: Haben Sie schon gewählt?
>
> Jana: Ja. Als Vorspeise nehme ich die Tomatensuppe. Ist sie scharf?
>
> Kellner: Nein, nur ein bisschen Pfeffer. Und als Hauptspeise?
>
> Jana: Den gebratenen Fisch mit grünem Salat, bitte.
>
> Tom: Für mich das Schnitzel mit Pommes. Und ein großes Glas kaltes Wasser, bitte.
>
> Kellner: Gern. Möchten Sie danach auch einen Nachtisch?
>
> Tom: Vielleicht später. Der Apfelkuchen mit warmer Vanillesoße klingt gut!

In English:

> Waiter: Have you decided?
>
> Jana: Yes. As a starter I'll have the tomato soup. Is it spicy?
>
> Waiter: No, just a bit of pepper. And for the main course?
>
> Jana: The fried fish with green salad, please.
>
> Tom: For me the schnitzel with chips. And a large glass of cold water, please.
>
> Waiter: Certainly. Would you like a dessert afterwards too?
>
> Tom: Maybe later. The apple cake with warm vanilla sauce sounds good!

*ein großes Glas kaltes Wasser*: *großes* follows *ein* (neuter, *-es*), and *kaltes Wasser* has no article (also *-es*). *mit grünem Salat* and *mit warmer Vanillesoße* are dative with no article. *den gebratenen Fisch* follows *den*. *gebraten* is the participle of *braten*, used as an adjective: participles take endings like any other adjective.

## All three tables side by side

| | masc. nom. | masc. acc. | fem. nom./acc. | neut. nom./acc. | dative (all) | plural nom./acc. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| after der-words | der gute | den guten | die gute | das gute | dem/der guten | die guten |
| after ein-words | ein guter | einen guten | eine gute | ein gutes | einem/einer guten | keine guten |
| no article | guter | guten | gute | gutes | gutem / guter, plural guten | gute |

Three questions decide every ending: Is there an article? Does the article show gender and case? If not, which letter would *der/die/das* end in? If the article shows it, the adjective ends in *-e* or *-en*. If the article doesn't, or there is none, the adjective shows it.

## Pronunciation

- *Löffel*: short *ö*, double *f*: LÖF-fel.
- *Zutat*: stress on the first part, TSOO-taht; *z* is always "ts".
- *Pfanne, Pfeffer*: say the *p* and the *f* together, a quick "pf". Don't drop the *p*.
- *Messer, Teller*: short *e*, as in English "mess" and "tell".
- *Rechnung*: the *ch* is the soft *ch* of *ich*.
- *Öl*: a long *ö*, made by saying "ay" with rounded lips.

## Common mistakes

- *mit warm Milch* — with no article the adjective must show the case: *mit warmer Milch*.
- *Ich trinke kalten Wasser.* — *Wasser* is neuter, and neuter accusative takes *-es*: *kaltes Wasser*.
- *frischen Eier* (as subject or object) — plural with no article is *-e*: *frische Eier*. It's *-en* only after *die* or in the dative.
- *alle frische Zutaten* — *alle* is a der-word: *alle frischen Zutaten*.
- *Die Suppe ist heiß* when you mean spicy — say *scharf*.
- *Ich habe die Kartoffeln geschneidet.* — The perfect of *schneiden* is *geschnitten*.
- *Er bratet den Fisch.* — *braten* has a vowel change: *er brät*.

## Key points

- With no article, the adjective takes the last letter(s) of *der/die/das/dem/den*: *-er, -e, -es, -em, -en*.
- Plural with no article: *-e* in the nominative and accusative, *-en* in the dative.
- Numbers and *ein paar* don't change; *viele, einige, wenige* take the same endings as the adjective after them; *alle* is a der-word.
- *braten* (er brät), *backen* (hat gebacken), *schneiden* (hat geschnitten).
- *scharf* = spicy or sharp; *probieren* = to taste; *Die Rechnung, bitte* and *Stimmt so*.
