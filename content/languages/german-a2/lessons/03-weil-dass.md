## What this lesson is for

At A1 you gave reasons with *denn*: *Ich bleibe zu Hause, denn ich bin krank.* That works, but in conversation Germans much more often say *weil*: *Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil ich krank bin.* Look at the end of that sentence: the verb *bin* has moved to the last place.

That small move is one of the most important patterns in German. It happens after *weil* (because), after *dass* (that) and after the words in lesson 4 (*wenn, als, ob*). Once you have it, you can give reasons, say what you think, hope and know, and talk about your feelings in longer, more natural sentences. That's exactly what A2 asks of you: not just "I like it", but "I like it because …" and "I think that …".

## Two kinds of clause

A clause is a group of words with its own verb. A **main clause** can stand alone as a sentence, and its verb is in position 2: *Ich bin krank.* A **subordinate clause** can't stand alone; it hangs on a main clause and adds information, such as a reason. In a German subordinate clause, the conjugated verb goes to the very end.

| main clause | , | subordinate clause (verb at the end) |
|---|---|---|
| Ich bleibe zu Hause | , | weil ich krank bin. |
| Ich glaube | , | dass sie heute kommt. |

Words like *weil* and *dass*, which start a subordinate clause and push the verb to the end, are called **subordinating conjunctions**. Words like *und, aber, oder, denn* and *sondern*, which just join two main clauses and change nothing, are **coordinating conjunctions**.

German always puts a comma between a main clause and a subordinate clause. In English you often don't, so this is a rule to watch when you write.

## weil: giving a reason

*weil* means "because". The subject usually comes right after *weil*, and the verb goes to the end.

- *Ich lerne Deutsch, weil meine Frau aus Österreich kommt.* — I'm learning German because my wife is from Austria.
- *Sie ist froh, weil sie die Prüfung bestanden hat.* — She's glad because she passed the exam.
- *Wir fahren nicht weg, weil wir kein Geld haben.* — We're not going away because we have no money.

### weil or denn?

Both mean "because", and you can usually use either. The difference is only word order:

| | word order | example |
|---|---|---|
| *denn* | main clause: verb in position 2 | *Ich gehe früh ins Bett, denn ich bin müde.* |
| *weil* | subordinate clause: verb at the end | *Ich gehe früh ins Bett, weil ich müde bin.* |

*weil* is more common in speech, and it's the only one you can use to answer a *warum* question on its own:

- *Warum lernst du so viel? – Weil ich nächste Woche eine Prüfung habe.* — Why are you studying so much? – Because I have an exam next week.

A *denn* sentence can't answer alone; it always follows a main clause.

## Two verbs at the end

If the subordinate clause has two verb parts, the conjugated one goes last of all. Everything else from A1 still applies; just pull the conjugated verb to the end.

### Separable verbs join up again

In a main clause a separable verb splits: *Ich stehe früh auf.* In a subordinate clause the conjugated verb moves to the end, right next to its prefix, and they're written as one word again:

- *Ich bin müde, weil ich jeden Tag um fünf aufstehe.* — I'm tired because I get up at five every day.
- *Er ist traurig, weil seine Freundin nicht anruft.* — He's sad because his girlfriend doesn't call.

### Modal verbs: the modal comes last

- *Ich kann nicht kommen, weil ich arbeiten muss.* — I can't come because I have to work.
- *Sie ist stolz, weil sie schon gut Deutsch sprechen kann.* — She's proud because she can already speak German well.

### The perfect: the auxiliary comes last

- *Ich bin enttäuscht, weil du nicht angerufen hast.* — I'm disappointed because you didn't call.
- *Er ist glücklich, weil er eine neue Stelle bekommen hat.* — He's happy because he's got a new job.
- *Wir sind spät gekommen, weil wir den Bus verpasst haben.* — We came late because we missed the bus.

So the order at the end is: participle or infinitive first, conjugated verb last: *angerufen hast, arbeiten muss, aufstehe*.

## dass: what you think, know and hope

*dass* means "that" and introduces what someone thinks, says, knows, hopes or feels. Like *weil*, it sends the verb to the end.

- *Ich glaube, dass er recht hat.* — I think (that) he's right.
- *Ich hoffe, dass das Wetter morgen schön ist.* — I hope (that) the weather is nice tomorrow.
- *Weißt du, dass Paula schwanger ist?* — Do you know that Paula is pregnant?
- *Ich finde, dass das Hotel zu teuer ist.* — I think (that) the hotel is too expensive.

Note *finden* here: besides "to find", it means "to think" when you give your opinion about something. *Wie findest du das Buch?* — What do you think of the book?

The most common verbs before *dass* are *denken, glauben, finden, meinen, hoffen, wissen, sagen*. Also common are adjectives with *sein*:

- *Es ist wichtig, dass du pünktlich bist.* — It's important that you're on time.
- *Ich bin froh, dass du da bist.* — I'm glad you're here.
- *Es ist schade, dass ihr schon fahrt.* — It's a pity that you're leaving already.

### Without dass

In English you can leave out "that", and in German, after verbs of thinking and saying, you can leave out *dass* too. But then the second part is a main clause again, with the verb in position 2:

| with dass | without dass |
|---|---|
| *Ich glaube, dass er krank ist.* | *Ich glaube, er ist krank.* |
| *Sie sagt, dass sie keine Zeit hat.* | *Sie sagt, sie hat keine Zeit.* |

Both are correct, and the version without *dass* is very common in speech. What you can't do is mix the two: *Ich glaube, dass er ist krank* is wrong.

Don't confuse *dass* with *das*. *das* (one *s*) is the article or "that" as a pronoun: *Das stimmt.* *dass* (double *s*) is the conjunction. A quick test: if you can replace it with *dies* or *welches*, it's *das*.

## Starting with the subordinate clause

You can put the subordinate clause first, for emphasis. Then the whole subordinate clause counts as position 1, so the verb of the main clause comes straight after the comma, and the subject follows it:

- *Weil ich krank bin, bleibe ich zu Hause.* — Because I'm ill, I'm staying at home.
- *Dass du gekommen bist, finde ich toll.* — I think it's great that you came.

The pattern is easy to remember as "verb, comma, verb": *… bin, bleibe …*. Saying *Weil ich krank bin, ich bleibe zu Hause* is a typical English-speaker mistake.

## Talking about opinions

| German | English |
|---|---|
| *Meiner Meinung nach ist das falsch.* | In my opinion that's wrong. |
| *Ich bin der Meinung, dass …* | I'm of the opinion that … |
| *Was meinst du?* | What do you think? |
| *Da hast du recht.* | You're right there. |
| *Das stimmt. / Das stimmt nicht.* | That's true. / That's not true. |
| *Ich stimme dir zu.* | I agree with you. |
| *Das ist mir egal.* | I don't mind. / I don't care. |
| *Das ist wahr.* | That's true. |
| *Sag mir bitte die Wahrheit.* | Please tell me the truth. |

*Meiner Meinung nach* counts as position 1, so the verb follows straight away: *Meiner Meinung nach ist …*. *zustimmen* (to agree) is separable and takes the dative: *Ich stimme dir zu. Stimmst du mir zu?* *recht haben* (to be right) uses *haben*, never *sein*: *Du hast recht.*

*unbedingt* means "absolutely" or "definitely", for something you really want or need: *Das musst du unbedingt sehen!* — You absolutely have to see that!

## Talking about feelings

| German | English |
|---|---|
| *froh, glücklich* | glad, happy |
| *zufrieden* | satisfied, content |
| *stolz (auf)* | proud (of) |
| *überrascht* | surprised |
| *traurig* | sad |
| *enttäuscht* | disappointed |
| *wütend* | furious, very angry |
| *nervös* | nervous |

*froh* is "glad about a situation"; *glücklich* is a deeper "happy" (*ein glückliches Leben*). The nouns *die Angst* (fear), *die Sorge* (worry), *die Hoffnung* (hope), *die Freude* (joy) and *das Gefühl* (feeling) all combine with *dass*:

- *Ich habe Angst, dass ich die Prüfung nicht schaffe.* — I'm afraid I won't pass the exam.
- *Ich habe das Gefühl, dass sie mich nicht mag.* — I have the feeling she doesn't like me.
- *Ich mache mir Sorgen, weil er nicht anruft.* — I'm worried because he isn't calling. (*sich Sorgen machen* = to worry; take it as a chunk.)

## Dialogue: Ab wann ein Handy?

Jana and Murat's son Emil is nine. Over dinner they discuss whether he should get a smartphone.

> Jana: Emil hat heute wieder gefragt, ob er ein Handy bekommt. Was meinst du?
>
> Murat: Ehrlich gesagt finde ich, dass neun zu jung ist. Ich habe Angst, dass er den ganzen Tag nur noch spielt.
>
> Jana: Da hast du schon recht. Aber in seiner Klasse haben fast alle Kinder eins. Er ist traurig, weil er in der Pause nicht mitreden kann.
>
> Murat: Das ist doch kein Grund! Nur weil die anderen eins haben, muss er nicht auch eins haben.
>
> Jana: Ein Handy hat aber auch Vorteile. Für mich ist es wichtig, dass er mich nach dem Training anrufen kann.
>
> Murat: Dafür braucht er kein Smartphone. Ein einfaches Handy ohne Internet reicht doch.
>
> Jana: Gute Idee. Ich glaube, damit ist er nicht ganz zufrieden, aber es ist ein Anfang.
>
> Murat: Und wenn er elf ist, sprechen wir noch einmal darüber. Einverstanden?
>
> Jana: Einverstanden. Ich bin sicher, dass er sich trotzdem freut.

In English:

> Jana: Emil asked again today whether he's getting a phone. What do you think?
>
> Murat: To be honest, I think nine is too young. I'm afraid he'll do nothing but play games all day.
>
> Jana: You're right about that. But almost all the children in his class have one. He's sad because he can't join in the conversation at break.
>
> Murat: That's no reason! Just because the others have one, he doesn't have to have one too.
>
> Jana: But a phone has advantages too. For me it's important that he can call me after training.
>
> Murat: He doesn't need a smartphone for that. A simple phone without internet is enough.
>
> Jana: Good idea. I don't think he'll be completely satisfied with that, but it's a start.
>
> Murat: And when he's eleven, we'll talk about it again. Agreed?
>
> Jana: Agreed. I'm sure he'll be pleased anyway.

Find the verbs at the end: *dass neun zu jung ist*, *dass er … spielt*, *weil er … nicht mitreden kann*, *dass er sich trotzdem freut*. *Ich glaube, damit ist er …* leaves out *dass*, so the verb stays in position 2. *ob* (whether) and *wenn* (when) come in lesson 4, and *sich freuen* (to be pleased) in lesson 5. (*ehrlich gesagt* = to be honest; *mitreden* = to join in a conversation; *reichen* = to be enough; *einverstanden* = agreed; *trotzdem* = anyway; *der Vorteil* = advantage, *der Nachteil* = disadvantage.)

## Pronunciation

- *weil* rhymes with English "mile"; *dass* has a short *a*, like *das*.
- *enttäuscht*: ent-TÄUSCHT; the two *t*s merge, and *äu* sounds like "oy".
- *wütend*: long *ü*, WÜ-tend. *überrascht*: ü-ber-RASCHT.
- *zufrieden*: zu-FRIE-den, long *ie*. *unbedingt*: UN-be-dingt.
- In speech, there's a tiny pause at the comma before *weil* and *dass*.

## Common mistakes

- *Ich lerne Deutsch, weil ich will in Wien wohnen.* — Verb to the end: *weil ich in Wien wohnen will.*
- *…, weil ich stehe früh auf.* — The separable verb joins up at the end: *weil ich früh aufstehe.*
- *…, weil ich habe den Bus verpasst.* — The auxiliary goes last: *weil ich den Bus verpasst habe.*
- *Ich glaube, dass er ist nett.* — Either *dass er nett ist* or *Ich glaube, er ist nett.*
- *Weil es regnet, ich nehme den Bus.* — Verb, comma, verb: *Weil es regnet, nehme ich den Bus.*
- *Ich bin recht.* — Use *haben*: *Ich habe recht.*
- *Ich denke, das …* written with *das* when you mean the conjunction: the conjunction is *dass*.
- Forgetting the comma: *Ich hoffe dass …* needs a comma: *Ich hoffe, dass …*

## Key points

- *weil* and *dass* start subordinate clauses: the conjugated verb goes to the end, and a comma separates the clauses.
- *weil* and *denn* both mean "because"; *denn* keeps main-clause order, *weil* sends the verb to the end.
- Separable verbs join up at the end (*aufstehe*); modals and auxiliaries come last (*arbeiten muss, verpasst habe*).
- After *glauben, denken, finden, sagen* you can leave out *dass*, but then use main-clause order.
- A subordinate clause in position 1 is followed directly by the main verb: verb, comma, verb.
- Opinions: *Meiner Meinung nach …, Ich finde, dass …, Da hast du recht, Ich stimme dir zu.*
