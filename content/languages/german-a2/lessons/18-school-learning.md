## What this lesson is for

In lesson 17 you talked about work. Before work comes learning, and in German-speaking countries learning never quite stops: school, vocational training, university, then evening classes and further training for adults. This lesson gives you the words to talk about schools, subjects, courses and exams, and to sign up for a course yourself.

The grammar answers a small but constant question: whose is it? You'll learn two everyday ways to show possession, *Annas Buch* and *das Buch von meiner Schwester*, and you'll learn to recognize a third, more formal one, the **genitive**: *das Buch meiner Schwester*. Finally, we review separable and inseparable verbs, because the language of learning is full of them: *sich anmelden, durchfallen, bestehen, wiederholen, verbessern*.

## Schools in Germany

Every German state (*Bundesland*) runs its own schools, so details differ, but the outline is similar everywhere. (Austria and Switzerland have their own systems; in Austria the final exam at a Gymnasium is called *die Matura*.)

| Stage | School | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| age 6–10 | *die Grundschule* (lesson 2) | primary school, usually four years |
| from about 10 | *das Gymnasium* | academic secondary school; ends with *das Abitur*, the exam you need for university |
| from about 10 | *die Realschule* | secondary school up to grade 10 |
| from about 10 | *die Gesamtschule* and others | comprehensive schools that combine the types |
| after school | *die Berufsschule* | vocational school, one or two days a week during an *Ausbildung* (apprenticeship) |
| after the Abitur | *die Universität* (A1), *die Hochschule* | university, college |

A *Gymnasium* is not a sports hall: that false friend is *die Turnhalle*. The plural is *die Gymnasien*. And *das Abitur* (often just *Abi*) is a school-leaving exam, not a university degree: *Ich habe 2015 Abitur gemacht.* — I did my Abitur in 2015.

In an *Ausbildung*, young people work in a company and go to the *Berufsschule* at the same time. Many well-paid jobs, from electrician to bank clerk, start this way.

## School subjects

| Subject | German |
|---|---|
| maths | *die Mathematik*, usually *Mathe* |
| biology, chemistry, physics | *die Biologie, die Chemie, die Physik* |
| geography | *die Erdkunde* (also *Geografie*) |
| history | *die Geschichte* |
| art, music, PE | *die Kunst, die Musik, der Sport* |
| German, English, French | *Deutsch, Englisch, Französisch* |
| foreign language | *die Fremdsprache* |

*die Geschichte* is the word you know for "story"; it also means history. *Physik* (phü-SIK) and *Chemie* (che-MIE) are both stressed on the last syllable. In Germany *Chemie* starts with a *ch* like in *ich*; in Austria and the south you'll hear a *k*.

Talk about subjects without an article: *Ich habe heute Mathe und Chemie.* — I have maths and chemistry today. *Mein Lieblingsfach war Erdkunde.* — My favourite subject was geography.

The lessons themselves are *der Unterricht* (no plural): *Der Unterricht beginnt um acht.* — Lessons start at eight. The verb is *unterrichten*, to teach: *Sie unterrichtet Kunst an einem Gymnasium.* — She teaches art at a Gymnasium.

## Courses for adults

Adults learn at a *Volkshochschule* (VHS), a public adult education centre found in almost every town, or at private schools and online. Learning for your job is *die Weiterbildung* — further training.

- *Ich möchte mich für den Spanischkurs anmelden.* — I'd like to sign up for the Spanish course.
- *Die Anmeldung ist bis zum 15. September möglich.* — Registration is possible until 15 September.
- *Die Gebühr beträgt 120 Euro.* — The fee is 120 euros. (*betragen* = to amount to)
- *Der Kurs hat zwölf Teilnehmer.* — The course has twelve participants. (One woman is *eine Teilnehmerin*.)
- *Am Ende bekommen Sie ein Zertifikat.* — At the end you get a certificate.

And the exam: *Ich habe die Prüfung bestanden!* — I passed the exam! The opposite is *durchfallen*: *Er ist bei der Prüfung durchgefallen.* — He failed the exam. (You'll also hear *durch die Prüfung gefallen*.) For small tests in class Germans say *der Test* (plural *die Tests*). *das Ergebnis* is the result: *Wann bekommen wir die Ergebnisse?*

If you have no idea, say so: *Keine Ahnung!* — No idea! (*die Ahnung* = idea, notion; mostly used in *keine Ahnung haben*: *Ich habe keine Ahnung von Physik.* — I know nothing about physics.)

## Whose is it? Name + s

With a name, German adds *-s* to the name and puts it in front of the thing, like English, but without an apostrophe:

- *Annas Buch* — Anna's book
- *Peters Lehrerin* — Peter's teacher
- *Frau Wagners Kurs* — Ms Wagner's course
- *Deutschlands Schulen* — Germany's schools

The thing has no article: not *das Annas Buch*, just *Annas Buch*.

When the name already ends in an *s*-sound (*s, ß, x, z*), you write an apostrophe instead of the *-s*: *Hans' Zeugnis, Max' Fahrrad*. These are awkward to say, so in speech people prefer *von*: *das Fahrrad von Max*.

## Whose is it? von + dative

For everything that isn't a name, everyday German uses *von* + dative after the thing:

| Possessor | Example |
|---|---|
| masculine | *das Auto von meinem Bruder* — my brother's car |
| feminine | *der Laptop von der Lehrerin* — the teacher's laptop |
| neuter | *das Zimmer von dem Kind* → *vom Kind* — the child's room |
| plural | *die Hefte von den Schülern* — the pupils' notebooks |

*von* always takes the **dative**, so remember the dative endings: *dem/einem/meinem* (masculine and neuter), *der/einer/meiner* (feminine), *den … -n* (plural). *von dem* contracts to *vom*.

*von* also works with names, and it's the natural choice in conversation: *Das ist die Tasche von Lisa.* — That's Lisa's bag. The question is *Von wem ist das?* — Whose is that? (Or, from lesson 7, *Wem gehört das?*)

## The genitive: recognize it

Written German, and careful speech, have a fourth case for possession: the genitive. It comes after the noun, like *von*, but has its own articles:

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| definite | *des Lehrers* | *der Lehrerin* | *des Kindes* | *der Kinder* |
| indefinite / my | *eines / meines Bruders* | *einer / meiner Schwester* | *eines / meines Kindes* | *meiner Kinder* |

Masculine and neuter nouns add *-s* or *-es*. You don't need to produce these yet; you need to recognize them in fixed expressions and texts:

- *am Anfang des Kurses* — at the beginning of the course
- *am Ende der Woche* — at the end of the week
- *die Hälfte der Teilnehmer* — half of the participants
- *der Name des Lehrers* — the teacher's name
- *die Universität Wien, die Stadt Salzburg* — and *die Hauptstadt Österreichs*, the capital of Austria

The question word is *wessen?* — whose? *Wessen Heft ist das?* You'll read it more than you'll say it; in speech, *Von wem …?* is normal.

## Review: separable and inseparable verbs in learning

Many verbs about learning have prefixes. Remember the rule: stressed prefixes separate, unstressed ones don't.

| Verb | Present | Perfect | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| *sich ANmelden* | *Ich melde mich an.* | *Ich habe mich angemeldet.* | separable |
| *DURCHfallen* | *Er fällt durch.* | *Er ist durchgefallen.* | separable, *sein* |
| *MITschreiben* | *Sie schreibt mit.* | *Sie hat mitgeschrieben.* | separable |
| *beSTEHen* | *Du bestehst bestimmt.* | *Du hast bestanden.* | inseparable |
| *verBESSern* | *Ich verbessere meine Aussprache.* | *Ich habe sie verbessert.* | inseparable |
| *unterRICHten* | *Er unterrichtet Mathe.* | *Er hat Mathe unterrichtet.* | inseparable |
| *wiederHOLen* | *Wir wiederholen die Wörter.* | *Wir haben sie wiederholt.* | inseparable (repeat, revise) |

Inseparable verbs never get *ge-* in the perfect: *verbessert*, not *geverbessert*. *-ieren* verbs don't either: *Ich habe Biologie studiert.* (*mitschreiben* = to take notes.)

With zu + infinitive from lesson 17: *Ich habe vergessen, mich anzumelden.* — I forgot to sign up. *Ich versuche, meine Noten zu verbessern.* — I'm trying to improve my grades.

## Dialogue: at the Volkshochschule

Lukas wants to sign up for an English course and talks to the woman at the registration desk.

> Lukas: Guten Tag. Ich möchte mich für einen Englischkurs anmelden. Ich brauche Englisch für meine Arbeit.
>
> Frau Haas: Gern. Haben Sie schon Englisch gelernt?
>
> Lukas: Ja, in der Schule, aber das ist lange her. Ich habe die Realschule besucht, und Englisch war nie mein Lieblingsfach. Ich hatte immer Angst, Fehler zu machen.
>
> Frau Haas: Kein Problem. Am Anfang des Kurses machen alle Teilnehmer einen kleinen Test. Dann wissen wir, welches Niveau für Sie richtig ist.
>
> Lukas: Und was passiert, wenn ich beim Test durchfalle?
>
> Frau Haas: Nein, nein! Der Test zeigt nur, was Sie schon können. Der Unterricht ist dienstags und donnerstags von 18 bis 19.30 Uhr.
>
> Lukas: Das passt gut. Wie hoch ist die Gebühr?
>
> Frau Haas: 145 Euro für zwölf Wochen. Manche Firmen bezahlen die Weiterbildung ihrer Mitarbeiter. Fragen Sie doch mal Ihren Chef.
>
> Lukas: Gute Idee. Bekomme ich am Ende ein Zertifikat?
>
> Frau Haas: Ja, wenn Sie die Prüfung bestehen. Hier ist das Anmeldeformular. Füllen Sie es bitte aus und unterschreiben Sie hier unten.

In English:

> Lukas: Hello. I'd like to sign up for an English course. I need English for my work.
>
> Ms Haas: Certainly. Have you learned English before?
>
> Lukas: Yes, at school, but that was a long time ago. I went to a Realschule, and English was never my favourite subject. I was always afraid of making mistakes.
>
> Ms Haas: No problem. At the beginning of the course all participants take a little test. Then we know which level is right for you.
>
> Lukas: And what happens if I fail the test?
>
> Ms Haas: No, no! The test only shows what you can already do. Lessons are on Tuesdays and Thursdays from 6 to 7.30 p.m.
>
> Lukas: That suits me. How much is the fee?
>
> Ms Haas: 145 euros for twelve weeks. Some companies pay for their employees' further training. Why don't you ask your boss?
>
> Lukas: Good idea. Do I get a certificate at the end?
>
> Ms Haas: Yes, if you pass the exam. Here's the registration form. Please fill it in and sign down here.

Find the genitives: *am Anfang des Kurses* and *die Weiterbildung ihrer Mitarbeiter* (their employees' further training). In speech, Frau Haas could also say *die Weiterbildung von ihren Mitarbeitern*. *das ist lange her* = that was long ago; *das Niveau* = level (pronounced the French way, "nee-VOH").

## A short text: Mias Weg

> Mia ist in Leipzig aufgewachsen. Nach der Grundschule ist sie aufs Gymnasium gegangen. Mathe und Physik fand sie schwer, aber in Kunst hatte sie immer gute Noten. Nach dem Abitur hat sie keinen Studienplatz bekommen und eine Ausbildung als Fotografin gemacht. Heute hat sie ein eigenes Studio. Am Wochenende besucht sie einen Kurs an der Volkshochschule, denn sie will ihr Italienisch verbessern. Das Studio von Mias Mutter ist gleich nebenan.

Mia grew up in Leipzig. After primary school she went to the Gymnasium. She found maths and physics hard, but she always had good grades in art. After the Abitur she didn't get a university place and did an apprenticeship as a photographer. Today she has her own studio. At the weekend she takes a course at the Volkshochschule, because she wants to improve her Italian. Mia's mother's studio is right next door.

Notice *das Studio von Mias Mutter*: *von* for the studio, name + *s* for the mother. (*eigen* = own; *nebenan* = next door; *der Studienplatz* = a place at university.)

## Common mistakes

- *Anna's Buch* — No apostrophe in German after an ordinary name: *Annas Buch*.
- *das Annas Buch* — No article before the name + s form: *Annas Buch*.
- *das Auto von mein Bruder* — *von* takes the dative: *von meinem Bruder*.
- *Ich bin in der Prüfung durchgefallen* is fine in speech, but the verb takes *sein*, not *haben*: *Ich habe durchgefallen* is wrong.
- *Ich habe die Prüfung gemacht* means "I took the exam", not that you passed it. "Passed" is *bestanden*.
- *Er hat Englisch geunterrichtet.* — *unterrichten* is inseparable: *hat unterrichtet*.
- Using *Gymnasium* for "gym" — a *Gymnasium* is a school. The gym where you work out is *das Fitnessstudio*; the one at school is *die Turnhalle*.

## Key points

- Schools: *Grundschule*, then *Gymnasium* (to the *Abitur*), *Realschule* or *Gesamtschule*; *Berufsschule* alongside an *Ausbildung*.
- Subjects take no article: *Ich habe Mathe.* *Geschichte* = story and history.
- Courses: *sich für einen Kurs anmelden*, *die Anmeldung*, *die Gebühr*, *die Teilnehmer*; exams: *bestehen* (pass), *durchfallen* (fail, with *sein*).
- Possession with names: *Annas Buch* (no apostrophe, no article). Everything else in speech: *von* + dative, *das Auto von meinem Bruder*.
- The genitive (*des Lehrers, der Lehrerin*) is mainly written; recognize it in phrases like *am Ende der Woche*.
- Stressed prefixes separate (*anmelden, durchfallen*); unstressed ones don't and take no *ge-* (*bestanden, verbessert, unterrichtet*).
