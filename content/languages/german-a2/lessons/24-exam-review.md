## What this lesson is for

This is the last lesson of German A2. Like the last lesson of A1, it does three things: it explains what A2 means, describes the best-known exam at this level, the Goethe-Zertifikat A2, with strategies for each part, and reviews the grammar of the whole course with pointers back to the lessons. The questions at the end cover the whole course, so use them as a final check. You don't have to take the exam, but the free official practice tests are the best way to find out whether you have really reached A2.

## What A2 means

A2 is the second level of the **CEFR**, the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages. In plain words, someone at A2:

- understands sentences and frequent expressions about the areas of life closest to them: their family, shopping, where they live, their work;
- can manage simple, routine situations that need a direct exchange of information on familiar topics;
- can describe, in simple terms, their background, their surroundings and the things they need right now.

The biggest difference (*der Unterschied*) from A1: you no longer depend on the other person speaking slowly and helping all the time. You can tell a short story in the past (lessons 1–2), give reasons and opinions (lesson 3), describe people and things in detail (lessons 8–12), and handle offices, doctors, shops and messages (lessons 17–23). Conversations on unfamiliar or abstract topics are still hard, and that is normal: that is B1.

## The Goethe-Zertifikat A2

The adult version of the exam has four parts. Lesen, Hören and Schreiben are taken in writing, one after the other; Sprechen is a separate oral exam, usually taken in pairs.

| part | time | parts | what you do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lesen (reading) | 30 minutes | 4 | a newspaper article; an information board (for example in a building or a shop); a personal e-mail; matching people to small ads |
| Hören (listening) | about 30 minutes | 4 | announcements and phone messages; a conversation whose details you match to pictures; short everyday conversations; a radio interview |
| Schreiben (writing) | 30 minutes | 2 | an informal SMS of 20–30 words; a semi-formal e-mail of 30–40 words; each covers three content points |
| Sprechen (speaking) | about 15 minutes | 3 | in pairs, with no preparation time: asking and answering questions with word cards; talking about your own life; planning an appointment together |

There are 100 points: 75 for the written parts and 25 for speaking. To pass you need 60 points overall, and at least 45 of the 75 written points and 15 of the 25 speaking points. The grades:

| points | grade |
|---|---|
| 90–100 | sehr gut (very good) |
| 80–89 | gut (good) |
| 70–79 | befriedigend (satisfactory) |
| 60–69 | ausreichend (sufficient, a pass) |

The Goethe-Institut offers free official practice material: a complete model test with audio and answers, a second practice test, and an interactive online version.

- [Practice test for adults (Übungssatz, PDF)](https://www.goethe.de/pro/relaunch/prf/materialien/A2/A2_Uebungssatz_Erwachsene.pdf)

Dates, fees and details can change, so check with the exam centre where you register. Other A2 exams (telc, ÖSD) test the same level, though the tasks are a little different (*unterschiedlich*).

## Strategies for each part

### Lesen

- Read the task and the questions first, then the text. You are looking for specific information, not translating every word.
- In matching tasks (people and ads), underline the key need of each person: a time, a price, an age, a place. Then look for an ad that fits all of it, not just one word. Watch out: one person may have no matching ad.
- If you don't know the meaning (*die Bedeutung*) of a word, guess it from the context and move on.
- Information boards are lists of floors, departments or offices: scan, don't read. The vocabulary of lessons 10, 13 and 19 helps.
- Read the small words carefully (*genau lesen*): *nur, nicht, kein, schon, noch nicht, erst*. They often decide the answer. A *Hinweis* (note) in small print may be important too.

### Hören

- Before each audio, read the questions and think about which words you will hear: a time? a place? a price?
- In announcements (*Durchsagen* at the station, *Ansagen* on the phone), listen for numbers: platforms, times, phone numbers. Note them on paper while you listen.
- People often correct themselves: *Wir treffen uns um sieben, nein, um halb acht.* The last information counts.
- Don't be put off by one word you missed. Keep listening; the answer often comes a moment later.

### Schreiben

- Lesson 23 is your preparation. Number the three points and write at least one sentence for each.
- Choose the register from the person: a friend → *du*, informal; a teacher, colleague or neighbour → *Sie*, semi-formal.
- Use one or two linking words (*weil, deshalb, trotzdem, dass*) and a polite form (*Könnten Sie …? Ich würde gern …*).
- Keep to the length. A few words more are fine; double the length means double the room for mistakes.
- Save three minutes at the end to check verb positions, capitals and endings.

### Sprechen

- The speaking test is a conversation (*ein Gespräch*), and the examiners want to see that you can react to your partner, not just recite.
- Questions with word cards: you get a topic and a word, and you make a question. Make it simple and complete: topic *Freizeit*, word *Wochenende* → *Was machst du am Wochenende?* Then answer your partner's question with two or three sentences, not one word.
- Talking about your life: you get a card with a question about your everyday life (*Wohnen, Essen, Reisen, Arbeit* …). Prepare at home: for each common topic, have four or five sentences ready, including the past (*Letztes Jahr bin ich …*), a reason (*weil …*) and an opinion (*Ich finde, dass …*).
- Planning together: you and your partner each have a calendar with appointments and must find a time for something you want to do together. Suggest, react, and agree. The phrases are below.
- If you don't understand: *Wie bitte? Kannst du das noch einmal sagen? Was bedeutet …?* Asking is part of the conversation; silence is not.

### Planning an appointment: phrases

| suggesting | reacting | agreeing |
|---|---|---|
| *Hast du am Samstag Zeit?* | *Am Samstag passt es mir leider nicht.* | *Gut, dann machen wir das so.* |
| *Wie wäre es mit Sonntag um zehn?* | *Da kann ich leider nicht, da habe ich …* | *Einverstanden!* |
| *Wollen wir uns nach dem Kurs treffen?* | *Um zehn ist es mir zu früh. Geht es auch später?* | *Super, das passt.* |
| *Sollen wir zusammen ein Geschenk kaufen?* | *Gute Idee! Aber wann?* | *Also treffen wir uns um drei am Bahnhof.* |

*Einverstanden* means "agreed, OK". *vereinbaren* is to arrange or agree on (*einen Termin vereinbaren*), and *der Zeitpunkt* is the point in time: *Ist das ein guter Zeitpunkt?*

## Exam language

Instructions are in German. These words come up often:

| German | English |
|---|---|
| die Anzeige | small ad, advertisement |
| die Durchsage / die Ansage | announcement (loudspeaker / recorded) |
| der Hinweis | note, tip, notice |
| das Thema | topic |
| beachten | to pay attention to, to note |
| verwechseln | to mix up, to confuse |
| zusammenfassen, die Zusammenfassung | to summarize, summary |
| die Lücke | gap (in a text) |
| die Reihenfolge | order, sequence |
| passend | suitable, matching |
| korrigieren | to correct |

- *Beachten Sie die Uhrzeit.* — Pay attention to the time.
- *Verwechseln Sie nicht Montag und Mittwoch!* — Don't mix up Monday and Wednesday!
- *Finden Sie die passende Anzeige.* — Find the matching ad.
- *Wann bekomme ich mein Ergebnis?* — When will I get my result?

If you remember only three things from A2, make them these: in a subordinate clause, the verb goes to the end; adjective endings show gender and case when the article doesn't; and polite German lives in *würde, hätte, könnte*.

## Dialogue: planning together

Two learners, Marta and Dev, practise the third speaking task. They want to visit their friend Paula, who is in hospital.

> Marta: Dev, wir wollen Paula im Krankenhaus besuchen. Wann hast du Zeit?
>
> Dev: Hm, ich schaue mal in meinen Kalender. Am Montag arbeite ich bis 18 Uhr. Wie wäre es mit Dienstagvormittag?
>
> Marta: Da kann ich leider nicht, da habe ich um zehn einen Termin beim Zahnarzt. Geht es bei dir am Dienstagnachmittag?
>
> Dev: Nachmittags habe ich meinen Deutschkurs, der dauert bis vier. Aber danach habe ich frei.
>
> Marta: Gut, um halb fünf passt es mir auch. Und wir sollten vorher ein Geschenk kaufen. Was meinst du, Blumen?
>
> Dev: Ich glaube, Blumen sind im Krankenhaus nicht so praktisch. Wir könnten ihr eine Zeitschrift kaufen, die sie gern liest.
>
> Marta: Gute Idee! Ich kenne eine, die sie toll findet. Also: Wir treffen uns am Dienstag um halb fünf am Kiosk vor dem Krankenhaus?
>
> Dev: Einverstanden. Aber nicht verwechseln: der Kiosk am Haupteingang, nicht der am Parkplatz!

Translation:

> Marta: Dev, we want to visit Paula in hospital. When are you free?
>
> Dev: Hm, let me look in my calendar. On Monday I'm working until 6 pm. How about Tuesday morning?
>
> Marta: I'm afraid I can't then, I've got a dentist's appointment at ten. Does Tuesday afternoon work for you?
>
> Dev: In the afternoon I have my German course, which lasts until four. But after that I'm free.
>
> Marta: Good, half past four suits me too. And we should buy a present first. What do you think, flowers?
>
> Dev: I don't think flowers are very practical in hospital. We could buy her a magazine she likes reading.
>
> Marta: Good idea! I know one she thinks is great. So: we'll meet on Tuesday at half past four at the kiosk in front of the hospital?
>
> Dev: Agreed. But don't mix them up: the kiosk at the main entrance, not the one at the car park!

The dialogue uses a suggestion (*Wie wäre es mit …?*), a refusal with a reason, *sollten* and *könnten*, a relative clause (*eine Zeitschrift, die sie gern liest*; *der dauert bis vier* is spoken style) and the pronoun *eine*. Dev's last line avoids a *Missverständnis* (misunderstanding).

## Pronunciation

- *Missverständnis*: MISS-ver-ständ-nis, stress on the first syllable.
- *Thema*: TEH-ma; the *th* is a plain *t*.
- *Zusammenfassung*: zu-SAM-men-fas-sung.
- *korrigieren*: kor-ri-GIE-ren, stress on *-gie-*, like all *-ieren* verbs.

## Common mistakes

- Answering a card with one word. *Wochenende? — Fußball.* Say a full sentence and add a detail: *Am Wochenende spiele ich meistens Fußball, weil …*
- Ignoring your partner in the planning task. React to what they say: *Ah, am Montag arbeitest du. Und am Dienstag?*
- In Schreiben, writing *du* to a teacher, or forgetting the opening and closing.
- Thinking 45 written points are enough. You also need 15 in Sprechen, and 60 overall.
- *Ich habe eine Missverständnis* → *ein Missverständnis* (neuter).

## What comes next: B1

At B1 you will talk about experiences, plans and opinions in more detail and follow the main points of clear speech on familiar topics. The grammar adds the simple past of all verbs, the passive and the genitive in full. The Goethe-Zertifikat B1 is a common requirement for work, study and citizenship in Germany.

Until then, keep your vocabulary reviews going every day, read graded readers from the reading list, and find German you enjoy: podcasts for learners, series with German subtitles, a tandem partner.

## Key points

- A2: you handle routine everyday situations and simple exchanges about familiar things, and describe your background and needs.
- Goethe A2: Lesen 30 min, Hören about 30 min, Schreiben 30 min (SMS 20–30 words, e-mail 30–40 words), Sprechen about 15 min in pairs. Pass with 60/100 and at least 45/75 written and 15/25 speaking.
- Practise with the free official model test, practice test and interactive version.
- Planning: *Hast du … Zeit? Wie wäre es mit …? Da kann ich leider nicht. Einverstanden!*
- Exam language: *die Anzeige, die Durchsage, der Hinweis, die Lücke, beachten, verwechseln, passend*.
