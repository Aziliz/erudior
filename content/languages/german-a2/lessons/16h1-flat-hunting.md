## What this lesson is for

In lesson 13 Lena moved into her first flat: boxes, a landlord, a deposit and a washing machine in the cellar. But how did she find the flat? In German-speaking countries most people rent, and finding a flat in a big city is hard work. You read dozens of short, packed adverts, you compare *Kaltmiete* and *Warmmiete*, you go to a viewing with twenty other people, and you ask polite questions.

This honors lesson prepares you for that. You'll learn to read a real-style flat advert with its abbreviations, to work out what a flat really costs per month and on the day you move in, and to name the floors of a building the German way (which is not the American way). Along the way you'll meet a piece of grammar that helps with every long German word: the **compound noun**. And you'll use the polite *Konjunktiv II* forms from lesson 15 at a viewing.

## Long words: how compound nouns work

German builds new nouns by joining old ones: *die Wohnung* (flat) + *die Anzeige* (advert) = *die Wohnungsanzeige* (flat advert). English often writes such pairs as two words ("flat advert"); German writes them as one. That is why German words can look frighteningly long. They aren't hard once you know three rules.

**1. The last part decides the gender and the basic meaning.** *die Wohnungsanzeige* is an *Anzeige*, so it's *die*. *das Dachgeschoss* (attic floor) is a *Geschoss* (floor, storey), so it's *das*, even though *das Dach* (roof) is also neuter: the first part never decides. *die Einbauküche* (fitted kitchen) is a *Küche*.

**2. The first part narrows the meaning.** It answers "what kind?": a *Kaltmiete* is a "cold" rent (without heating), a *Wohnfläche* is the *Fläche* (area) you live in.

**3. Sometimes a small joint is added.** Between the parts you often find an extra *-s-*, *-n-*, *-en-* or *-e-*, called a **linking element**: *Wohnung + s + Anzeige*, *Straße + n + Bahn*. Words ending in *-ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft* and *-ion* almost always take *-s-*: *die Wohnungstür, die Heizungskosten*. For other words there's no rule you can learn at A2: just learn the compound as a whole word.

To read a long word, cut it from the end:

| Long word | Last part (decides gender) | First part(s) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| *die Einbauküche* | *die Küche* | *ein-bau-* (built in) | fitted kitchen |
| *das Dachgeschoss* | *das Geschoss* | *das Dach* | attic floor, top floor |
| *der Mietvertrag* | *der Vertrag* | *die Miete* | rental contract, lease |
| *die Nebenkosten* | *die Kosten* (plural) | *neben* (beside, extra) | extra costs |
| *die Waschmaschine* | *die Maschine* | *waschen* | washing machine |
| *die Wohnungsbesichtigung* | *die Besichtigung* | *die Wohnung* + *s* | flat viewing |

In speech, the stress almost always falls on the first part: *WOHnungsanzeige*, *DACHgeschoss*, *EINbauküche*.

### Example 1: Working out a word you've never seen

You read *der Fahrradkeller*. Cut from the end: *Keller* (cellar, from lesson 13), so it's *der*. Before it: *Fahrrad* (bicycle). So it's a cellar for bicycles: a bike room in the basement. And *die Balkontür*? *Tür* is feminine, so *die*: the balcony door. You didn't need a dictionary for either.

## Reading a flat advert

Adverts are short because space used to cost money, and the style has stayed. Here is a typical one:

> **Helle 2-Zi.-Whg. im Altbau, ab sofort**
>
> 58 m², 2. OG, kein Aufzug, EBK, Balkon zum Hof, Bad mit Fenster. KM 690 €, NK 160 € (inkl. Heizung), Kaution 3 KM. Keine Haustiere. Besichtigung am Samstag, 10–12 Uhr.

| Abbreviation | Full form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *Zi.* | *Zimmer* | rooms (the kitchen and bathroom aren't counted) |
| *Whg.* | *Wohnung* | flat |
| *m²* | *Quadratmeter* | square metres (said "Quadratmeter") |
| *EG / OG / DG* | *Erdgeschoss / Obergeschoss / Dachgeschoss* | ground floor / upper floor / attic floor |
| *EBK* | *Einbauküche* | fitted kitchen (often not included in German flats!) |
| *KM / WM* | *Kaltmiete / Warmmiete* | rent without / with the extra costs |
| *NK* | *Nebenkosten* | extra costs |
| *inkl.* | *inklusive* | including |

So the advert says: bright two-room flat in an old building, available now; 58 square metres, second floor (European counting, see below), no lift, fitted kitchen, balcony facing the courtyard, bathroom with a window. Cold rent 690 euros, extra costs 160 euros including heating, deposit three months' cold rent. No pets. Viewing on Saturday from 10 to 12.

Two words in the title tell you a lot. *Der Altbau* is an old building, in German usage usually one built before about 1950, often with high ceilings and big windows. *Der Neubau* is a new building. *Ab sofort* means "available immediately". A flat can be *möbliert* (furnished), but most German flats are let unfurnished, sometimes even without a kitchen: that's why *EBK* is worth mentioning.

## What does it really cost?

German rents come in layers:

- *die Kaltmiete* (cold rent): the rent for the flat itself.
- *die Nebenkosten*: water, rubbish collection, cleaning the stairs, and often *die Heizung* (heating). They are paid every month as an advance, and once a year the landlord works out the real figure: you pay extra or get money back.
- *die Warmmiete* (warm rent) = Kaltmiete + Nebenkosten. This is what leaves your account each month. (Electricity, *der Strom*, is usually not included: you have your own contract.)
- *die Kaution* (deposit): paid once. By German law it may be at most three months' cold rent, and tenants may pay it in three monthly parts. (That's the basic rule, simplified; contracts have details.)

### Example 2: The monthly rent and the cost of moving in

Take the advert above. KM 690 €, NK 160 €.

1. Warm rent: 690 € + 160 € = **850 €** a month.
2. Deposit: 3 × 690 € = **2,070 €** (three times the *cold* rent, not the warm one).
3. Paying the whole deposit and the first month's rent before moving in: 2,070 € + 850 € = **2,920 €**.
4. Price per square metre (cold), a figure Germans love to compare: 690 € ÷ 58 m² ≈ **11.90 € per m²**.

In German you would say: *Die Warmmiete beträgt 850 Euro. Die Kaution ist dreimal so hoch wie die Kaltmiete.* (*beträgt* = amounts to.)

Some people use *der Makler* (estate agent) to find a flat. Since 2015 the rule in Germany is that whoever hires the agent pays the agent; usually that's the landlord, so tenants rarely pay. An advert may say *provisionsfrei* (no agent's fee).

## Which floor? Counting like a European

Floors are where English speakers get lost, because Germany counts like Britain, not like the US.

![A cross-section of a four-storey apartment building. At the bottom, below the ground, is the Keller with a bicycle. Above it, level with the street, is the Erdgeschoss with the front door. Above that are the 1. Stock and the 2. Stock, each with a window; the 2. Stock has a balcony. At the top, under the sloping roof, is the Dachgeschoss with a small window in the roof. A staircase runs up through all the floors on the right, and the ground line is labeled Straße.](images/de2-m4-house-floors.svg)

| German | Abbreviation | British English | American English |
|---|---|---|---|
| *der Keller* (*das Untergeschoss*) | *UG* | basement, cellar | basement |
| *das Erdgeschoss* | *EG* | ground floor | first floor |
| *der erste Stock* / *das erste Obergeschoss* | *1. OG* | first floor | second floor |
| *der zweite Stock* / *das zweite Obergeschoss* | *2. OG* | second floor | third floor |
| *das Dachgeschoss* | *DG* | top floor (under the roof) | top floor |

You live *im Erdgeschoss*, *im zweiten Stock*, *im Dachgeschoss* (all dative: *Wo?*), and you carry the sofa *in den dritten Stock* (accusative: *Wohin?*, lesson 13). In Austria the ground floor is also *das Parterre*.

So the flat in the advert, *2. OG, kein Aufzug*, means two flights of stairs above street level, with no lift: remember that on moving day.

## At the viewing: polite questions

A *Besichtigung* is short, and you want to make a good impression. The *Konjunktiv II* from lesson 15 is exactly what you need.

- *Ich hätte noch eine Frage: Ist die Heizung in den Nebenkosten enthalten?* — I'd have one more question: is the heating included in the extra costs?
- *Wäre es möglich, die Kaution in drei Raten zu zahlen?* — Would it be possible to pay the deposit in three instalments? (*die Rate* = instalment)
- *Könnten Sie mir sagen, wie hoch die Nebenkosten letztes Jahr waren?* — Could you tell me how high the extra costs were last year?
- *Dürfte ich mir den Keller auch ansehen?* — Might I see the cellar too?
- *Wann könnte ich frühestens einziehen?* — When could I move in at the earliest?

Then, if you get the flat, you sign *den Mietvertrag* (the lease). *unterschreiben* (to sign) you know from A1.

### Example 3: From advert to questions

You read: *3 Zi., 74 m², EG, NK 210 € (ohne Heizung)*. What should you ask? The heating isn't included (*ohne Heizung*), so the warm rent will be higher than KM + NK. A good question: *Wie hoch sind die Heizkosten ungefähr im Monat?* And because the flat is on the ground floor: *Gibt es Gitter vor den Fenstern?* (Are there bars on the windows?) or simply *Ist es im Erdgeschoss laut?*

## Dialogue: Die Besichtigung

Amira is viewing the flat from the advert. Herr Brandt is the landlord.

> Herr Brandt: Guten Tag! Kommen Sie herein. Das ist der Flur, links ist das Bad, rechts die Küche.
>
> Amira: Oh, die Küche ist schön! Ist die Einbauküche in der Miete enthalten?
>
> Herr Brandt: Ja, Herd, Kühlschrank und Spülmaschine bleiben in der Wohnung.
>
> Amira: Sehr gut. Ich hätte noch eine Frage zu den Nebenkosten. Sind 160 Euro realistisch?
>
> Herr Brandt: Letztes Jahr waren es ungefähr 150 Euro im Monat. Die Heizung ist ja inklusive.
>
> Amira: Und wäre es möglich, die Kaution in drei Raten zu zahlen?
>
> Herr Brandt: Natürlich, das ist kein Problem.
>
> Amira: Dürfte ich mir auch den Keller ansehen? Ich habe ein Fahrrad.
>
> Herr Brandt: Gern. Unten gibt es auch einen Fahrradkeller für alle Mieter.
>
> Amira: Wunderbar. Wann könnte ich frühestens einziehen?
>
> Herr Brandt: Ab dem ersten. Wenn Sie die Wohnung möchten, schicke ich Ihnen den Mietvertrag.

In English:

> Herr Brandt: Good afternoon! Come in. This is the hall; the bathroom is on the left, the kitchen on the right.
>
> Amira: Oh, the kitchen is lovely! Is the fitted kitchen included in the rent?
>
> Herr Brandt: Yes, the cooker, fridge and dishwasher stay in the flat.
>
> Amira: Very good. I have one more question about the extra costs. Is 160 euros realistic?
>
> Herr Brandt: Last year it was about 150 euros a month. The heating is included, after all.
>
> Amira: And would it be possible to pay the deposit in three instalments?
>
> Herr Brandt: Of course, that's no problem.
>
> Amira: Might I see the cellar as well? I have a bike.
>
> Herr Brandt: Certainly. Downstairs there's also a bike room for all the tenants.
>
> Amira: Wonderful. When could I move in at the earliest?
>
> Herr Brandt: From the first. If you want the flat, I'll send you the lease.

(*herein* = in, *enthalten* = included, *realistisch* = realistic, *frühestens* = at the earliest, *ab dem ersten* = from the first of the month.)

## Pronunciation

- *Quadratmeter*: kva-DRAHT-mee-ter; *qu* is always "kv" in German.
- *Besichtigung*: be-ZICH-ti-gung, soft *ch* as in *ich*.
- *Altbau* and *Neubau*: stress on the first part, ALT-bau, NEU-bau (*eu* like "oy").
- *möbliert*: mö-BLEERT, stress at the end like all *-ieren* verbs.

## Common mistakes

- *das Einbauküche* — the last part decides: *die Küche*, so *die Einbauküche*.
- *im ersten Stock* translated as "on the first floor" for an American reader: it's one floor above the ground, the US "second floor".
- Working out the deposit from the warm rent. *Kaution 3 KM* means three times the *cold* rent.
- Adding *-s-* everywhere: *Wohnungstür* has one, but *Haustür*, *Mietvertrag* and *Fahrradkeller* don't. Learn compounds as whole words.
- *Können Sie mir die Kaution in Raten …?* sounds abrupt at a viewing. Use *Wäre es möglich, …?* or *Könnten Sie …?*

## Key points

- A **compound noun** takes its gender and basic meaning from its last part; read long words from the end.
- Between the parts there may be a **linking element** (*-s-, -n-, -en-, -e-*); *-ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -ion* words take *-s-*.
- Warmmiete = Kaltmiete + Nebenkosten; the Kaution is at most three cold rents.
- *Erdgeschoss* = ground floor (US first floor); *erster Stock* = one floor up (US second floor).
- At a viewing: *Ich hätte eine Frage … Wäre es möglich, …? Könnten Sie …? Dürfte ich …?*
