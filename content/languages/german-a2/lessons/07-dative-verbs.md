## What this lesson is for

In A1 you used the dative after prepositions (*mit dem Bus*, *bei meiner Mutter*) and in chunks like *Kannst du mir helfen?* and *Gib mir das Buch*. This lesson makes the dative a full part of your German. You will learn the verbs that take a dative object instead of an accusative one, the verbs that take both (you give *someone something*), and the rules for putting two objects in the right order: *dem Kind das Buch*, *es dem Kind*, *es ihm*.

The theme fits the grammar: presents, birthdays, lending and borrowing, and saying that something fits, suits or belongs to someone. And because many of these verbs are the ones Germans use to say what they like (*Das gefällt mir*), they will make you sound much more natural.

## The dative forms: a quick review

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| definite | *dem* | *der* | *dem* | *den* (+ *-n* on the noun) |
| indefinite / *kein* / *mein* | *einem, keinem, meinem* | *einer, keiner, meiner* | *einem, keinem, meinem* | *keinen, meinen* (+ *-n*) |

| nominative | *ich* | *du* | *er* | *sie* | *es* | *wir* | *ihr* | *sie* | *Sie* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dative | *mir* | *dir* | *ihm* | *ihr* | *ihm* | *uns* | *euch* | *ihnen* | *Ihnen* |

Two things to notice: the plural noun takes an extra *-n* (*den Kindern*, *meinen Freunden*), unless it already ends in *-n* or *-s* (*den Frauen*, *den Autos*). And *ihr* means both "to her" (dative of *sie*) and "you all" (nominative); context always makes it clear.

## Verbs with only a dative object

A handful of very common verbs take their object in the dative, not the accusative. These are **dative verbs**, and their object is a **dative object**. You know *helfen* already. Here are the others to learn at A2:

| verb | English | example |
|---|---|---|
| *helfen* | to help | *Ich helfe dir.* |
| *danken* | to thank | *Ich danke Ihnen für die Einladung.* |
| *antworten* | to answer (someone) | *Sie hat mir nicht geantwortet.* |
| *gratulieren* | to congratulate | *Wir gratulieren dir zum Geburtstag!* |
| *vertrauen* | to trust | *Ich vertraue meiner Schwester.* |
| *verzeihen* | to forgive | *Verzeih mir!* |
| *glauben* | to believe (someone) | *Ich glaube dir.* |

There is no logic you can see from English: "I help *you*" looks like a direct object. Think of it as "I give help *to* you", "I give thanks *to* you", "I give my congratulations *to* you": the dative is the "to" person. *gratulieren* adds *zu* + dative for the occasion: *zum Geburtstag, zur Hochzeit*. *danken* adds *für* + accusative for the reason: *Danke für die Blumen.* (*sich bedanken* means the same as *danken* but is reflexive and uses *bei*: *Ich bedanke mich bei dir für die Hilfe.*)

## The "upside-down" verbs: gefallen, gehören, passen …

Some dative verbs work the other way round from English. The thing is the subject, and the person who likes it, owns it or misses it is in the dative:

| verb | literally | English | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| *gefallen* | to be pleasing to | to like (how it looks or seems) | *Die Kette gefällt mir.* — I like the necklace. |
| *schmecken* | to taste good to | to like (food) | *Schmeckt dir die Suppe?* — Do you like the soup? |
| *gehören* | to belong to | to belong to | *Der Regenschirm gehört mir.* — The umbrella is mine. |
| *passen* | to fit / suit | to fit, suit, be convenient | *Der Ring passt mir nicht.* — The ring doesn't fit me. |
| *stehen* | to stand (to) | to suit (clothes) | *Die Sonnenbrille steht dir gut.* — The sunglasses suit you. |
| *fehlen* | to be missing to | to miss (someone) | *Du fehlst mir.* — I miss you. |
| *wehtun* | to hurt | to hurt | *Mir tut der Kopf weh.* — My head hurts. |

Because the thing is the subject, the verb agrees with the thing, not with you:

- *Die Ohrringe gefallen mir.* — I like the earrings. (plural thing → *gefallen*)
- *Gefallen dir meine Schuhe?* — Do you like my shoes?
- *Du fehlst mir.* — I miss you. (literally "you are missing to me")
- *Ihr fehlt mir.* — I miss you all.

*fehlen* is the hardest one for English speakers, because the roles are reversed: *Ich fehle dir* means "you miss me". Read it as "I am missing *for you*". Without a person, *fehlen* simply means "to be missing": *Hier fehlt eine Seite.* — There's a page missing here.

*gefallen* or *mögen*? Both mean "like". *gefallen* is mostly about how something looks or seems, often the first impression: *Wie gefällt dir Berlin?* — How do you like Berlin? *mögen* is for general, lasting likes: *Ich mag Berlin.* For food, use *schmecken* or *mögen*: *Das schmeckt mir.* Note the stem change: *es gefällt*, perfect *hat gefallen*.

*passen* has three common uses: size (*Die Jacke passt mir nicht, sie ist zu klein*), matching (*Der Schal passt gut zur Jacke* — goes well with) and time (*Passt dir Montag?* — Does Monday suit you?).

## Verbs with two objects

Verbs of giving, showing and telling often have two objects: a thing (accusative, the **direct object**) and a person who receives it (dative, the **indirect object**).

*Ich gebe dem Mann den Schlüssel.* — I give the man the key.

| verb | example |
|---|---|
| *geben* | *Gib mir bitte das Salz.* — Pass me the salt, please. |
| *schenken* | *Wir schenken unserer Tochter ein Fahrrad.* — We're giving our daughter a bike. |
| *zeigen* | *Zeigst du mir deine Fotos?* — Will you show me your photos? |
| *bringen* | *Ich bringe dir einen Tee.* — I'll bring you a tea. |
| *schicken* | *Sie hat ihrer Oma ein Päckchen geschickt.* — She sent her grandma a small parcel. |
| *erklären* | *Kannst du mir das erklären?* — Can you explain that to me? |
| *leihen* | *Leihst du mir dein Fahrrad?* — Will you lend me your bike? |
| *empfehlen* | *Ich empfehle Ihnen den Fisch.* — I recommend the fish (to you). |
| *wünschen* | *Ich wünsche dir viel Glück!* — I wish you lots of luck! |
| *erzählen* | *Erzähl mir alles!* — Tell me everything! |

*empfehlen* changes its vowel: *du empfiehlst, er empfiehlt*, perfect *hat empfohlen*. *leihen* is strong: *hat geliehen*. *leihen* and *borgen* both mean "to lend" with a dative person (*Ich leihe/borge dir das Geld*) and "to borrow" with a reflexive dative (*Ich leihe/borge mir das Geld von meinem Bruder* — I borrow the money from my brother).

## Word order: who comes first?

When a sentence has a dative and an accusative object, the order depends on whether they are nouns or pronouns. There are three rules, and you can check every sentence against them.

1. Two nouns: dative before accusative. Person, then thing.
- *Ich schenke meinem Vater ein Buch.* — I'm giving my father a book.

2. A pronoun comes before a noun, whichever one it is.
- *Ich schenke ihm ein Buch.* — I'm giving him a book.
- *Ich schenke es meinem Vater.* — I'm giving it to my father.

3. Two pronouns: accusative before dative. Thing, then person.
- *Ich schenke es ihm.* — I'm giving it to him.

| | dative noun | dative pronoun |
|---|---|---|
| accusative noun | *Ich gebe dem Kind den Ball.* | *Ich gebe ihm den Ball.* |
| accusative pronoun | *Ich gebe ihn dem Kind.* | *Ich gebe ihn ihm.* |

A memory hook: short words go first. Pronouns are short, so they jump ahead of nouns; and of two pronouns, the accusative (*es, ihn, sie*) is usually the shorter, quick one. Rule 3 also gives you the very common *Gib es mir!* (Give it to me!) and *Ich zeige es dir* (I'll show you).

These rules apply in every sentence type: *Hast du ihm das Geld schon gegeben?* — Have you given him the money yet? *…, weil ich es ihr gezeigt habe.* — … because I showed it to her.

## Reading: Ein Geschenk für Opa

> Nächste Woche hat mein Opa Geburtstag. Er wird achtzig, und wir wollen ihm etwas Besonderes schenken. Aber was? Er hat schon alles. Meine Schwester wollte ihm ein Parfüm kaufen, aber das gefällt ihm bestimmt nicht. Er benutzt seit vierzig Jahren dieselbe Seife.
>
> Dann hatte meine Mutter eine Idee: Opa fehlt das Meer. Früher sind wir jeden Sommer mit ihm an die Ostsee gefahren. Also schenken wir ihm einen Gutschein für ein Wochenende in Warnemünde, und wir fahren alle mit. Ein Freund hat uns ein kleines Hotel empfohlen, direkt am Strand.
>
> Wir haben den Gutschein schön eingepackt und ihn Oma gezeigt. Sie hat gelacht: „Das passt perfekt zu ihm!" Am Sonntag geben wir ihn ihm beim Kaffee. Hoffentlich ist die Überraschung groß. Und ich habe schon eine Frage für Opa: Kannst du mir am Strand deinen Regenschirm leihen? Er nimmt ihn nämlich überallhin mit, auch im Sommer.

In English:

> Next week it's my grandpa's birthday. He's turning eighty, and we want to give him something special. But what? He already has everything. My sister wanted to buy him a perfume, but he certainly won't like that. He has used the same soap for forty years.
>
> Then my mother had an idea: Grandpa misses the sea. In the past we went to the Baltic every summer with him. So we're giving him a voucher for a weekend in Warnemünde, and we're all going with him. A friend recommended a small hotel to us, right on the beach.
>
> We wrapped the voucher nicely and showed it to Grandma. She laughed: "That's perfect for him!" On Sunday we'll give it to him over coffee. Hopefully the surprise will be big. And I've already got a question for Grandpa: can you lend me your umbrella on the beach? You see, he takes it everywhere, even in summer.

Notes: *Er wird achtzig* uses *werden* (to become), which is lesson 8; *etwas Besonderes* (something special) is also explained there. *dieselbe* means "the same", *nämlich* "you see, namely", *überallhin* "everywhere". *ein kleines Hotel* has an adjective ending (module 3). Look at the order: *wir wollen ihm etwas Besonderes schenken* (the short personal pronoun *ihm* first), *Wir haben ... ihn Oma gezeigt* (pronoun before noun), *geben wir ihn ihm* (two pronouns: accusative *ihn* before dative *ihm*).

## Things you give, lend and lose

| German | English |
|---|---|
| *der Gutschein* | voucher, gift card |
| *das Paket / das Päckchen* | parcel / small parcel |
| *die Überraschung*, *überraschen* | surprise, to surprise |
| *der Wunsch* | wish |
| *die Empfehlung* | recommendation |
| *einpacken* | to wrap (up), pack |
| *der Schmuck* | jewellery |
| *die Kette*, *der Ring*, *das Armband* | necklace, ring, bracelet |
| *das Parfüm* | perfume |
| *das Portemonnaie* | wallet, purse |
| *der Regenschirm* | umbrella |
| *die Sonnenbrille* | sunglasses (one pair, singular) |

*die Sonnenbrille* is singular like *die Brille*: *Wo ist meine Sonnenbrille? Sie gehört mir!* In southern Germany you'll hear *der Geldbeutel* for *das Portemonnaie*; *die Geldbörse* is a more formal word.

## Pronunciation

- *Portemonnaie*: French spelling, German sound: port-mo-NEE, stress on the last syllable.
- *Parfüm*: par-FÜM, stress at the end. *Päckchen*: short *ä*, soft *ch*.
- *gratulieren*: -ie- stressed, gra-tu-LIE-ren. *empfehlen*: emp-FEH-len, long *e*.
- *ihm* and *ihn* sound almost the same; listen for the final *m* or *n*.

## Common mistakes

- Accusative after a dative verb: ~~Ich helfe dich.~~ ~~Ich danke dich.~~ → *Ich helfe dir. Ich danke dir.*
- Using *gefallen* like *like*: ~~Ich gefalle die Kette.~~ → *Die Kette gefällt mir.* (The thing is the subject.)
- Wrong verb form: ~~Die Schuhe gefällt mir.~~ → *Die Schuhe gefallen mir.* (plural subject)
- *fehlen* backwards: "I miss you" is *Du fehlst mir*, not ~~Ich fehle dir~~ (that means "you miss me").
- English order with pronouns: ~~Ich gebe ihm es.~~ → *Ich gebe es ihm.*
- Forgetting the plural *-n*: ~~Ich schenke den Kinder Bücher.~~ → *den Kindern*.
- *gratulieren* with *für*: ~~Ich gratuliere dir für den Geburtstag.~~ → *zum Geburtstag*.

## Key points

- Dative verbs: *helfen, danken, antworten, gratulieren, vertrauen, verzeihen, glauben* + dative person.
- *gefallen, schmecken, gehören, passen, stehen, fehlen, wehtun*: the thing is the subject, the person is dative. *Die Kette gefällt mir. Du fehlst mir.*
- Giving verbs have a dative person and an accusative thing: *Ich schenke meinem Vater ein Buch.*
- Order: two nouns → dative first; pronoun before noun; two pronouns → accusative first (*es ihm*).
