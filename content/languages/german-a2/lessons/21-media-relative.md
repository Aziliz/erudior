## What this lesson is for

You can already say a lot about the things around you: *Ich habe ein neues Handy. Es ist sehr schnell.* But that takes two sentences, and real speech packs the description into one: "I have a new phone that is very fast." The word "that" (or "which", "who") starts a **relative clause**, a clause that describes a noun. Relative clauses let you describe people (*die Frau, die neben mir wohnt*), explain words you don't know and add details without starting a new sentence.

The good news: you already know the hard part. A relative clause is a **subordinate clause**, so the verb goes to the end, exactly as after *weil*, *dass* and *wenn* (lessons 3 and 4). And the words that start it look almost exactly like *der, die, das*.

The theme is media and technology: the internet, computers, television and the press, where people constantly explain which device, app or programme they mean.

## Words for screens and media

| speichern, löschen | to save, to delete |

A few notes:

- *das Programm* is a computer program, but also a TV channel or a TV schedule: *Was kommt heute im Programm?* — What's on today? One show is *die Sendung*.
- *das Fernsehen* is television as a medium (*im Fernsehen* — on TV); the device is *der Fernseher*.
- *die Nachrichten* (plural) are the news: *Hast du die Nachrichten gehört?* In the singular, *die Nachricht* is a message, as in A1.
- *herunterladen* and *hochladen* are separable, and *laden* changes its vowel like *fahren*: *Ich lade die App herunter. Er lädt ein Video hoch.* Perfect: *hat heruntergeladen, hat hochgeladen*.
- *aufnehmen* is separable and works like *nehmen*: *Sie nimmt die Sendung auf* — she's recording the programme; *hat aufgenommen*.
- *sich einloggen* is reflexive (lesson 5): *Ich logge mich ein. Hast du dich schon eingeloggt?*

## What a relative clause does

Compare these two ways of saying the same thing:

- *Ich habe einen Laptop. Der Laptop ist sehr leicht.* — I have a laptop. The laptop is very light.
- *Ich habe einen Laptop, der sehr leicht ist.* — I have a laptop that is very light.

*Ich habe einen Laptop* is the **main clause**; it could stand alone. *der sehr leicht ist* is the relative clause; it only describes *Laptop*. The noun it describes is the **antecedent**, and the word that starts it, here *der*, is the **relative pronoun**, which stands for the antecedent inside the relative clause.

Three rules make a German relative clause:

1. The relative pronoun comes first in the relative clause.
2. The conjugated verb goes to the very end.
3. Commas separate the relative clause from the rest of the sentence, always.

English can sometimes drop "that" (*the film I saw*). German never drops the relative pronoun: *der Film, den ich gesehen habe*.

## The relative pronouns: nominative

The relative pronouns are the same as the definite article in the nominative and accusative. In the nominative you use them when the antecedent is the subject of the relative clause, the one doing the action.

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nominative | der | die | das | die |

- *Das ist der Kollege, der unsere Webseite macht.* — That's the colleague who does our website.
- *Ich lese eine Zeitschrift, die jeden Monat erscheint.* — I read a magazine that comes out every month. (*erscheinen* = to appear, to come out)
- *Wo ist das Kabel, das neben dem Bildschirm lag?* — Where's the cable that was lying next to the screen?
- *Ich mag Sendungen, die nicht so lang sind.* — I like programmes that aren't so long.

In each relative clause, ask: who or what does the action? *Der* (= the colleague) makes the website; *die* (= the magazine) comes out every month. So the relative pronoun is the subject, and the subject is nominative.

## The accusative

When the antecedent is the direct object inside the relative clause, the relative pronoun is accusative. As with the articles, only the masculine changes: *der* becomes *den*.

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nominative | der | die | das | die |
| accusative | den | die | das | die |

- *Der Laptop, den ich gekauft habe, war nicht teuer.* — The laptop I bought wasn't expensive.
- *Kennst du die App, die alle benutzen?* — Do you know the app everybody uses?
- *Das Video, das du hochgeladen hast, ist super.* — The video you uploaded is great.
- *Wo sind die Fotos, die wir im Urlaub gemacht haben?* — Where are the photos we took on holiday?

In *den ich gekauft habe*, the subject is *ich*; the laptop is the thing bought, the object. So: accusative, *den*.

### Choosing the right pronoun: two questions

This is the key idea of the whole lesson. You need two pieces of information, and they come from two different places:

1. Gender and number come from the antecedent, the noun before the comma. *der Bericht* → masculine. *die Nachrichten* → plural.
2. Case comes from the job the pronoun does inside its own clause, after the comma. Subject → nominative. Direct object → accusative.

Look at the same noun in two different jobs:

- *Das ist der Artikel, der gestern online war.* — That's the article that was online yesterday. (The article was online: subject → *der*.)
- *Das ist der Artikel, den ich dir geschickt habe.* — That's the article I sent you. (I sent it: object → *den*.)

A memory hook: gender looks back, case looks inside.

The case of the antecedent in the main clause doesn't matter: in *Ich habe einen Laptop, der sehr leicht ist*, the main clause has *einen*, but inside its own clause the laptop is the subject, so: *der*.

## Word order inside the relative clause

The verb goes to the end, as in every subordinate clause. That has the usual consequences:

- Perfect: the auxiliary comes last, after the participle: *die Mail, die ich gestern geschrieben habe*.
- Separable verbs join up again at the end: *das Programm, das ich gerade herunterlade*; *die Sendung, die um acht anfängt*.
- Modal verbs come last, after the infinitive: *ein Passwort, das man nicht vergessen kann*; *Dateien, die du löschen musst* (*die Datei* = file).
- Reflexive pronouns come early, right after the relative pronoun: *die Nachbarin, die sich nie einloggen kann* — the neighbour who can never log in.

### A relative clause in the middle

The relative clause comes straight after its antecedent. If the antecedent is at the start of the sentence, the relative clause sits in the middle, with a comma on each side, and then the main clause continues with its verb:

- *Der Film, den wir gestern gesehen haben, war langweilig.* — The film we saw yesterday was boring.
- *Die Werbung, die vor dem Video kommt, nervt mich.* — The ad that comes before the video annoys me. (*nerven* = to annoy, colloquial)

Notice "verb, comma, verb" in the middle: *haben, war* and *kommt, nervt*. The first verb ends the relative clause; the second is the verb of the main clause, which is still in position 2 (the whole subject *der Film, den …* counts as position 1).

## Preview: the dative forms

You will also meet relative pronouns in the dative, after verbs like *helfen* and *gefallen* (lesson 7) and after prepositions like *mit* and *bei*. Here, three forms are new: masculine and neuter *dem*, feminine *der*, and the plural *denen* (with an extra *-en*).

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dative | dem | der | dem | denen |

- *Das ist der Freund, dem ich mit dem Laptop geholfen habe.* — That's the friend I helped with the laptop.
- *Die Kollegin, mit der ich spreche, kommt aus Graz.* — The colleague I'm talking to is from Graz.
- *Das sind die Nachbarn, denen ich unser WLAN-Passwort gegeben habe.* — Those are the neighbours I gave our Wi-Fi password to.

With a preposition, the preposition comes first, before the relative pronoun: *der Bildschirm, auf dem* … (the screen on which …), never at the end as in English ("the screen I work on"). You only need to recognize these forms now; the reference sheet has the full table.

### alles, was

After *alles*, *etwas*, *nichts* and *das*, German uses *was* as the relative pronoun: *Das ist alles, was ich weiß.* — That's everything I know. *Gibt es etwas, was du nicht online kaufst?* — Is there anything you don't buy online?

## Defining words with relative clauses

When you don't know a word, describe it: *Das ist ein Ding, das man für den Computer braucht. Man schreibt damit.* (*die Tastatur*) The pattern *ein Ding / eine Person / ein Ort, der/die/das …* works for almost everything, in conversation and in the speaking exam.

## Dialogue: Oma gets a laptop

Lukas helps his grandmother with her new laptop.

> Oma: Lukas, kannst du mir mal helfen? Das ist der Laptop, den mir deine Mutter geschenkt hat. Aber der Bildschirm ist ganz schwarz.
>
> Lukas: Du musst ihn erst einschalten, Oma. Hier, die Taste, die oben rechts ist. So!
>
> Oma: Ah, jetzt sehe ich etwas. Und wie komme ich ins Internet?
>
> Lukas: Zuerst brauchst du das WLAN. Hast du den Zettel, den der Techniker dir gegeben hat? Da steht das Passwort.
>
> Oma: Ja, hier. Aber die Tastatur ist so klein! Ich finde die Buchstaben nicht.
>
> Lukas: Kein Problem, ich tippe es ein. So, jetzt bist du online. Was möchtest du machen?
>
> Oma: Ich möchte die Sendung über Italien sehen, die gestern Abend im Fernsehen kam. Ich war beim Arzt und habe sie verpasst.
>
> Lukas: Die findest du in der Mediathek. Das ist eine Webseite, auf der man alle Sendungen noch einmal sehen kann. Schau, hier ist sie.
>
> Oma: Wunderbar! Und kann ich sie speichern?
>
> Lukas: Du kannst sie herunterladen. Aber ich würde sie mir einfach online ansehen. Und, Oma: Bitte lösch nichts, was du nicht kennst!

Translation:

> Grandma: Lukas, can you help me? This is the laptop your mother gave me. But the screen is completely black.
>
> Lukas: You have to switch it on first, Grandma. Here, the key at the top right. There!
>
> Grandma: Ah, now I can see something. And how do I get on the internet?
>
> Lukas: First you need the Wi-Fi. Have you got the note the technician gave you? The password is on it.
>
> Grandma: Yes, here. But the keyboard is so small! I can't find the letters.
>
> Lukas: No problem, I'll type it in. There, now you're online. What would you like to do?
>
> Grandma: I'd like to watch the programme about Italy that was on TV last night. I was at the doctor's and missed it.
>
> Lukas: You'll find it in the Mediathek. That's a website where you can watch all the programmes again. Look, here it is.
>
> Grandma: Wonderful! And can I save it?
>
> Lukas: You can download it. But I'd just watch it online. And Grandma: please don't delete anything you don't know!

The relative pronouns: *den* (masculine accusative: the mother gave the laptop), *die oben rechts ist* (feminine nominative), *die gestern Abend … kam* (*die Sendung*, nominative), *auf der* (dative after a preposition) and *was* after *nichts*. Other words: *einschalten* = to switch on, *die Taste* = key (on a keyboard), *der Zettel* = note, slip of paper, *eintippen* = to type in, *verpassen* = to miss, *die Mediathek* = the online archive of a TV channel.

## Pronunciation

- *Tastatur* is stressed on the last syllable: tas-ta-TUR.
- *herunterladen*: he-RUN-ter-la-den. The stress is on *-run-*, not on *her-*.
- *Bildschirm*: BILD-schirm; the *sch* is like English "sh".
- *WLAN* is said "VAY-lahn"; Germans rarely say "Wi-Fi". *Video* is VI-de-o, *Internet* IN-ter-net.
- *Kommentar*: kom-men-TAR, stress at the end.

## Common mistakes

- Wrong gender from the wrong noun. In *die Frau, die das Video gemacht hat*, the pronoun agrees with *Frau*, not with *Video*.
- Taking the case from the main clause. *Ich kenne einen Mann, den in Wien wohnt* is wrong: the man lives in Vienna, so he is the subject: *der in Wien wohnt*.
- Verb not at the end: *der Film, den ich habe gesehen* → *den ich gesehen habe*.
- Splitting a separable verb: *die Sendung, die fängt um acht an* → *die um acht anfängt*.
- Forgetting the commas. German relative clauses always have them, on both sides if the sentence continues.
- *was* after a noun: *das Handy, was ich gekauft habe* is heard in casual speech but wrong in writing. Use *das*.
- Dropping the pronoun as in English: *der Film ich gesehen habe* → *der Film, den ich gesehen habe*.

## Key points

- A relative clause describes a noun (the antecedent). It starts with a relative pronoun, ends with the conjugated verb, and is set off by commas.
- Nominative and accusative relative pronouns = the definite article: *der, die, das, die*; only masculine accusative changes to *den*.
- Gender and number come from the antecedent; case comes from the pronoun's job inside the relative clause.
- Dative forms (preview): *dem, der, dem, denen*. After a preposition: *mit der, auf dem*.
- After *alles, etwas, nichts* use *was*.
- Media words: *herunterladen, hochladen, speichern, löschen, sich einloggen; die Sendung, die Nachrichten, die Werbung*.
