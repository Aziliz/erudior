## What this lesson is for

In lesson 6 you linked facts that clash (*obwohl, trotzdem*). This lesson is about goals: why someone does something, what they want to achieve. English says *I'm learning German to work in Vienna* or *so that my children understand their grandparents*. German has two tools for this, *um … zu* and *damit*, and the choice between them depends on one simple question: who is doing it? Then come two more infinitive patterns that work just like *um … zu*: *ohne … zu* (without doing) and *statt … zu* (instead of doing). Finally, *brauchen nicht … zu*, the everyday way to say "you don't have to".

The theme is studying: universities, courses, internships and the skills you need to get a place.

## Review: zu + infinitive

In A2 you learned the infinitive clause: *Ich habe keine Zeit, den Text zu lesen. Ich habe vor, mich zu bewerben.* Remember the rules, because everything in this lesson builds on them:

- *zu* + infinitive goes at the very end of the clause.
- With separable verbs, *zu* goes inside: *anzumelden, einzuschreiben, mitzukommen*.
- The infinitive clause has no subject of its own; the doer is the subject of the main clause.
- A comma usually separates it from the main clause.

## um … zu: in order to

A **purpose clause** says what an action is for. It answers *Wozu?* (What for?) or *Warum?* When the person who acts and the person who has the goal are the same, use *um … zu*: *um* at the start of the clause, *zu* + infinitive at the end, everything else in between.

| main clause | , | *um* | middle | *zu* + infinitive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Ich lerne Deutsch* | , | *um* | *in Wien* | *zu arbeiten.* |
| *Sie jobbt in der Mensa* | , | *um* | *ihr Studium* | *zu finanzieren.* |
| *Er geht in die Bibliothek* | , | *um* | *in Ruhe* | *zu lernen.* |
| *Wir fahren früh los* | , | *um* | *pünktlich zur Vorlesung* | *zu kommen.* |
| *Du musst dich bis Juli einschreiben* | , | *um* | *im Oktober* | *anzufangen.* |

English uses a plain "to" here (*I'm learning German to work in Vienna*), so learners often forget *um*. In German, a purpose needs *um*: ~~Ich lerne Deutsch, in Wien zu arbeiten~~ is wrong.

The *um … zu* clause can also come first. It then takes position 1 and the main verb follows straight after the comma:

- *Um ein Stipendium zu bekommen, braucht man sehr gute Noten.* — To get a scholarship, you need very good grades.
- *Um Geld zu sparen, wohnen viele Studierende in einer WG.* — To save money, many students live in a flat-share.

## damit: so that

When the goal belongs to someone else, you can't use *um … zu*, because the infinitive has no subject to show who that other person is. Use *damit* with a full clause, verb at the end, like *weil*:

- *Ich erkläre es dir noch einmal, damit du es verstehst.* — I'll explain it to you again so that you understand it.
- *Die Uni bietet Kurse an, damit die Studierenden ihr Deutsch verbessern können.* — The university offers courses so that the students can improve their German.
- *Der Professor spricht langsam, damit alle mitschreiben können.* — The professor speaks slowly so that everyone can take notes.

The test is the subject:

| same person acts and wants | different people | 
|---|---|
| *Ich spare, um ein Auslandssemester zu machen.* (I save, I go) | *Meine Eltern sparen, damit ich ein Auslandssemester machen kann.* (they save, I go) |
| *Sie lernt viel, um die Klausur zu bestehen.* | *Der Dozent wiederholt alles, damit wir die Klausur bestehen.* |

With the same subject, *damit* is also possible, and many people say it: *Ich spare, damit ich ein Auslandssemester machen kann.* It isn't wrong, but *um … zu* is shorter and sounds better. In the exam, use *um … zu* when you can.

Note the modal in many *damit* clauses: *damit … kann*. A purpose often means "so that it's possible", so *können* is very common. Don't add *wollen*: the wanting is already in *damit*.

### damit has two meanings

You know *damit* from A2 as a da-word: *Ich habe einen neuen Laptop. Damit arbeite ich viel schneller.* (With it I work much faster.) The da-word *damit* is an adverb: verb straight after. The conjunction *damit* (so that) sends the verb to the end. Usually the context makes it obvious, and the word order always does:

- *Damit kann ich arbeiten.* — I can work with that. (adverb)
- *…, damit ich arbeiten kann.* — … so that I can work. (conjunction)

## ohne … zu and statt … zu

Two more conjunctions build infinitive clauses the same way as *um … zu*:

- *ohne … zu* — without doing something
- *statt … zu* or *anstatt … zu* — instead of doing something

| | example | English |
|---|---|---|
| *ohne … zu* | *Er hat das Praktikum angefangen, ohne den Vertrag zu lesen.* | He started the internship without reading the contract. |
| *ohne … zu* | *Sie hat den Master gemacht, ohne einen Kredit aufzunehmen.* | She did her master's without taking out a loan. |
| *statt … zu* | *Statt für die Klausur zu lernen, hat er Serien geschaut.* | Instead of studying for the exam, he watched TV series. |
| *anstatt … zu* | *Anstatt den Bus zu nehmen, fahre ich mit dem Rad zur Uni.* | Instead of taking the bus, I cycle to university. |

English uses an *-ing* form after *without* and *instead of*. German never does: the verb is an infinitive with *zu*, at the end. ~~ohne lesen den Vertrag~~ is impossible.

When the subjects differ, use *ohne dass* and *(an)statt dass* with a full clause:

- *Er ist gegangen, ohne dass ich es gemerkt habe.* — He left without me noticing.
- *Statt dass die Uni mehr Plätze schafft, werden die Kurse immer größer.* — Instead of the university creating more places, the courses keep getting bigger.

*statt dass* is less common; *ohne dass* is very frequent.

## brauchen nicht … zu: don't have to

*brauchen* + *zu* + infinitive is used in negative sentences (and with *nur*) as an alternative to *müssen*:

- *Du brauchst nicht zu kommen.* = *Du musst nicht kommen.* — You don't have to come.
- *Sie brauchen keine Gebühren zu bezahlen.* — You don't have to pay any fees.
- *Du brauchst mich nur anzurufen.* — You only have to call me.

*brauchen* takes the place of the modal, so the infinitive goes to the end with *zu*. In speech, many Germans drop the *zu* (*Du brauchst nicht kommen*); in writing and in the exam, keep it. Note: *nicht müssen* means "not have to", never "must not". "You mustn't" is *nicht dürfen*: *Du darfst hier nicht rauchen.*

## Studying in Germany: words

Germany has two main types of university. A *Universität* (often *Uni*) is more academic and research-based; a *Fachhochschule* (now often called *Hochschule für angewandte Wissenschaften*) is more practical, with internships built in. *die Hochschule* is the general word for any institution of higher education.

| German | English |
|---|---|
| *der Studiengang* | degree programme, course |
| *das Semester* | semester, term (*das Wintersemester*, *das Sommersemester*) |
| *die Vorlesung* | lecture (*eine Vorlesung halten / besuchen*) |
| *das Seminar* | seminar, small class |
| *der Hörsaal* | lecture hall |
| *die Hausarbeit* | term paper, essay |
| *die Klausur* | written exam (at university) |
| *der Bachelor*, *der Master* | bachelor's, master's degree |
| *das Stipendium* | scholarship, grant |
| *die Zulassung* | admission, acceptance |
| *der Studienplatz* | place at university |
| *die Mensa* | university canteen |
| *das Studentenwohnheim* | hall of residence |

*sich einschreiben* (to enrol, register) is separable and strong, like *schreiben*: *Ich schreibe mich ein, ich habe mich eingeschrieben*. The infinitive with *zu* is *sich einzuschreiben*. Many universities also say *sich immatrikulieren*.

For jobs and applications: *die Voraussetzung* (requirement, condition), *die Qualifikation* (qualification), *qualifiziert* (qualified), *die Kenntnisse* (knowledge, skills; usually plural: *Deutschkenntnisse, Computerkenntnisse*), *die Fähigkeit* (ability) and *fähig* (capable): *Sie ist fähig, ein Team zu leiten.*

*die Studierenden* (the students) is a gender-neutral word that German universities use a lot. It is an adjectival noun like *die Erwachsenen* (lesson 5): *ein Studierender, eine Studierende*.

## An e-mail: Bewerbung um ein Praktikum

Kemal is in his second year at a Fachhochschule. He writes to a friend who already works in Berlin.

> Hallo Jule,
>
> wie geht es dir in Berlin? Bei mir ist gerade viel los. Ich bin jetzt im vierten Semester, und im Sommer muss ich ein Praktikum machen, um meinen Bachelor abzuschließen. Ich habe mich bei drei Firmen beworben, aber bis jetzt hat niemand geantwortet.
>
> Gestern war ich bei der Studienberatung, um mir Tipps zu holen. Die Beraterin hat gesagt, dass ich meinen Lebenslauf ändern soll, damit man meine Qualifikationen besser sieht. Ich hatte zum Beispiel meine Programmierkenntnisse ganz unten geschrieben, statt sie gleich am Anfang zu nennen. Außerdem braucht man für viele Praktika gute Englischkenntnisse. Deshalb besuche ich jetzt zusätzlich ein Seminar auf Englisch.
>
> Deine Firma sucht doch auch Praktikanten, oder? Könntest du mir sagen, welche Voraussetzungen man braucht? Du brauchst natürlich nicht mit deinem Chef zu sprechen, ich will nur wissen, ob sich eine Bewerbung lohnt. Ich möchte mich nicht bewerben, ohne etwas über die Firma zu wissen.
>
> Ach ja, und falls ich die Stelle bekomme: Kann ich die ersten Wochen bei dir wohnen, damit ich in Ruhe ein Zimmer suchen kann?
>
> Viele Grüße
> Kemal

In English:

> Hi Jule,
>
> how are things in Berlin? There's a lot going on for me at the moment. I'm in my fourth semester now, and in the summer I have to do an internship to complete my bachelor's. I've applied to three companies, but so far nobody has replied.
>
> Yesterday I went to the student advice centre to get some tips. The adviser said I should change my CV so that people can see my qualifications better. For example, I had put my programming skills right at the bottom instead of mentioning them straight away at the start. Besides, for many internships you need good English. That's why I'm now also attending a seminar in English.
>
> Your company is looking for interns too, isn't it? Could you tell me what requirements you need? Of course you don't have to talk to your boss, I just want to know whether it's worth applying. I don't want to apply without knowing anything about the company.
>
> Oh yes, and in case I get the position: can I stay with you for the first few weeks so that I can look for a room without any stress?
>
> Best wishes,
> Kemal

Notes: *Bei mir ist viel los* = I have a lot going on; *die Studienberatung* = student advice service; *der Lebenslauf* = CV; *zusätzlich* = additionally; *in Ruhe* = in peace, without hurrying. Spot the patterns: two *um … zu*, two *damit*, one *statt … zu*, one *ohne … zu* and one *brauchen nicht … zu*. Notice *um mir Tipps zu holen*: the reflexive pronoun stays inside the clause.

## Pronunciation

- *Vorlesung*: FOR-le-sung; *Semester*: se-MES-ter.
- *Stipendium*: sti-PEN-di-um, with *st* as *scht*.
- *Praktikum*: PRAK-ti-kum; *Praktikant*: prak-ti-KANT (stress at the end, like most nouns in *-ant*).
- *Mensa*: MEN-za; *Hörsaal*: HÖR-zahl, a long *aa*.
- *damit* as "so that" is stressed on the second syllable: da-MIT. As the da-word "with it" it is often stressed on the first: DA-mit.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving out *um*: ~~Ich gehe in die Bibliothek, zu lernen.~~ → *…, um zu lernen.*
- *um … zu* with two subjects: ~~Ich gebe dir das Buch, um du es liest.~~ → *…, damit du es liest.*
- English *-ing* after *ohne*: ~~ohne fragen~~ → *ohne zu fragen*. ~~statt lernen~~ → *statt zu lernen*.
- *zu* in the wrong place with separable verbs: ~~um zu einschreiben~~ → *um mich einzuschreiben*.
- Using *für* + infinitive like English *for*: ~~für Geld zu verdienen~~ → *um Geld zu verdienen*. (*für* + noun is fine: *für das Studium*.)
- *damit* + *wollen*: ~~damit er Deutsch lernen will~~. The goal is in *damit*; use *damit er Deutsch lernt* or *… lernen kann*.
- *nicht müssen* ≠ *nicht dürfen*: *Du musst nicht / brauchst nicht zu kommen* (it's optional); *Du darfst nicht kommen* (it's forbidden).

## Key points

- Same subject: *um … zu* + infinitive at the end: *Ich spare, um zu reisen.*
- Different subjects: *damit* + verb at the end: *Ich spare, damit meine Tochter reisen kann.*
- *ohne … zu* (without -ing) and *(an)statt … zu* (instead of -ing) work like *um … zu*; with different subjects use *ohne dass*, *statt dass*.
- *brauchen nicht / nur … zu* = *nicht / nur müssen*.
- Separable verbs: *zu* in the middle (*anzufangen*); the reflexive pronoun stays in the clause (*um mich einzuschreiben*).
