## What this lesson is for

You can already give reasons with *weil* and *denn* and results with *deshalb*. Real texts, though, especially letters from banks, landlords and insurance companies, use a wider set: *da* for a reason everyone already knows, *falls* for a possibility you prepare for, *sodass* for a result, and the adverbs *also, daher, deswegen, darum*. This lesson sorts them by meaning and by word order, so that you can read an official letter calmly and write a clear one yourself.

The theme fits: insurance, contracts, deadlines and claims, the paperwork of independent adult life in a German-speaking country.

## da: since, as

*da* is a subordinating conjunction meaning "since" or "as" (the reason, not the time). The verb goes to the end, as with *weil*:

- *Da ich umgezogen bin, muss ich meine Adresse ändern.* — Since I've moved, I have to change my address.
- *Da der Vertrag im Mai endet, sollten Sie rechtzeitig kündigen.* — As the contract ends in May, you should give notice in good time.

So what's the difference from *weil*? Three things:

1. *da* usually gives a reason the listener already knows or that is obvious. *weil* gives new information, often the most important point of the sentence.
2. That's why *da* clauses usually come first, and *weil* clauses usually come second.
3. Only *weil* can answer a question on its own: *Warum hast du gekündigt? – Weil es zu teuer war.* ~~Da es zu teuer war~~ is impossible as a short answer.

| | *weil* | *da* |
|---|---|---|
| meaning | because | since, as |
| the reason is … | new, the main point | known, background |
| usual position | after the main clause | before the main clause |
| short answer to *Warum?* | yes | no |
| style | everyday and written | a bit more formal, written |

- *Ich habe das Auto verkauft, weil es zu viele Reparaturen brauchte.* — the reason is the news.
- *Da Sie seit zehn Jahren Kundin bei uns sind, bekommen Sie einen Rabatt.* — the customer knows this; the news is the discount.

Don't confuse the conjunction with the adverb *da* (there, then): *Da ist der Bahnhof* (verb second) vs *Da der Bahnhof weit ist, …* (verb last). The word order tells you which one it is.

## falls: in case, if

You met *falls* in A2. It's a **conditional clause** like *wenn*, but it stresses that something might or might not happen; *wenn* can also mean "whenever" or "when" (it will happen). The verb goes to the end:

- *Falls Sie Fragen haben, rufen Sie uns an.* — If you have any questions, call us.
- *Nehmen Sie die Unterlagen mit, falls die Versicherung einen Nachweis braucht.* — Take the documents with you in case the insurance company needs proof.
- *Falls der Schaden über 500 Euro liegt, zahlen Sie keine Selbstbeteiligung.* — If the damage comes to more than 500 euros, you don't pay an excess.

Letters and contracts often use a noun phrase instead: *im Fall eines Unfalls* (in the event of an accident; a genitive, lesson 13), *im Schadensfall* (in the event of a claim), or *für den Fall, dass …* (in case …): *Für den Fall, dass Sie umziehen, informieren Sie uns bitte schriftlich.*

English speakers often use *falls* for "in case" in the sense of "as a precaution": *Ich nehme einen Schirm mit, falls es regnet.* — I'm taking an umbrella in case it rains. That's correct German too.

## sodass and so … dass: so that, with the result that

*sodass* introduces a **result clause**: what happened as a consequence. The verb goes to the end:

- *Das Wasser lief in die Wohnung unter uns, sodass dort die Decke beschädigt wurde.* — The water ran into the flat below us, so that the ceiling there was damaged.
- *Ich habe die Frist verpasst, sodass mein Vertrag automatisch verlängert wurde.* — I missed the deadline, with the result that my contract was automatically renewed.

When the result depends on how strong something was, split it: *so* + adjective in the main clause, *dass* at the start of the result:

- *Der Sturm war so stark, dass viele Bäume umgefallen sind.* — The storm was so strong that many trees fell over.
- *Das Kleingedruckte war so klein, dass ich es kaum lesen konnte.* — The small print was so small that I could hardly read it.

Both spellings *sodass* and *so dass* are correct; this course uses *sodass*.

Be careful: English "so that" has two meanings. If it means a goal (*I'm saving so that I can travel*), it's *damit* (lesson 7). If it means a result (*It rained a lot, so that the street flooded*), it's *sodass*. A test: if you can replace it with "in order that", it's *damit*; if you can replace it with "and as a result", it's *sodass*.

## Consequences in position 1: also, daher, deswegen, darum

You know *deshalb*. Four more adverbs mean the same, "so, therefore, that's why". All of them take position 1 (or a place after the verb), and the verb comes straight after:

| adverb | style | example |
|---|---|---|
| *deshalb* | neutral | *Ich hatte einen Unfall, deshalb bin ich zu spät.* |
| *deswegen* | neutral, common in speech | *Das Handy war nicht versichert, deswegen muss ich alles selbst bezahlen.* |
| *darum* | spoken | *Es ist billiger, darum habe ich es genommen.* |
| *daher* | written, formal | *Die Unterlagen sind nicht vollständig. Daher können wir Ihren Antrag nicht bearbeiten.* |
| *also* | so, then (a conclusion) | *Der Vertrag läuft bis Juni. Also kann ich erst dann wechseln.* |

Watch out: German *also* is a false friend. It means "so, therefore, well then", never English "also" (which is *auch*). *Also, was machen wir jetzt?* — So, what are we doing now?

One more trap: in German the adverb, not a conjunction, is used here, so it changes the word order. ~~Daher wir können nicht zahlen~~ is wrong: *Daher können wir nicht zahlen.*

## The reason family at a glance

| meaning | verb at the end | verb straight after (position 1) | position 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| reason | *weil*, *da* | — | *denn* |
| result | *sodass*, *so … dass* | *deshalb, deswegen, darum, daher, also* | — |
| condition | *wenn*, *falls* | *sonst* (if not, otherwise) | — |
| purpose | *damit*, *um … zu* | — | — |
| concession | *obwohl* | *trotzdem, dennoch* | *aber* |

## Insurance and contracts: words

| German | English |
|---|---|
| *eine Versicherung abschließen* | to take out insurance |
| *die Haftpflichtversicherung* | personal liability insurance |
| *die Hausratversicherung* | home contents insurance |
| *versichert sein* | to be insured |
| *der Schaden, der Schadensfall* | damage, claim (an insured incident) |
| *die Selbstbeteiligung* | excess, deductible |
| *der Anspruch (auf + Akk)* | right, claim (to) |
| *die Laufzeit* | term, duration (of a contract) |
| *die Frist*, *die Kündigungsfrist* | deadline, notice period |
| *verlängern* | to extend, renew |
| *gültig / ungültig* | valid / invalid |
| *die Bedingung* | condition |
| *das Kleingedruckte* | the small print |

In Germany the *Haftpflichtversicherung* is the one most people have: it pays when you damage someone else's things, for example when your washing machine floods the neighbours' flat. *Anspruch* is a key word in official letters: *Sie haben Anspruch auf …* (You are entitled to …). *das Kleingedruckte* is an adjectival noun: *im Kleingedruckten*.

## A letter: Ihre Schadensmeldung

Tobias's bike was stolen from the cellar. He reported it to his insurance company and got this answer.

> Sehr geehrter Herr Lindner,
>
> vielen Dank für Ihre Meldung vom 3. März. Da Ihr Fahrrad aus einem abgeschlossenen Kellerraum gestohlen wurde, besteht grundsätzlich ein Anspruch auf Ersatz im Rahmen Ihrer Hausratversicherung.
>
> Leider sind Ihre Unterlagen noch nicht vollständig, sodass wir den Schaden noch nicht bearbeiten können. Es fehlen die Rechnung für das Fahrrad und die Bescheinigung der Polizei über den Diebstahl. Falls Sie die Rechnung nicht mehr haben, genügt auch ein anderer Nachweis, zum Beispiel ein Kontoauszug oder ein Foto des Fahrrads.
>
> Bitte beachten Sie: Nach den Bedingungen Ihres Vertrags gilt eine Selbstbeteiligung von 150 Euro. Wir zahlen also den Zeitwert des Fahrrads minus 150 Euro.
>
> Wir bitten Sie, uns die fehlenden Dokumente innerhalb von 14 Tagen zu schicken. Nach Ablauf dieser Frist sind wir leider verpflichtet, den Fall zu schließen. Für Ausnahmen, zum Beispiel bei längerer Krankheit, wenden Sie sich bitte an Ihre Sachbearbeiterin, Frau Kurz.
>
> Mit freundlichen Grüßen
> Ihre Nordstern Versicherung

In English:

> Dear Mr Lindner,
>
> thank you for your report of 3 March. As your bicycle was stolen from a locked cellar room, you are in principle entitled to compensation under your home contents insurance.
>
> Unfortunately your documents are not yet complete, so we cannot process the claim yet. The invoice for the bicycle and the police certificate about the theft are missing. If you no longer have the invoice, other proof will also do, for example a bank statement or a photo of the bicycle.
>
> Please note: under the conditions of your contract an excess of 150 euros applies. We will therefore pay the current value of the bicycle minus 150 euros.
>
> We ask you to send us the missing documents within 14 days. After this deadline we are unfortunately obliged to close the case. For exceptions, for example in the case of a longer illness, please contact your case handler, Ms Kurz.
>
> Yours sincerely,
> Nordstern Insurance

Notes: *gestohlen wurde* is a passive (lessons 16–17): "was stolen". *grundsätzlich* = in principle; *im Rahmen* = within the scope of; *der Ersatz* = replacement, compensation; *genügen* = to be enough; *der Kontoauszug* = bank statement; *der Zeitwert* = current value; *nach Ablauf* = after the end of; *sich wenden an* = to contact, turn to. *innerhalb von* means "within". Find the connectors: *da* (known reason, first), *sodass* (result), *falls* (condition), *also* (conclusion).

## Pronunciation

- *sodass*: so-DASS, stress on the second syllable.
- *daher*: da-HER; *darum*: da-RUM; *deswegen*: DES-we-gen.
- *Anspruch*: AN-spruch, the *sp* is *schp* and the *ch* after *u* is the throaty *ch* (as in *Buch*).
- *Diebstahl*: DIEP-stahl, a *p* sound for the *b* because of final devoicing.
- *Haftpflicht*: HAFT-pflicht; say the *pf* together.

## Common mistakes

- *da* as a short answer: ~~Warum bist du nicht versichert? – Da es zu teuer ist.~~ → *Weil es zu teuer ist.*
- *damit* for a result: ~~Es hat stark geregnet, damit der Keller nass war.~~ → *…, sodass der Keller nass war.*
- English "also": ~~Ich habe also eine Hausratversicherung~~ when you mean "too" → *Ich habe auch eine Hausratversicherung.*
- Subject first after *daher / deswegen*: ~~Deswegen ich muss zahlen.~~ → *Deswegen muss ich zahlen.*
- Word order after *falls*: ~~falls Sie haben Fragen~~ → *falls Sie Fragen haben*.
- *Anspruch* with the wrong preposition: ~~Anspruch für Geld~~ → *Anspruch auf Geld*.

## Key points

- *da* = since, as: a known reason, usually at the start; verb at the end. *weil* gives new information and answers *Warum?*
- *falls* = in case, if (maybe); verb at the end. Also *im Fall + genitive*, *für den Fall, dass*.
- *sodass* / *so … dass* = result; *damit* = goal.
- *also, daher, deswegen, darum, deshalb* = so, therefore; position 1, verb straight after. *also* ≠ English "also".
