## What this lesson is for

In the last lesson you dealt with doctors and insurance. This lesson stays at home: the building you live in, the people next door, and what to do when something breaks. In a block of flats you rarely fix things yourself. You *have* them fixed: *Die Hausverwaltung lässt die Heizung reparieren.* And sometimes you need to *let* someone do something, or *make* them do it.

German does all of this with one verb, *lassen*. You met it in A2 (*Ich lasse mein Fahrrad reparieren*). Now you'll learn all its uses, its unusual perfect (*Ich habe das Fahrrad reparieren lassen*), and the pattern *sich lassen*, which you'll meet in instructions and complaints: *Das Fenster lässt sich nicht öffnen.*

## The four jobs of lassen

*lassen* is a strong verb: *ich lasse, du lässt, er lässt*; Präteritum *ließ*; perfect *hat gelassen* (but see below).

### 1. To leave

On its own, with a normal object, *lassen* means to leave something somewhere or in a certain state:

- *Ich habe meinen Schlüssel im Auto gelassen.* — I left my key in the car.
- *Lass mich in Ruhe!* — Leave me alone!
- *Lass die Tür bitte offen.* — Please leave the door open.

### 2. To have something done

*lassen* + infinitive at the end: somebody else does the work for you. This is the **causative** use, and the most important one in daily life:

- *Wir lassen die Wohnung renovieren.* — We're having the flat renovated.
- *Ich muss den Wasserhahn reparieren lassen.* — I need to get the tap repaired.
- *Er lässt sich die Haare schneiden.* — He's having his hair cut.

When it's something done to your own body or for you personally, add a dative reflexive pronoun: *Ich lasse mir die Haare schneiden. Lass dir das erklären!* (Get someone to explain that to you!) If you want to say who does the job, use *von*: *Wir lassen die Heizung von einem Handwerker prüfen.*

### 3. To let, allow

*lassen* + a person (accusative) + infinitive: you allow someone to do something.

- *Meine Eltern lassen mich allein in den Urlaub fahren.* — My parents let me go on holiday alone.
- *Lass mich das machen!* — Let me do that!
- *Lass uns gehen!* — Let's go!

*Lass uns …* / *Lasst uns …* is a common way of making a suggestion, a bit like *Wollen wir …?*

### 4. To make someone do something

The same pattern can mean that you cause or make someone do something. The context decides whether it's "let" or "make":

- *Die Lehrerin lässt uns jeden Tag einen Text schreiben.* — The teacher makes us write a text every day.
- *Der Vermieter lässt uns lange warten.* — The landlord keeps us waiting.

### Word order

*lassen* works like a modal verb: it takes the second position, and the other infinitive goes to the end.

| | position 2 | | end |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Ich* | *lasse* | *das Auto morgen* | *reparieren.* |
| *Wann* | *lässt* | *du dir die Haare* | *schneiden?* |
| *Wir* | *müssen* | *die Heizung* | *reparieren lassen.* |
| *…, weil ich* | | *das Auto* | *reparieren lasse.* |

## The perfect: hat … reparieren lassen

When *lassen* is used with another infinitive, its perfect uses the infinitive *lassen*, not the participle *gelassen*. You get two infinitives at the end, the same pattern as with modal verbs (*hat … machen müssen*):

- *Ich habe die Waschmaschine reparieren lassen.* — I had the washing machine repaired.
- *Sie hat ihren Sohn allein fahren lassen.* — She let her son go alone.
- *Wir haben uns beraten lassen.* — We got advice.

Without another infinitive, the normal participle is used: *Ich habe meine Brille zu Hause gelassen.* — I left my glasses at home.

In everyday speech, Germans often avoid this perfect and use the Präteritum *ließ* or just rephrase: *Ich ließ die Waschmaschine reparieren.* In a subordinate clause the word order gets unusual (*…, dass ich das Auto habe reparieren lassen*), so most people simply say *…, dass das Auto repariert worden ist* or *…, dass ich das Auto in der Werkstatt hatte*. Recognize the form; you don't need to build it.

## sich lassen: "can be done" *(receptive)*

*sich lassen* + infinitive means that something *can* be done, almost like a passive with *können*. It's very common in complaints and instructions:

- *Das Fenster lässt sich nicht öffnen.* — The window won't open / can't be opened.
- *Der Mangel lässt sich leicht beheben.* — The defect can easily be fixed.
- *Das lässt sich machen.* — That can be done. (= OK, no problem.)
- *Darüber lässt sich streiten.* — That's debatable.

## Living together in a building

A block of flats in Germany usually has a *Hausordnung* (house rules) and a *Hausverwaltung* (property management company) that looks after the building for the owner. You call them when there's a *Mangel* (defect): the *Heizung* (heating) doesn't work, the *Wasserhahn* (tap) drips, a window is *undicht* (leaks), or there's *Schimmel* (mould) in the bathroom. They send a *Handwerker* or *Handwerkerin* (tradesperson) to *beheben* (fix, remedy) it.

If the landlord doesn't fix a serious defect, tenants in Germany may have the right to a *Mietminderung* (rent reduction). Before you pay less rent, though, get advice: the local *Mieterverein* (tenants' association) helps its members for a small yearly fee.

Many conflicts are about shared spaces: the *Treppenhaus* (stairwell), the *Hof* (courtyard), the *Mülltonnen* (bins). Common complaints: a *Kinderwagen* (pushchair) or bikes are *abgestellt* (left, parked) in the hall, someone *grillt* on the balcony, a dog barks, the *Klingel* (doorbell) is broken, post ends up in the wrong *Briefkasten* (letterbox). Most of this can be solved with a friendly conversation. If not, a neutral person can *vermitteln* (mediate) until both sides *sich einigen* (come to an agreement). Some towns offer free *Vermittlung* (mediation) between neighbours, and an *Einigung* (agreement) reached that way usually lasts longer than one forced by a lawyer.

- *Der Wasserhahn tropft seit einer Woche.* — The tap has been dripping for a week.
- *Könnten Sie den Mangel bitte bis Freitag beheben lassen?* — Could you please have the defect fixed by Friday?

## Reading: a blog post

> *Mein Haus, meine Nachbarn*
>
> Als ich vor zwei Jahren in dieses Haus gezogen bin, kannte ich niemanden. Im Treppenhaus haben wir uns gegrüßt, mehr nicht. Das hat sich im letzten Winter geändert. Die Heizung war drei Tage lang kaputt, und es war eiskalt. Die Hausverwaltung war telefonisch nicht zu erreichen. Frau Özdemir aus dem Erdgeschoss hat dann eine Liste im Hof aufgehängt, und alle Mieter haben unterschrieben. Am nächsten Tag hat die Verwaltung endlich einen Handwerker geschickt und die Heizung reparieren lassen.
>
> Seitdem ist das Zusammenleben anders. Wir haben eine Chatgruppe gegründet, in der wir uns gegenseitig helfen. Wer eine Bohrmaschine braucht, kann sie bei Herrn Lang ausleihen. Herr Lang lässt sich dafür beim Einkaufen helfen, weil er nicht mehr gut laufen kann. Die Familie im dritten Stock lässt ihren Kinderwagen zwar immer noch im Flur stehen, aber jetzt redet man darüber, statt Zettel an die Tür zu kleben.
>
> Natürlich gibt es auch Streit. Im Sommer wollte ein Nachbar jeden Abend auf dem Balkon grillen, und die Leute über ihm haben sich über den Rauch beschwert. Frau Özdemir hat vermittelt, und wir haben uns geeinigt: Gegrillt wird nur am Wochenende und nur bis zehn Uhr. Das war ein guter Kompromiss.
>
> Im September haben wir zum ersten Mal ein Nachbarschaftsfest im Hof gefeiert. Jeder hat etwas mitgebracht, und die Kinder durften bis spät draußen bleiben. Ich habe gelernt: Eine Gemeinschaft entsteht nicht von allein. Aber ein bisschen Hilfsbereitschaft und Toleranz machen das Leben in einem großen Haus viel angenehmer.

Translation:

> *My building, my neighbours*
>
> When I moved into this building two years ago, I didn't know anyone. We said hello in the stairwell, nothing more. That changed last winter. The heating was broken for three days and it was freezing. The property management company couldn't be reached by phone. Mrs Özdemir from the ground floor then put up a list in the courtyard, and all the tenants signed it. The next day the management finally sent a tradesman and had the heating repaired.
>
> Since then, living together has been different. We set up a chat group where we help each other. Anyone who needs a drill can borrow it from Mr Lang. In return Mr Lang gets help with his shopping, because he can't walk well any more. The family on the third floor still leaves their pushchair in the hall, but now people talk about it instead of sticking notes on the door.
>
> Of course there are arguments too. In the summer one neighbour wanted to barbecue on his balcony every evening, and the people above him complained about the smoke. Mrs Özdemir mediated and we came to an agreement: barbecues only at the weekend, and only until ten o'clock. That was a good compromise.
>
> In September we had a neighbourhood party in the courtyard for the first time. Everyone brought something, and the children were allowed to stay outside until late. I've learned that a community doesn't happen by itself. But a bit of helpfulness and tolerance make life in a big building much more pleasant.

Notes: *war nicht zu erreichen* (couldn't be reached) is *sein + zu*, a passive-like form from lesson 18. *Gegrillt wird nur am Wochenende* is a passive without a subject (lesson 17). *entstehen* means "to come into being, arise". Find the uses of *lassen*: *reparieren lassen* (had it done), *lässt sich helfen* (lets people help him), *lässt … stehen* (leaves standing).

## Being helpful and polite

Some useful words for the friendly side of living together:

- *Die neuen Nachbarn sind sehr hilfsbereit.* — The new neighbours are very helpful.
- *Frau Lang hat freundlicherweise unsere Pakete angenommen.* — Mrs Lang kindly took in our parcels.
- *Wir tolerieren den Lärm am Wochenende, aber nicht nachts.* — We put up with the noise at the weekend, but not at night.
- *Kann ich mir deine Leiter ausleihen?* — Can I borrow your ladder?

*ausleihen* is separable and works in both directions: *sich etwas ausleihen* is to borrow, *jemandem etwas ausleihen* is to lend: *Ich leihe dir mein Fahrrad aus.* Tenants who share pets, parcels and tools often notice how much easier the *Zusammenleben* gets. Is a *Haustier* (pet) allowed? Check your rental contract.

## Pronunciation

- *lässt*: short *ä* like in *Bett*; *ließ*: long *ie*, sharp *ß*.
- *Treppenhaus*: TREP-pen-haus; *Hausverwaltung*: HAUS-ver-wal-tung, stress on the first part as in most compounds.
- *Schimmel*: SCHIM-mel, short *i*.
- *beheben*: be-HE-ben, long *e*.

## Common mistakes

- Using *machen* for "have something done": ~~Ich mache meine Haare schneiden.~~ → *Ich lasse mir die Haare schneiden.*
- Putting *zu* before the infinitive: ~~Ich lasse das Auto zu reparieren.~~ *lassen*, like modals, takes a bare infinitive.
- The perfect with *gelassen* and another verb: ~~Ich habe das Auto reparieren gelassen.~~ → *Ich habe das Auto reparieren lassen.* (Some native speakers do say it, but it's not standard.)
- The vowel change: *du lässt, er lässt* (not ~~lasst~~, which is *ihr lasst*).
- *ausleihen* vs *leihen*: both exist; *ausleihen* is especially common for libraries and for borrowing.
- *der Mangel* has an umlaut in the plural: *die Mängel*.

## Key points

- *lassen* + object: to leave (*Ich lasse die Tür offen*).
- *lassen* + infinitive: to have something done (*Ich lasse das Fahrrad reparieren*); with a dative reflexive for yourself (*Ich lasse mir die Haare schneiden*).
- *lassen* + person + infinitive: to let or make someone do something (*Lass mich helfen. Er lässt uns warten.*). *Lass uns …!* = Let's …!
- Perfect with two infinitives: *Ich habe die Heizung reparieren lassen*; but *Ich habe den Schlüssel im Auto gelassen*.
- *sich lassen* + infinitive = can be done: *Das Fenster lässt sich nicht öffnen.*
- Defects go to the *Hausverwaltung*; for advice on your rights, ask the *Mieterverein*.
