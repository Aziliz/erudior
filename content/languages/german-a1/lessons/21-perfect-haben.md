## What this lesson is for

Until now every sentence in this course has been about the present or the future: what you
do, what you can do, what you will do tomorrow. In this module you finally learn to talk
about the past: what you did at the weekend, where you went on holiday, what happened.

Spoken German uses one main past tense for almost everything: the **perfect tense**
(*das Perfekt*). This lesson shows you how to build it with *haben*, which is the auxiliary
for most verbs. Lesson 22 adds the verbs that use *sein* instead, plus the two short past
forms *war* and *hatte*.

The good news: you already know the sentence pattern. The perfect works exactly like the
modal verbs from lesson 14. One verb sits in position 2, and a second verb waits at the end
of the sentence.

## How the perfect is built

The perfect has two parts:

1. a form of *haben* in the present tense (ich habe, du hast, er hat…). This is the
   **auxiliary verb**: a helper that carries the person and the ending;
2. the **past participle** of the main verb, which goes to the very end of the sentence and
   never changes.

| | haben (position 2) | … | participle (end) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ich | habe | Pizza | gegessen. |
| du | hast | ein Buch | gelesen. |
| er/sie/es | hat | gestern | gearbeitet. |
| wir | haben | einen Ausflug | gemacht. |
| ihr | habt | den Film | gesehen. |
| sie/Sie | haben | das Haus | gekauft. |

- *Ich habe Pizza gegessen.* — I ate pizza. / I've eaten pizza.
- *Hat er gestern gearbeitet?* — Did he work yesterday?

English has two tenses here ("I ate" and "I have eaten"), but in
everyday German the perfect covers both. *Ich habe Pizza gegessen* can answer "What did you
have for lunch?" and "Have you ever eaten pizza?" alike. So don't try to match the English
tense: if it happened in the past and you are speaking, use the perfect.

### The sentence bracket again

The two parts form a bracket around the rest of the sentence, just like *kann … spielen* in
lesson 14. Everything else (the object, the time, the place) goes inside.

- *Ich habe am Samstag meine Oma besucht.* — I visited my grandma on Saturday.
- *Am Samstag habe ich meine Oma besucht.* — On Saturday I visited my grandma.
- *Hast du die Postkarte bekommen?* — Did you get the postcard?
- *Was habt ihr am Wochenende gemacht?* — What did you do at the weekend?

When a time phrase starts the sentence, *haben* still stays in position 2 and the subject
follows it (lesson 12). In a yes/no question *haben* moves to position 1. In a W-question
the question word comes first and *haben* second. The participle is always last.

## Building the past participle

### Pattern 1: regular verbs, ge- + stem + -t

Most verbs are **weak verbs**: their stem doesn't change, and the participle is *ge-* +
stem + *-t*. Take the infinitive, remove *-en*, put *ge-* in front and *-t* at the end.

| infinitive | stem | participle | |
|---|---|---|---|
| machen | mach | gemacht | done, made |
| kaufen | kauf | gekauft | bought |
| spielen | spiel | gespielt | played |
| hören | hör | gehört | heard |
| lernen | lern | gelernt | learned |
| sagen | sag | gesagt | said |
| lachen | lach | gelacht | laughed |
| weinen | wein | geweint | cried |
| glauben | glaub | geglaubt | believed |
| schenken | schenk | geschenkt | given (as a present) |

If the stem ends in *-t* or *-d*, add an extra *e* so you can pronounce it, the same way as
*er arbeitet* in the present:

- *arbeiten* → *gearbeitet* (worked)
- *antworten* → *geantwortet* (answered)
- *warten* → *gewartet* (waited)

Examples:

- *Wir haben viel gelacht.* — We laughed a lot.
- *Das Baby hat die ganze Nacht geweint.* — The baby cried all night.
- *Ich habe meinem Bruder ein Buch geschenkt.* — I gave my brother a book (as a present).
- *Sie hat lange auf den Bus gewartet.* — She waited a long time for the bus.

*Lange* means "for a long time". *Meinem Bruder* is "to my brother", a dative chunk like
*mir* and *dir* in lesson 20.

### Pattern 2: irregular verbs, ge- + stem + -en

**Strong verbs** end in *-en*, and the vowel often changes. You can't work these out from
the infinitive, so learn each participle with its verb. Here are the ones you need most with
*haben*:

| infinitive | participle | | infinitive | participle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sehen | gesehen | | schreiben | geschrieben |
| lesen | gelesen | | sprechen | gesprochen |
| essen | gegessen | | nehmen | genommen |
| geben | gegeben | | helfen | geholfen |
| schlafen | geschlafen | | treffen | getroffen |
| waschen | gewaschen | | finden | gefunden |
| trinken | getrunken | | singen | gesungen |

- *Hast du den Film gesehen?* — Have you seen the film?
- *Ich habe meinen Freund im Park getroffen.* — I met my friend in the park.
- *Er hat mir geholfen.* — He helped me.

A memory hook: the verbs that change their vowel in the present (*sieht, liest, isst,
spricht*) are almost always strong, so they end in *-en* in the participle too.

### Pattern 3: mixed verbs

A small group of **mixed verbs** change the vowel like strong verbs but end in *-t* like weak
ones. At A1 you need four:

| infinitive | participle | |
|---|---|---|
| bringen | gebracht | brought |
| denken | gedacht | thought |
| kennen | gekannt | known (a person or place) |
| wissen | gewusst | known (a fact) |

- *Hast du Brot gebracht?* — Did you bring bread?
- *Das habe ich nicht gewusst!* — I didn't know that!
- *Ich habe an dich gedacht.* — I thought of you.

*Wissen* is also unusual in the present: *ich weiß, du weißt, er/sie/es weiß, wir wissen,
ihr wisst, sie/Sie wissen*. Note that ich and er have the same form, with no *-e* or *-t*.

*Kennen* or *wissen*? Both mean "to know". Use *kennen* for people, places and things you
are familiar with, and *wissen* for facts and information.

- *Kennst du Frau Weber?* — Do you know Ms Weber?
- *Weißt du ihre Telefonnummer?* — Do you know her phone number?
- *Ich kenne Berlin gut.* — I know Berlin well.
- *Das weiß ich nicht.* — I don't know (that).

### Pattern 4: separable verbs, -ge- in the middle

For separable verbs (lesson 12), *ge-* goes between the prefix and the stem, and the whole
participle is written as one word:

| infinitive | participle |
|---|---|
| einkaufen | eingekauft |
| aufräumen | aufgeräumt |
| abholen | abgeholt |
| anrufen | angerufen |
| fernsehen | ferngesehen |
| anfangen | angefangen |
| einladen | eingeladen |
| kennenlernen | kennengelernt |

- *Ich habe gestern eingekauft.* — I went shopping yesterday.
- *Hast du deine Mutter angerufen?* — Did you call your mother?
- *Wir haben Anna im Kurs kennengelernt.* — We got to know (met) Anna on the course.

*Kennenlernen* means "to get to know" or "to meet for the first time". Use *treffen* for
meeting someone you already know.

### Pattern 5: no ge- at all

Two groups of verbs have no *ge-* in the participle.

Verbs ending in *-ieren* end in *-t*: *studieren* → *studiert*, *fotografieren* →
*fotografiert*, *telefonieren* (to talk on the phone) → *telefoniert*.

Verbs with an **inseparable prefix**. The prefixes *be-*, *ver-*, *er-* (and a few
others such as *ent-* and *ge-*) never separate from the verb, are never stressed, and block
the *ge-*. The participle may be weak or strong.

| infinitive | participle | |
|---|---|---|
| besuchen | besucht | visited |
| bezahlen | bezahlt | paid |
| benutzen | benutzt | used |
| bekommen | bekommen | got, received |
| erzählen | erzählt | told |
| verkaufen | verkauft | sold |
| verstehen | verstanden | understood |
| vergessen | vergessen | forgotten |
| verlieren | verloren | lost |

- *Wir haben das Auto verkauft.* — We sold the car.
- *Ich habe meinen Schlüssel verloren.* — I've lost my key.
- *Hast du das verstanden?* — Did you understand that?
- *Opa hat eine Geschichte erzählt.* — Grandpa told a story.
- *Ich habe den Brief bekommen.* — I got the letter.

A quick test: if you can hear the stress on the first syllable of the verb (*EIN-kaufen*,
*AN-rufen*), the prefix is separable and *ge-* goes inside. If the stress is on the stem
(*be-SU-chen*, *ver-KAU-fen*), there is no *ge-*. *Bekommen* and *vergessen* look the same
in the infinitive and the participle; only the sentence tells you which one it is.

## Time words for the past

These words tell you a sentence is about the past:

| German | English |
|---|---|
| gestern | yesterday |
| vorgestern | the day before yesterday |
| gestern Abend | last night, yesterday evening |
| letzte Woche | last week |
| letzten Montag | last Monday |
| letztes Wochenende | last weekend |
| vor zwei Tagen | two days ago |
| damals | back then, at that time |
| gerade | just (a moment ago) |
| schon | already, ever |

*Letzte* ("last") takes the ending of the noun's gender: *letzte Woche* (die Woche),
*letzten Monat* (der Monat), *letztes Wochenende* (das Wochenende). Learn them as chunks.
*Vor* + a time means "ago" and the time comes after it, not before: *vor drei Jahren*
(three years ago). The *-n* on *Jahren* and *Tagen* is the dative plural; just copy it.

- *Ich habe gerade gegessen.* — I've just eaten.
- *Hast du schon die Hausaufgabe gemacht?* — Have you done the homework yet?
- *Vorgestern habe ich einen Brief geschrieben.* — The day before yesterday I wrote a letter.
- *Damals habe ich in Wien gewohnt.* — Back then I lived in Vienna.

## Dialogue: Monday morning

Lena and Tom work in the same office.

> Lena: Hallo Tom! Na, wie war dein Wochenende?
>
> Tom: Super! Am Samstag habe ich meine Eltern besucht.
>
> Lena: Schön. Was habt ihr gemacht?
>
> Tom: Wir haben einen Ausflug gemacht und im Park ein Picknick gemacht. Mein Vater hat
> viele Geschichten von damals erzählt. Wir haben viel gelacht.
>
> Lena: Und am Sonntag?
>
> Tom: Da habe ich lange geschlafen und dann eingekauft. Aber ich habe mein Handy im
> Supermarkt vergessen!
>
> Lena: Oh nein! Hast du es verloren?
>
> Tom: Nein, eine Verkäuferin hat es gefunden und mich angerufen.
>
> Lena: Da hast du aber Glück gehabt!

Translation:

> Lena: Hi Tom! So, how was your weekend?
>
> Tom: Great! On Saturday I visited my parents.
>
> Lena: Nice. What did you do?
>
> Tom: We went on an outing and had a picnic in the park. My dad told lots of stories from
> the old days. We laughed a lot.
>
> Lena: And on Sunday?
>
> Tom: I slept in and then went shopping. But I left my phone in the supermarket!
>
> Lena: Oh no! Have you lost it?
>
> Tom: No, a shop assistant found it and called me.
>
> Lena: You were lucky there!

Notes: *wie war …?* means "how was …?"; *war* comes in lesson 22. *Glück haben* means "to be
lucky", and its perfect is *Glück gehabt*. German says *ein Handy vergessen* for leaving
something behind by accident.

## Pronunciation

- The prefix *ge-* is never stressed and its *e* is a weak, short sound, like the "a" in
  "about": *ge-MACHT*, *ge-SE-hen*. Stress the stem.
- In *-ieren* verbs the stress is on *-ie-*: *stu-DIERT*, *fo-to-gra-FIERT*.
- *Gewusst* has a short *u* and a sharp *ss*: [gəˈvʊst]. Compare *weiß* with a long
  diphthong and *ß*.

## Common mistakes

- Putting the participle in the middle. *Ich habe gegessen Pizza* is English word order.
  The participle goes last: *Ich habe Pizza gegessen.*
- Adding ge- to inseparable verbs. Not *gebesucht* or *geverkauft*, but *besucht*,
  *verkauft*. Not *gestudiert* either, but *studiert*.
- ge- in the wrong place. Not *geeinkauft* or *eingekaufen*, but *eingekauft*.
- Weak endings on strong verbs. Not *gesehet* or *getrinkt*, but *gesehen*, *getrunken*.
- Using "did" for questions. German has no "do/did" helper. "Did you see it?" is
  *Hast du es gesehen?*
- Mixing up kennen and wissen. *Ich weiß Anna* is wrong; you *know* a person with
  *kennen*: *Ich kenne Anna.*

## Key points

- The perfect = *haben* (position 2, with the normal present endings) + past participle
  (last). It translates both "I did" and "I have done".
- Weak verbs: *ge- … -t* (*gemacht*, *gearbeitet*). Strong verbs: *ge- … -en*, often with a
  new vowel (*gesehen*, *getrunken*). Mixed: *gebracht, gedacht, gekannt, gewusst*.
- Separable verbs put *-ge-* after the prefix: *eingekauft*, *angerufen*.
- No *ge-* for *-ieren* verbs and verbs with *be-*, *ver-*, *er-*: *studiert*, *besucht*,
  *verloren*, *erzählt*.
- *Kennen* is for people and places, *wissen* for facts.
- Past time words: *gestern, vorgestern, letzte Woche, vor zwei Tagen, damals, gerade,
  schon*.
