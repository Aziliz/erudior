## What this lesson is for

In lesson 09 you learned to order food and drink with the accusative: *Ich nehme einen Kaffee.* Now you go shopping. You will learn the big numbers (from 100 up to a million), how Germans write and say prices, how to ask what something costs, how to ask for a kilo, a litre or a bottle of something, and how to point at things with *dieser* (this) and ask about them with *welcher* (which). You will also meet *es gibt* (there is, there are), one of the most useful phrases in the language.

## Numbers from 100 up

You already know 0 to 100 (lesson 03). Bigger numbers follow the same logic and are written as one word, however long.

| Number | German |
|---|---|
| 100 | hundert / einhundert |
| 101 | hunderteins |
| 110 | hundertzehn |
| 125 | hundertfünfundzwanzig |
| 200 | zweihundert |
| 350 | dreihundertfünfzig |
| 999 | neunhundertneunundneunzig |
| 1.000 | tausend / eintausend |
| 1.500 | tausendfünfhundert / eintausendfünfhundert |
| 2.026 | zweitausendsechsundzwanzig |
| 10.000 | zehntausend |
| 100.000 | hunderttausend |
| 1.000.000 | eine Million |
| 3.000.000 | drei Millionen |

The pattern is: hundreds, then the rest exactly as you learned it. So 347 is *dreihundert* + *siebenundvierzig* = *dreihundertsiebenundvierzig*. Inside the last two digits the order is still "units and tens": 47 = *sieben-und-vierzig*.

Only *Million* is a separate word, and it is a feminine noun with a plural: *eine Million, zwei Millionen*.

### Writing numbers

German swaps the English comma and point:

- A point (or a small space) separates thousands: *1.000* or *1 000* = one thousand.
- A comma marks decimals: *2,5* = two point five, read *zwei Komma fünf*.

So *1.250* is one thousand two hundred and fifty, not one and a quarter. This matters a lot when you read prices.

## Prices: Euro and Cent

Germany, Austria and most EU countries pay in *Euro* (*der Euro*) and *Cent* (*der Cent*). Prices are written with a comma, and the euro sign usually comes after the number: *3,50 €*.

| Written | Said |
|---|---|
| 1,00 € | ein Euro |
| 3,50 € | drei Euro fünfzig |
| 12,99 € | zwölf Euro neunundneunzig |
| 0,80 € | achtzig Cent |
| 249,00 € | zweihundertneunundvierzig Euro |

Two habits to copy. First, you don't say "and" or "Cent" in the middle: 3,50 € is just *drei Euro fünfzig*. Second, *Euro* doesn't take a plural ending after a number: *zehn Euro*, never *zehn Euros*. The same goes for *Cent*: *fünfzig Cent*.

### Asking what it costs

There are two equally common questions:

- *Was kostet das Brot?* / *Wie viel kostet das Brot?* — How much is the bread?
- *Was kosten die Äpfel?* / *Wie viel kosten die Äpfel?* — How much are the apples?

The verb agrees with the thing: one thing *kostet*, several things *kosten*. The answer: *Das Brot kostet 2,80 €.* *Die Äpfel kosten 3,20 € das Kilo* (3.20 a kilo). At the till you'll hear the total as *Das macht 8,40 €* — That's 8.40.

*wie viel* (how much, how many) is two words. You also use it for other quantities: *Wie viel Geld hast du?* — How much money do you have?

## Quantities: no "of"

To buy a kilo of apples, say *ein Kilo Äpfel*. German puts the amount straight in front of the food, with no word for "of".

| German | English |
|---|---|
| ein Kilo Äpfel | a kilo of apples |
| 200 Gramm Käse | 200 grams of cheese |
| ein Liter Milch | a litre of milk |
| eine Flasche Wein | a bottle of wine |
| eine Packung Nudeln | a packet of pasta |
| ein Stück Kuchen | a piece of cake |

Measures that are masculine or neuter (*das Kilo*, *das Gramm*, *der Liter*, *das Stück*) stay singular after a number: *zwei Kilo Kartoffeln, drei Liter Wasser, zwei Stück Kuchen*. Feminine containers take their plural: *zwei Flaschen Wasser, drei Packungen Reis*. An easy rule of thumb: if it ends in *-e* or *-ung* like *Flasche* or *Packung*, use the plural.

The amount and the food together are one object, so the article of the measure word changes as usual: *Ich kaufe einen Liter Milch* (der Liter → einen).

## dieser and welcher

*dieser* means "this" (or "that", when you point); a pointing word like this is called a **demonstrative**. *welcher* means "which". Both take the same endings as *der, die, das* — compare the last letters.

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| article | der / den | die | das | die |
| nominative | dieser / welcher | diese / welche | dieses / welches | diese / welche |
| accusative | diesen / welchen | diese / welche | dieses / welches | diese / welche |

Once again only the masculine changes in the accusative, and it gets the same *-n* as *den*: *der → den*, *dieser → diesen*, *welcher → welchen*.

- *Welcher Käse ist gut?* — Which cheese is good? *Dieser Käse ist sehr gut.* — This cheese is very good.
- *Welchen Wein möchten Sie?* — Which wine would you like? *Ich nehme diesen Wein.* — I'll take this wine.
- *Welche Flasche kostet 4 Euro?* — Which bottle costs 4 euros? *Diese.* — This one.
- *Welches Brot ist frisch?* — Which bread is fresh? (*frisch* = fresh) *Dieses Brot hier.* — This bread here.

You can drop the noun when it's clear: *Ich nehme diesen.* — I'll take this one (a masculine thing).

## es gibt: there is, there are

*es gibt* means "there is" or "there are". It never changes for plural, and the thing that exists is in the accusative.

- *Hier gibt es einen Supermarkt.* — There's a supermarket here.
- *Gibt es hier eine Bäckerei?* — Is there a bakery here?
- *Heute gibt es Fisch.* — There's fish today.
- *Es gibt keinen Reis mehr.* — There's no rice left. (*mehr* = more, any more)
- *Es gibt viele Geschäfte.* — There are many shops. (*viele* = many)

Use *es gibt* for what exists or is available somewhere. Use *Das ist …* to identify a thing in front of you.

## Shopping verbs

All of these take an accusative object.

| German | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| kaufen | to buy | *Ich kaufe einen Apfel.* — I'm buying an apple. |
| brauchen | to need | *Wir brauchen Brot und Milch.* — We need bread and milk. |
| suchen | to look for | *Ich suche den Markt.* — I'm looking for the market. |
| bezahlen | to pay (for) | *Er bezahlt den Kaffee.* — He's paying for the coffee. |
| kosten | to cost | *Das kostet 5 Euro.* — That costs 5 euros. |

Note that *suchen* and *bezahlen* need no preposition: "look *for*" and "pay *for*" are just *suchen* and *bezahlen* plus the object.

Some useful little words: *viel* (a lot, much), *wenig* (little, not much), *alles* (everything), *zusammen* (together). *Das kostet viel.* — That costs a lot. *Ich habe wenig Geld.* — I have little money. *Ist das alles?* — Is that everything? In a café the waiter asks *Zusammen oder getrennt?* — Together or separately? (*getrennt* = separately.)

## Dialogue: At the market

> Verkäuferin: Guten Tag! Was brauchen Sie?\
> Lena: Guten Tag! Was kosten die Äpfel?\
> Verkäuferin: Welche? Diese hier kosten 2,40 € das Kilo, und diese dort 3,10 €.\
> Lena: Dann nehme ich die hier. Ein Kilo, bitte. Und 200 Gramm Käse.\
> Verkäuferin: Gern. Welchen Käse möchten Sie? Der Gouda ist heute im Angebot: 100 Gramm kosten nur 1,20 €.\
> Lena: Super, dann 200 Gramm Gouda. Gibt es auch Eier?\
> Verkäuferin: Ja, natürlich. Zehn Stück kosten 3,20 €.\
> Lena: Gut, zehn Eier, bitte. Das ist alles. Was macht das zusammen?\
> Verkäuferin: Das macht zusammen acht Euro.\
> Lena: Hier sind zehn Euro.\
> Verkäuferin: Und zwei Euro zurück. Vielen Dank und einen schönen Tag noch!

In English:

> Saleswoman: Hello! What do you need?\
> Lena: Hello! How much are the apples?\
> Saleswoman: Which ones? These here are 2.40 a kilo, and those there 3.10.\
> Lena: Then I'll take these here. A kilo, please. And 200 grams of cheese.\
> Saleswoman: Sure. Which cheese would you like? The Gouda is on special offer today: 100 grams cost only 1.20.\
> Lena: Great, 200 grams of Gouda then. Are there eggs too?\
> Saleswoman: Yes, of course. Ten cost 3.20.\
> Lena: Good, ten eggs, please. That's everything. What does that come to altogether?\
> Saleswoman: That's eight euros altogether.\
> Lena: Here's ten euros.\
> Saleswoman: And two euros back. Thank you and have a nice day!

Check the maths: 2,40 + 2,40 + 3,20 = 8,00 €. *Verkäuferin* is a female shop assistant (lesson 07 had *der Verkäufer*). *im Angebot* means "on special offer"; take it as a fixed phrase. *zurück* = back; *einen schönen Tag noch* = have a nice day.

## Places to shop

*das Geschäft* is the general word for a shop. *der Supermarkt* is a supermarket, *der Markt* an open-air market, *die Bäckerei* a bakery (from *der Bäcker*, the baker). In a supermarket you pay at *die Kasse*, the checkout, and you look for *das Angebot*, the special offer. *der Preis* is the price: *Der Preis ist gut.*

## Pronunciation

- *Euro* is "OY-ro": *eu* sounds like the *oy* in "boy".
- *Bäckerei* is stressed on the last syllable: bä-cke-REI. Most words ending in *-ei* do this.
- *Supermarkt*: stress on the first syllable, SU-per-markt, and say the *k* and the *t*.
- *Million* is mil-YOHN, stressed on the last syllable.
- *Gramm* and *Kilo*: short *a* in *Gramm*, long *i* in *Kilo*.

## Common mistakes

- *zehn Euros*, *zwei Kilos* — No plural after a number: *zehn Euro, zwei Kilo*.
- *ein Kilo von Äpfeln* — No *von* after a measure: *ein Kilo Äpfel*.
- *Wie viel kosten das?* — One thing, singular verb: *Wie viel kostet das?*
- Reading *1.500 €* as "one and a half euros". The point is the thousands separator: 1,500 euros.
- *Welche Käse möchten Sie?* — *Käse* is masculine and the object: *Welchen Käse …?*
- *Es gibt ein Supermarkt.* — *es gibt* takes the accusative: *einen Supermarkt*.
- *Ich suche für eine Bäckerei.* — No preposition: *Ich suche eine Bäckerei.*

## Key points

- Big numbers are one word: *dreihundertsiebenundvierzig*. Only *Million* is separate.
- A point separates thousands, a comma marks decimals and cents: *1.000*, *3,50 €*.
- Say prices as *drei Euro fünfzig*; no plural after numbers: *zehn Euro*.
- Amount + food with no "of": *ein Kilo Äpfel, eine Flasche Wasser*.
- *dieser* and *welcher* take the endings of *der/die/das*; masculine accusative is *diesen, welchen*.
- *es gibt* + accusative = there is / there are.
