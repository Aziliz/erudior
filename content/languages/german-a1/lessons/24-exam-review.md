## What this lesson is for

This is the last lesson of the course. It does three things. First, it explains what
"A1" actually means and what you can now do. Second, it describes the best-known A1 exam,
the Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1, with strategies for each part. Third, it reviews
the grammar of the whole course and tells you where to look if something is still shaky.
The questions at the end of this lesson cover all 24 lessons, so treat them as a final
check.

You don't have to take an exam. But even if you never sit one, the free official practice
tests are a good way to see whether you have really reached A1.

## What A1 means

The levels A1 to C2 come from the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages
(CEFR), used across Europe and beyond. A1 is the first level. In plain words, a learner at
A1 can:

- understand and use very common phrases from everyday life, as long as they are about
  concrete, immediate needs: shopping, food, times, directions;
- introduce themselves and other people, and ask and answer simple questions about where
  someone lives, who they know and what they have;
- take part in a simple conversation, if the other person talks slowly and clearly and is
  willing to help.

That last point matters. At A1 nobody expects you to follow a fast conversation between two
native speakers. You are expected to manage with patience on both sides, and to ask for help
when you need it. That is a skill too, and this lesson gives you the words for it.

## The Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1

The exam has four parts. Hören, Lesen and Schreiben are written in one block of about 65
minutes; Sprechen is a separate oral exam.

| part | time (about) | what it tests |
|---|---|---|
| Hören (listening) | 20 minutes | short everyday conversations, telephone messages, announcements |
| Lesen (reading) | 25 minutes | short notes and e-mails, signs and notices, small ads |
| Schreiben (writing) | 20 minutes | filling in a form; writing a short message of about 30 words |
| Sprechen (speaking) | 15 minutes | in a small group, with no time to prepare: introducing yourself, asking and answering questions on a topic with word cards, making requests and responding |

There are 100 points in total, and you pass with 60. The grades are:

| points | grade |
|---|---|
| 90–100 | sehr gut (very good) |
| 80–89 | gut (good) |
| 70–79 | befriedigend (satisfactory) |
| 60–69 | ausreichend (sufficient, a pass) |

The Goethe-Institut publishes the official description and free practice material,
including a full model test (*Modellsatz*) with audio and answers:

- [Exam overview: Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1](https://www.goethe.de/ins/si/de/spr/prf/gzsd1.html)
- [Free practice material (Modellsatz and Übungssätze)](https://www.goethe.de/de/spr/prf/ueb/pa1.html)

Details such as dates, fees and the exact format can change, so always check with the exam
centre where you register. Other A1 exams (for example telc or ÖSD) are similar in level.

## Strategies for each part

### Hören

- Read the questions before you listen. They tell you what to listen for: a time, a price,
  a place, a day.
- You don't need every word. Listen for the key facts: numbers, times and days (lessons 3,
  10, 11, 16) come up again and again.
- Watch out for changes: *Der Termin ist am Montag … nein, am Dienstag.* The second piece of
  information is usually the one that counts.

### Lesen

- Look at the type of text first: a sign, an ad, a short e-mail. Then read the question,
  then look for the part of the text that answers it.
- Signs and notices use the words of lesson 15: *geöffnet, geschlossen, verboten, nicht
  rauchen, bitte warten*.
- Don't panic about unknown words. Many tasks can be answered with the words you know.

### Schreiben

- In the form, copy the information exactly, with correct capital letters and umlauts.
  Dates go day.month.year (lesson 23).
- In the message, cover every point the task gives you. A point you forget costs more than
  a small grammar mistake.
- Use a correct frame: *Liebe …, / Lieber …,* or *Sehr geehrte …,* and a matching closing.
  Link your sentences with *und, aber, denn*.
- Check at the end: verb in position 2? Nouns with capitals? *du* or *Sie* all the way
  through?

### Sprechen

- Prepare your self-introduction at home until it is automatic: name, age, country, where you
  live, languages, job, hobbies. Use lessons 2, 3, 6, 7 and 13.
- When you get a word card, make a simple, correct question: *Was isst du gern zum
  Frühstück?* A short question you can say clearly is better than a long one you can't
  finish.
- For requests, use the polite forms from lessons 14, 15 and 17: *Können Sie bitte …? Gib
  mir bitte …, Darf ich …?* And answer other people's requests: *Ja, gern. / Hier, bitte. /
  Nein, tut mir leid.*
- If you don't understand, ask. That is a normal part of the conversation.

## Words for the classroom and the exam

These phrases help in any lesson, exam or real conversation:

- *Können Sie das bitte wiederholen?* — Could you repeat that, please?
- *Ich verstehe das nicht.* — I don't understand (that).
- *Was bedeutet das?* — What does that mean?
- *Wie sagt man das auf Deutsch?* — How do you say that in German?
- *Können Sie das bitte erklären?* — Could you explain that, please?

And these are typical words in exam instructions and textbooks:

| German | English |
|---|---|
| die Aufgabe | task, exercise |
| der Teil | part |
| der Text | text |
| das Beispiel | example |
| die Lösung | answer, solution |
| die Seite | page |
| der Punkt | point |
| der Fehler | mistake |
| ankreuzen | to tick, to mark with a cross |
| ergänzen | to complete, to fill in (a gap) |
| üben | to practise |

- *Lesen Sie den Text und kreuzen Sie an.* — Read the text and tick (the right answer).
- *Ergänzen Sie die Wörter.* — Fill in the words.
- *Wie viele Punkte hast du?* — How many points have you got?
- *Ich habe drei Fehler gemacht.* — I made three mistakes.

### etwas, nichts, jemand, niemand, alle, beide, genug

These small words are everywhere, so they finish off your A1 vocabulary:

- *Hast du etwas gesagt? — Nein, ich habe nichts gesagt.* — Did you say something? — No, I
  didn't say anything.
- *Ist jemand da? — Nein, hier ist niemand.* — Is anybody there? — No, nobody's here.
- *Alle sind gekommen.* — Everybody came.
- *Wir haben beide bestanden.* — We both passed.
- *Hast du genug Zeit?* — Do you have enough time?

Note that German uses one negative word only: *Ich habe nichts gesagt* ("I have nothing
said"), never *nicht nichts*. *Nichts* and *etwas* never change their form.

If you had to remember only three things from this course, make them these: the verb is
always the second element in a statement; every noun has a gender, so learn it with *der*,
*die* or *das*; and the second verb (modal, perfect, separable prefix) goes to the end.

## Dialogue: practising for the speaking test

Ben and Yuki are practising together for the speaking part.

> Ben: Okay, du bist dran. Stell dich bitte vor.
>
> Yuki: Ich heiße Yuki. Ich bin 28 Jahre alt und komme aus Japan. Ich wohne seit einem
> Jahr in Leipzig. Ich spreche Japanisch, Englisch und ein bisschen Deutsch.
>
> Ben: Sehr gut! Jetzt die Karte. Das Thema ist „Freizeit“, das Wort ist „Sport“.
>
> Yuki: Machst du gern Sport?
>
> Ben: Ja, ich spiele Fußball. Was bedeutet „Verein“?
>
> Yuki: Das weiß ich nicht. Ich glaube, das ist eine Gruppe. Wie sagt man das auf Englisch?
>
> Ben: „Club“. Gut, jetzt eine Bitte. Deine Karte zeigt ein Fenster.
>
> Yuki: Kannst du bitte das Fenster öffnen?
>
> Ben: Ja, natürlich. Siehst du? Die Prüfung ist kein Problem für uns!

Translation:

> Ben: OK, it's your turn. Introduce yourself, please.
>
> Yuki: My name is Yuki. I'm 28 and I'm from Japan. I've been living in Leipzig for a year.
> I speak Japanese, English and a little German.
>
> Ben: Very good! Now the card. The topic is "free time", the word is "sport".
>
> Yuki: Do you like doing sport?
>
> Ben: Yes, I play football. What does "Verein" mean?
>
> Yuki: I don't know. I think it's a group. How do you say it in English?
>
> Ben: "Club". OK, now a request. Your card shows a window.
>
> Yuki: Can you open the window, please?
>
> Ben: Yes, of course. See? The exam is no problem for us!

Notes: *du bist dran* means "it's your turn"; *sich vorstellen* is "to introduce yourself";
*der Verein* is a club or association.

## Pronunciation

- *wiederholen* is stressed on *-ho-*: wie-der-HO-len. The *ie* is a long "ee".
- *jemand* and *niemand* are stressed on the first syllable: JE-mand, NIE-mand.
- *ergänzen*: stress on *-gän-*, and *z* is "ts": er-GÄN-tsen.
- *ankreuzen*: AN-kreu-zen, with *eu* like "oy".

## Common mistakes

- Answering a different question. In the speaking test, listen for the question word: *wo*
  (where), *wohin* (where to), *woher* (where from), *wann* (when), *wie lange* (how long).
- Leaving out a point in the message task. Tick off each point as you write.
- Translating word for word from English: *Ich bin 28 Jahre alt* (not *Ich habe 28 Jahre*),
  *Mir ist kalt* (not *Ich bin kalt*).
- Double negatives: *Ich habe nichts gesagt* (not *Ich habe nicht nichts gesagt*).
- Staying silent. If you didn't understand, say *Wie bitte?* or *Können Sie das bitte
  wiederholen?*

## What comes next

At A2 you will learn to talk about your past in more detail, compare things (*größer, am
größten*), use adjective endings (*ein großes Haus*), say why with *weil* and give opinions
with *dass*, and use more dative and two-way prepositions. Keep your vocabulary reviews
going: the words you learned here are the foundation for everything that follows. Reading
graded readers from the reading list and listening to slow German podcasts are the best
ways to keep growing on your own.

## Key points

- A1 means you can manage simple, everyday situations if people speak slowly and help.
- The Goethe A1 exam has four parts: Hören (about 20 min), Lesen (about 25 min), Schreiben
  (about 20 min), Sprechen (about 15 min, in a group). You need 60 of 100 points to pass.
- Practise with the free official model tests on goethe.de.
- Ask for help in German: *Können Sie das bitte wiederholen? Was bedeutet das?*
- *Etwas/nichts, jemand/niemand, alle, beide, genug* complete your A1 word list.
