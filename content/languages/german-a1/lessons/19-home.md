## What this lesson is for

In lesson 18 you learned the dative articles (*dem, der, dem, den*) after prepositions like *mit*
and *zu*. This lesson uses the same dative for a new job: saying where something is. *Der Schlüssel
ist auf dem Tisch.* — The key is on the table. *Wir sind im Wohnzimmer.* — We're in the living room.

You'll also learn the rooms and furniture of a flat, how to talk about rent, and how to refer to a
thing with *er*, *sie* or *es*, or *ihn* in the accusative: *Der Schrank? Ich finde ihn schön.*

## Rooms and furniture

| German | English | German | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| *die Wohnung* | flat, apartment | *das Sofa* | sofa, couch |
| *das Haus* | house | *der Schrank* | cupboard, wardrobe |
| *das Zimmer* | room | *das Regal* | shelf, bookcase |
| *die Küche* | kitchen | *der Kühlschrank* | fridge |
| *das Bad* | bathroom | *die Wand* | wall |
| *das Wohnzimmer* | living room | *der Boden* | floor (that you walk on) |
| *das Schlafzimmer* | bedroom | *die Treppe* | stairs, staircase |
| *der Balkon* | balcony | *der Stock* | floor, storey |
| *der Garten* | garden, yard | *die Miete* | rent |

From earlier lessons you already know *der Tisch*, *der Stuhl*, *die Lampe*, *das Fenster*, *die Tür*
(lesson 5) and *das Bett* (lesson 12).

Two useful facts about German flats:

- A *Drei-Zimmer-Wohnung* (three-room flat) has three rooms plus a kitchen and a bathroom. The
  kitchen and bathroom aren't counted.
- Floors are counted from the ground floor, *das Erdgeschoss*. So *im ersten Stock* is the first
  floor in British English but the second floor in American English.

Describing a flat uses adjectives after *sein* (lesson 8): *hell* (bright, light), *dunkel* (dark),
*modern* (modern), *ruhig* (quiet), *gemütlich* (cosy, comfortable). *Gemütlich* is a favourite
German word: a room that is warm, relaxed and pleasant to be in.

To say what a flat has, use *es gibt* or *haben* with the accusative (lesson 9):

- *Die Wohnung hat einen Balkon.* — The flat has a balcony.
- *In der Küche gibt es einen Kühlschrank.* — There's a fridge in the kitchen.
- *Es gibt leider keinen Garten.* — Unfortunately there's no garden.

The verb *mieten* means to rent: *Wir mieten eine Wohnung.* — We're renting a flat. And *zu Hause*
means at home: *Ich bin heute zu Hause.* — I'm at home today.

## Where is it? Prepositions + dative

To say where something is, answer the question *Wo?* with one of these prepositions and the
dative:

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *in* | in, inside | *Die Milch ist im Kühlschrank.* — The milk is in the fridge. |
| *an* | on (a vertical surface), at, by | *Das Foto ist an der Wand.* — The photo is on the wall. |
| *auf* | on (top of) | *Das Buch ist auf dem Tisch.* — The book is on the table. |
| *unter* | under, below | *Die Katze ist unter dem Bett.* — The cat is under the bed. |
| *über* | over, above | *Die Lampe ist über dem Tisch.* — The lamp is above the table. |
| *neben* | next to, beside | *Das Regal ist neben dem Schrank.* — The shelf is next to the cupboard. |
| *zwischen* | between | *Der Stuhl ist zwischen dem Sofa und dem Fenster.* — The chair is between the sofa and the window. |
| *hinter* | behind | *Der Garten ist hinter dem Haus.* — The garden is behind the house. |
| *vor* | in front of | *Das Taxi ist vor dem Haus.* — The taxi is in front of the house. |

You know *vor* from lesson 11 as "before" (*vor dem Essen*); with places it means "in front of".

These nine prepositions are called **two-way prepositions**. With *Wo?* (location, no movement),
they take the dative. With *Wohin?* (movement to a place), they take the accusative: *Ich lege das
Buch auf den Tisch* (I put the book onto the table). That second use is for later; at A1 you only
need location, so: *Wo?* → dative.

### Contractions: im and am

Just as *zu dem* becomes *zum*, two more pairs fuse:

| Long form | Contraction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *in dem* | *im* | *im Wohnzimmer, im Bad, im Garten, im ersten Stock* |
| *an dem* | *am* | *am Fenster, am Tisch* |

Feminine nouns have no contraction: *in der Küche*, *an der Wand*, *an der Tür*.

### in, an or auf?

English often says "on" or "at" where German makes a finer choice:

- *auf* — on top of a horizontal surface: *auf dem Tisch*, *auf dem Sofa*, *auf dem Boden*, *auf dem
  Balkon*.
- *an* — touching a vertical surface, or right next to an edge: *an der Wand*, *am Fenster*, *an der
  Tür*.
- *in* — inside something closed or a room: *im Schrank*, *in der Küche*, *im Garten*.

*Im Garten* but *auf dem Balkon*: a garden is an area you are inside; a balcony is a platform you
stand on.

## Pronouns for things: er, sie, es and ihn

In English every thing is "it". In German, a pronoun takes the gender of the noun it replaces, so a
table is "he" and a lamp is "she":

| Noun | Nominative (subject) | Accusative (object) |
|---|---|---|
| *der Schrank* | *er* | *ihn* |
| *die Lampe* | *sie* | *sie* |
| *das Sofa* | *es* | *es* |
| *die Stühle* (plural) | *sie* | *sie* |

Only the masculine changes in the accusative: *er → ihn*, just as *der → den*.

- *Wo ist der Schlüssel? — Er ist auf dem Tisch.* — Where's the key? It's on the table.
- *Wie findest du den Schrank? — Ich finde ihn toll.* — What do you think of the cupboard? I think
  it's great.
- *Die Wohnung ist schön. Ich möchte sie mieten.* — The flat is nice. I'd like to rent it.
- *Das Regal ist neu. Ich finde es praktisch.* — The shelf is new. I find it practical.

*Wie findest du …? — Ich finde … + adjective* is the normal way to ask for and give an opinion.

## A text: Mein Zimmer

> Ich wohne in einer Wohnung im zweiten Stock. Mein Zimmer ist klein, aber hell und gemütlich. Das
> Bett ist an der Wand, neben dem Fenster. Unter dem Bett ist mein Koffer. Der Schrank ist hinter der
> Tür. Über dem Tisch ist eine Lampe, und auf dem Regal sind viele Bücher. Am liebsten bin ich aber im
> Wohnzimmer auf dem Sofa.

*I live in a flat on the second floor. My room is small but bright and cosy. The bed is against the
wall, next to the window. My suitcase is under the bed. The wardrobe is behind the door. There's a
lamp above the table, and there are lots of books on the shelf. But my favourite place is the living
room, on the sofa.*

## Dialogue: Eine Wohnung besichtigen

Mr Okafor is viewing a flat. The landlady (*die Vermieterin*) shows him round.

> Vermieterin: Guten Tag, Herr Okafor. Kommen Sie herein!
>
> Herr Okafor: Guten Tag. Die Wohnung ist im dritten Stock, oder?
>
> Vermieterin: Ja, und es gibt leider keinen Aufzug. Hier links ist die Küche.
>
> Herr Okafor: Oh, die Küche ist schön hell. Gibt es auch einen Kühlschrank?
>
> Vermieterin: Ja, der Kühlschrank ist dort, neben dem Fenster.
>
> Herr Okafor: Und wo ist das Bad?
>
> Vermieterin: Das Bad ist rechts, zwischen dem Schlafzimmer und dem Wohnzimmer.
>
> Herr Okafor: Das Wohnzimmer ist wirklich gemütlich. Hat die Wohnung einen Balkon?
>
> Vermieterin: Ja, hier hinter der Tür ist der Balkon.
>
> Herr Okafor: Toll! Und wie hoch ist die Miete?
>
> Vermieterin: 930 Euro warm.
>
> Herr Okafor: Ich finde die Wohnung super. Ich möchte sie mieten!

*Landlady: Hello, Mr Okafor. Come in! — Mr Okafor: Hello. The flat is on the third floor, isn't it? —
Landlady: Yes, and unfortunately there's no lift. Here on the left is the kitchen. — Mr Okafor: Oh,
the kitchen is nice and bright. Is there a fridge too? — Landlady: Yes, the fridge is over there,
next to the window. — Mr Okafor: And where's the bathroom? — Landlady: The bathroom is on the right,
between the bedroom and the living room. — Mr Okafor: The living room is really cosy. Does the flat
have a balcony? — Landlady: Yes, the balcony is here behind the door. — Mr Okafor: Great! And how
much is the rent? — Landlady: 930 euros including heating and bills. — Mr Okafor: I think the flat
is great. I'd like to rent it!*

*Herein* means "in" (into here), *der Aufzug* is the lift (elevator), and *wie hoch* means "how high".
*Warm* rent includes heating and service costs; *kalt* rent doesn't. Always ask which one you are
being told.

## Pronunciation

- *Küche* and *Kuchen* (cake, lesson 9) are a famous pair. *Küche* has *ü* and the soft *ich*
  sound; *Kuchen* has *u* and the throaty *ach* sound.
- *Regal*: stress on the second syllable, re-GAAL. *Balkon* is stressed on *-kon*; many Germans say
  it like French, bal-KONG.
- *gemütlich*: ge-MÜT-lich, long *ü*, soft *ch* at the end.
- *Kühlschrank*: long *ü* (the *h* makes it long), then *sch*.
- *zwischen*: *z* = *ts*, then *w* = English *v*: TSVI-shen.

## Common mistakes

- Using the nominative or accusative for location: *Das Buch ist auf der Tisch* or *auf den Tisch* is
  wrong. *Tisch* is masculine, so: *auf dem Tisch*.
- Using *es* for every thing: *Der Schrank ist groß. Es ist neu.* should be *Er ist neu.*
- Forgetting *ihn*: *Ich finde er schön* is wrong; the object form is *Ich finde ihn schön.*
- Saying *auf der Wand*: pictures hang *an der Wand*. *Auf* is for horizontal surfaces.
- Writing *in dem* and *an dem* where Germans say *im* and *am*.
- Mixing up *der Boden* (the floor you stand on) and *der Stock* (a storey of a building).

## Key points

- *Wo?* + *in, an, auf, unter, über, neben, zwischen, hinter, vor* + dative: *auf dem Tisch*, *an der
  Wand*, *im Bad*.
- Contractions: *im* = *in dem*, *am* = *an dem*; none for feminine *in der*, *an der*.
- *Auf* = on top, *an* = on a wall or at an edge, *in* = inside.
- Pronouns follow the noun's gender: *der Schrank → er / ihn*, *die Lampe → sie*, *das Sofa → es*.
- *Wie findest du …? — Ich finde ihn/sie/es …* for opinions.
- A *Drei-Zimmer-Wohnung* has three rooms plus kitchen and bathroom; *im ersten Stock* is one floor up.
