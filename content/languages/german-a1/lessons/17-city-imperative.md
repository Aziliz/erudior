## What this lesson is for

Module 4 was about free time, plans and rules. Module 5 takes you out of the door: into town, onto
trains, into a flat and, finally, to the doctor. This first lesson gives you the places you find in
any German town, the words for giving and following directions, and a new verb form, the
**imperative**, which you use to tell or ask someone to do something: *Gehen Sie geradeaus!* — Go
straight ahead! *Nimm die zweite Straße links!* — Take the second street on the left!

You already know most of the pieces. The imperative is built from the present-tense forms you
learned in lesson 4, and the stem changes from lesson 13 (*nehmen → du nimmst*) come back here.
Separable verbs from lesson 12 behave exactly as they do in statements: the prefix goes to the end.

## Places in town

Learn every place with its article; you'll need the gender in the next lesson.

| German | English | German | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| *die Straße* | street, road | *die Kirche* | church |
| *der Platz* | square, place | *der Park* | park |
| *die Bank* | bank | *die Brücke* | bridge |
| *die Post* | post office | *das Zentrum* | centre, town centre |
| *die Apotheke* | pharmacy | *die Haltestelle* | stop (bus, tram) |
| *das Krankenhaus* | hospital | *die Ecke* | corner |
| *das Hotel* | hotel | *die Ampel* | traffic lights |
| *das Restaurant* | restaurant | *der Stadtplan* | city map |
| *das Café* | café | *der Weg* | way, path |
| *das Museum* | museum | | |

To ask whether something exists nearby, use *es gibt* (lesson 10) with the accusative:

- *Gibt es hier in der Nähe eine Apotheke?* — Is there a pharmacy nearby?
- *Gibt es hier einen Park?* — Is there a park here?
- *Im Zentrum gibt es viele Cafés.* — There are lots of cafés in the town centre.

To ask where a particular place is, use *Wo ist …?*:

- *Entschuldigung, wo ist die Post?* — Excuse me, where is the post office?
- *Wo ist hier ein Hotel?* — Where is there a hotel around here?

*In der Nähe* (nearby) is a fixed chunk; learn it as a whole. *Im Zentrum* means "in the centre";
you'll see why *in dem* becomes *im* in lesson 19.

## Directions: links, rechts, geradeaus

| German | English |
|---|---|
| *links* | (on the) left |
| *rechts* | (on the) right |
| *geradeaus* | straight ahead, straight on |
| *die erste / zweite / dritte Straße* | the first / second / third street |
| *an der Ampel* | at the traffic lights |
| *an der Ecke* | on the corner |
| *bis zur Ampel* | as far as the traffic lights |
| *weit* | far |
| *nah* | near, close |
| *zu Fuß* | on foot |
| *gegenüber* | opposite, across the street |

Some examples:

- *Die Bank ist links.* — The bank is on the left.
- *Das Hotel ist nicht weit. Sie können zu Fuß gehen.* — The hotel isn't far. You can walk.
- *Der Park ist ganz nah.* — The park is very close.
- *Die Apotheke ist direkt gegenüber.* — The pharmacy is directly opposite.
- *Die Post ist gegenüber der Kirche.* — The post office is opposite the church.
- *Biegen Sie an der Ecke rechts ab.* — Turn right at the corner.

*An der Ampel*, *an der Ecke*, *bis zur Ampel* and *gegenüber der Kirche* use the dative case,
which you'll learn properly in lessons 18 and 19. For now, treat them as ready-made chunks.

### Asking the way: Wie komme ich zum …?

The standard question is *Wie komme ich zum … / zur …?* — How do I get to the …?

- *Wie komme ich zum Museum?* — How do I get to the museum?
- *Wie komme ich zum Bahnhof?* — How do I get to the station? (*der Bahnhof*, station)
- *Wie komme ich zur Post?* — How do I get to the post office?

Use *zum* before masculine and neuter places and *zur* before feminine places. That's why you
learn the gender with every noun. Lesson 18 explains where *zum* and *zur* come from (*zu dem*,
*zu der*). If you want someone to show you the way, say *Können Sie mir den Weg zeigen?* — Can you
show me the way? (*mir* = me, to me; more in lesson 20.)

## The imperative: telling people what to do

German has three imperative forms, one for each way of saying "you": *du*, *ihr* and *Sie*. In all
three, the verb comes first.

### The Sie form: the polite imperative

Take the *Sie* form of the present tense and swap the order: verb first, then *Sie*.

| Statement | Imperative |
|---|---|
| *Sie gehen geradeaus.* | *Gehen Sie geradeaus!* — Go straight ahead! |
| *Sie nehmen die erste Straße.* | *Nehmen Sie die erste Straße!* — Take the first street! |
| *Sie warten hier.* | *Warten Sie hier!* — Wait here! |

The *Sie* is never dropped. It looks like a yes/no question (*Gehen Sie geradeaus?*); your voice
falls at the end of an instruction instead of rising, and in writing you use *!* or a full stop.

### The du form: drop the pronoun and the ending

Start from the *du* form, drop *du*, and drop the ending *-st* (or just *-t* when the stem ends in
*s*, *ß* or *z*):

| Infinitive | du form | du imperative |
|---|---|---|
| *gehen* | *du gehst* | *Geh!* — Go! |
| *kommen* | *du kommst* | *Komm!* — Come! |
| *zeigen* | *du zeigst* | *Zeig!* — Show! |
| *warten* | *du wartest* | *Warte!* — Wait! |
| *finden* | *du findest* | *Finde!* — Find! |
| *nehmen* | *du nimmst* | *Nimm!* — Take! |
| *sprechen* | *du sprichst* | *Sprich!* — Speak! |
| *lesen* | *du liest* | *Lies!* — Read! |
| *essen* | *du isst* | *Iss!* — Eat! |

Two details:

1. e → i and e → ie verbs keep their change: *nimm*, *sprich*, *lies*, *iss*, *gib*, *hilf*.
   This is why starting from the *du* form works so well.
2. a → ä verbs lose the umlaut: *du fährst → Fahr!*, *du schläfst → Schlaf gut!* — Sleep well!

Verbs whose stem ends in *-t* or *-d* keep an *-e*: *Warte!*, *Finde!*, *Antworte!* — Answer!
With other verbs you may also see an *-e* in careful writing (*Gehe!*, *Komme!*), but in everyday
speech the short form is normal.

### The ihr form: the ihr verb without ihr

For two or more friends, use the normal *ihr* form and drop *ihr*:

| ihr form | ihr imperative |
|---|---|
| *ihr geht* | *Geht!* — Go! |
| *ihr nehmt* | *Nehmt den Bus!* — Take the bus! (*der Bus*, bus) |
| *ihr fahrt* | *Fahrt langsam!* — Drive slowly! |
| *ihr wartet* | *Wartet hier!* — Wait here! |

No stem change appears in the *ihr* imperative, because there is none in the *ihr* form.

### sein is irregular

| du | ihr | Sie |
|---|---|---|
| *Sei!* | *Seid!* | *Seien Sie!* |

- *Sei bitte pünktlich!* — Please be on time!
- *Seid leise, bitte!* — Please be quiet!
- *Seien Sie vorsichtig!* — Be careful! (*vorsichtig*, careful)

### Separable verbs: the prefix goes to the end

*Abbiegen* (to turn) is a separable verb. In the imperative, the verb part comes first and the
prefix goes to the end, just as in a statement:

- *Biegen Sie an der Ampel links ab!* — Turn left at the lights!
- *Bieg an der Ecke rechts ab!* — Turn right at the corner!
- *Biegt hier ab!* — Turn here!

### Making it polite

An imperative on its own can sound blunt. Add *bitte*, at the start or after the verb:

- *Bitte warten Sie hier.* / *Warten Sie bitte hier.* — Please wait here.
- *Zeig mir bitte den Weg.* — Please show me the way.

The word *mal* (roughly "just") also softens a request: *Komm mal!* — Come here a moment! For a
very polite request, a question with a modal verb (lesson 14) is often better: *Können Sie das
bitte noch einmal sagen?* — Could you say that again, please?

## Dialogue: Wie komme ich zum Museum?

A tourist stops a woman on a square.

> Tourist: Entschuldigung, wie komme ich zum Stadtmuseum?
>
> Frau: Zum Stadtmuseum? Das ist nicht weit. Gehen Sie hier geradeaus bis zur Ampel.
>
> Tourist: Bis zur Ampel, okay.
>
> Frau: An der Ampel biegen Sie links ab. Dann nehmen Sie die zweite Straße rechts.
>
> Tourist: Die zweite Straße rechts. Und dann?
>
> Frau: Dann sehen Sie eine Kirche. Das Museum ist direkt gegenüber.
>
> Tourist: Kann ich zu Fuß gehen?
>
> Frau: Ja, natürlich. Das sind nur zehn Minuten.
>
> Tourist: Gibt es dort auch ein Café?
>
> Frau: Ja, das Museum hat ein Café. Es ist sehr schön.
>
> Tourist: Super, vielen Dank!
>
> Frau: Bitte schön!

*Tourist: Excuse me, how do I get to the city museum? — Woman: To the city museum? It's not far. Go
straight ahead as far as the traffic lights. — Tourist: As far as the lights, OK. — Woman: At the
lights you turn left. Then take the second street on the right. — Tourist: The second street on the
right. And then? — Woman: Then you'll see a church. The museum is directly opposite. — Tourist: Can I
walk? — Woman: Yes, of course. It's only ten minutes. — Tourist: Is there a café there too? — Woman:
Yes, the museum has a café. It's very nice. — Tourist: Great, thanks a lot! — Woman: You're welcome!*

Notice that the woman mixes imperatives (*Gehen Sie*, *nehmen Sie*) with plain statements (*An der
Ampel biegen Sie links ab*). Both are normal ways to give directions.

## A text message: the way to Lena's flat

> Hallo Tom! Unser Haus findest du ganz leicht. Nimm am Platz die erste Straße rechts. Geh dann
> geradeaus bis zur Ecke. Dort ist eine Apotheke. Unser Haus ist gegenüber, Nummer 12. Sei bitte
> pünktlich! Bis später, Lena

*Hi Tom! You'll find our house really easily. At the square, take the first street on the right.
Then go straight ahead as far as the corner. There's a pharmacy there. Our house is opposite,
number 12. Please be on time! See you later, Lena*

## Pronunciation

- *geradeaus*: the stress falls on the last part: ge-ra-de-AUS.
- *Café*: stress on the second syllable, ka-FEE, as in French. *Museum* is mu-ZE-um, and
  *Apotheke* is a-po-TE-ke.
- *Straße*: long *a*; the *ß* is a sharp *s*.
- *gegenüber*: ge-gen-Ü-ber; round your lips for *ü*.
- *rechts*: the *ch* is the soft *ich* sound, then *ts*: [rɛçts]. Don't let it become "rex".

## Common mistakes

- Keeping du in the imperative. *Geh du links!* sounds odd; say *Geh links!* or *Bieg links
  ab!* The pronoun appears only for strong emphasis.
- Dropping Sie. *Gehen geradeaus!* is wrong; you need *Gehen Sie geradeaus!* (An infinitive on
  its own, *Bitte nicht rauchen*, is used only on signs and in instructions.)
- Adding an umlaut. *Fähr langsam!* is wrong. The a → ä verbs drop the umlaut: *Fahr langsam!*
- Forgetting the e → i change. *Nehm den Bus!* is a very common mistake. Say *Nimm den Bus!*
- Leaving -st on the verb. *Nimmst den Bus!* is a statement missing its subject, not an
  imperative.
- Putting the prefix in front. *Abbiegen Sie links!* is wrong; say *Biegen Sie links ab!*
- Using bist for sein. *Bist ruhig!* is wrong. The imperative of *sein* is *Sei ruhig!*
- Mixing up rechts and richtig. *Rechts* means "right" as a direction; *richtig* means
  "right" as in correct.

## Key points

- Places: learn every one with *der/die/das*. Ask with *Wo ist …?*, *Gibt es hier in der Nähe …?*
  and *Wie komme ich zum (der, das nouns) / zur (die nouns) …?*
- Directions: *links*, *rechts*, *geradeaus*, *die erste/zweite Straße*, *weit*, *nah*,
  *zu Fuß*, *gegenüber*.
- Imperative, verb first: *Gehen Sie!* (keep *Sie*), *Geh!* (*du* form without *du* and *-st*),
  *Geht!* (*ihr* form without *ihr*).
- In the *du* imperative, e → i/ie stays (*nimm, lies*), a → ä goes (*fahr*), and *-t/-d* stems
  keep *-e* (*warte*).
- *sein*: *Sei! Seid! Seien Sie!* Separable prefixes go to the end: *Biegen Sie links ab!*
- Add *bitte* to sound polite.
