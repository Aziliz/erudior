## What this lesson is for

So far most of your sentences have been short and single: *Ich bin müde. Ich gehe nach
Hause.* That is correct, but it sounds a bit like a robot. In this lesson you learn five
small words that join two sentences into one, and one word that links them in a different
way. Then you put everything to work in two practical skills: writing a short message or
e-mail (to a friend, or to someone you don't know), and filling in a form. Both are tasks
you meet in daily life in a German-speaking country, and both are part of the writing
section of the A1 exam (lesson 24).

## Joining sentences: und, oder, aber, denn, sondern

A **conjunction** is a word that links words or sentences. The five in this lesson are
easy for one important reason: they don't change the word order. They stand in front of
the second sentence in a slot outside the sentence, called **position zero**, and the
sentence after them stays exactly as it was, with the verb in position 2.

| conjunction | meaning | example |
|---|---|---|
| und | and | *Ich koche, und du räumst auf.* |
| oder | or | *Kommst du mit, oder bleibst du hier?* |
| aber | but | *Das Hotel ist alt, aber es ist schön.* |
| denn | because, as | *Ich bleibe zu Hause, denn ich bin krank.* |
| sondern | but (rather), instead | *Er kommt nicht heute, sondern morgen.* |

Look at the second half of each example. *Du räumst auf*, *bleibst du hier*, *es ist schön*,
*ich bin krank*: these are complete normal sentences, as if the conjunction weren't there.

- *Ich habe Hunger, aber ich habe kein Geld.* — I'm hungry, but I have no money.
- *Wir können ins Kino gehen, oder wir sehen zu Hause einen Film.* — We can go to the
  cinema, or we can watch a film at home.
- *Ich lerne Deutsch, denn meine Freundin kommt aus Österreich.* — I'm learning German
  because my girlfriend is from Austria.
- *Sie hat die Prüfung bestanden, und jetzt feiert sie.* — She passed the exam, and now
  she's celebrating.

### Leaving words out

When both halves have the same subject, you can drop it after *und*, *oder* and *aber*:

- *Ich stehe auf und dusche.* — I get up and shower.
- *Ich lese am Abend oder höre Musik.* — In the evening I read or listen to music.

Don't do this with *denn*; it always needs a full sentence after it.

### denn: the A1 way to say "because"

*Denn* gives a reason. It always comes second, after the thing it explains: *Ich komme
nicht, denn ich muss arbeiten.* It can't stand alone as the answer to *Warum?* For that,
Germans use *weil*, which sends the verb to the end and comes at A2. For now, answer with
a full sentence: *Warum kommst du nicht? — Ich muss arbeiten.*

### sondern: not this, but that

*Sondern* corrects something negative. You need *nicht* or *kein* in the first half, and
then *sondern* gives the right information. If the first half is not negative, use *aber*.

- *Das ist kein Tee, sondern Kaffee.* — That's not tea, it's coffee.
- *Ich wohne nicht in Berlin, sondern in Hamburg.* — I don't live in Berlin but in Hamburg.
- *Das Zimmer ist klein, aber hell.* — The room is small but bright. (no negative, so
  *aber*)

### Commas

Put a comma before *aber*, *denn* and *sondern*. Before *und* and *oder* a comma is
optional when they join two complete sentences, and there is none when they join single
words: *Brot und Butter*, *Tee oder Kaffee*.

## deshalb: a preview

*Deshalb* (that's why, so, therefore) also links two ideas, but it works differently. It is
not a conjunction but an adverb, so it takes position 1 itself. The verb comes straight
after it, and the subject moves behind the verb, like after *heute* or *dann*.

- *Ich bin krank. Deshalb komme ich nicht.* — I'm ill. That's why I'm not coming.
- *Der Bus ist nicht gekommen, deshalb bin ich zu Fuß gegangen.* — The bus didn't come, so
  I walked.

Compare: *…, denn ich bin krank* (reason, normal order) but *Deshalb komme ich nicht*
(result, verb first). Using *deshalb* correctly in a message shows good control of word
order, but you can always say the same thing with *denn*.

## Writing a short message

Personal messages and e-mails in German follow a simple frame.

| | informal (du) | formal (Sie) |
|---|---|---|
| opening | *Liebe Anna,* / *Lieber Tom,* / *Hallo Anna,* | *Sehr geehrte Frau Weber,* / *Sehr geehrter Herr Klein,* |
| unknown person | — | *Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,* |
| closing | *Viele Grüße* / *Liebe Grüße* / *Bis bald!* | *Mit freundlichen Grüßen* |
| "your" | *dein, deine* | *Ihr, Ihre* |

Some rules that make your message look right:

- *Liebe* is for a woman, *Lieber* for a man. In the formal opening it's *Sehr geehrte*
  for a woman and *Sehr geehrter* for a man.
- After the opening put a comma, and start the next line with a small letter (unless the
  word is a noun or *Sie*): *Liebe Anna, vielen Dank für …*
- No comma after the closing; just put your name on the next line.
- Stay with one form: if you start with *Liebe Anna*, write *du* and *dein* throughout.
  To a stranger, an office or a landlord, use *Sie* and *Ihr*.

### What goes in the message

A good short message has a clear purpose and answers the points it must cover. A typical
pattern is: say why you are writing, give the information or reason, ask a question or
make a suggestion, and close. About 30 words is enough.

Here is an informal message that says no to an invitation (an *Absage*):

> Liebe Julia,
>
> vielen Dank für die Einladung! Leider kann ich am Samstag nicht zur Party kommen, denn
> meine Mutter hat Geburtstag. Hast du am Sonntag Zeit? Wir können zusammen frühstücken.
>
> Viele Grüße
> Marco

Translation: Dear Julia, thanks a lot for the invitation! Unfortunately I can't come to the
party on Saturday, because it's my mother's birthday. Are you free on Sunday? We could have
breakfast together. Best wishes, Marco.

And a formal one that cancels an appointment (*einen Termin absagen*):

> Sehr geehrte Frau Berger,
>
> ich habe am Dienstag um 10 Uhr einen Termin bei Ihnen. Leider kann ich nicht kommen, denn
> ich bin krank. Haben Sie am Freitag Zeit? Bitte schicken Sie mir eine Nachricht.
>
> Mit freundlichen Grüßen
> Paul Novak

Translation: Dear Ms Berger, I have an appointment with you on Tuesday at 10 o'clock.
Unfortunately I can't come, because I'm ill. Do you have time on Friday? Please send me a
message. Yours sincerely, Paul Novak.

Notice how both use *denn* for the reason and a question to keep things moving. Useful
building blocks:

- *Vielen Dank für deine Nachricht.* — Thanks for your message.
- *Leider habe ich keine Zeit.* — Unfortunately I don't have time.
- *Ich habe ein Problem.* — I have a problem.
- *Kannst du mir bitte antworten?* — Can you answer me, please?
- *Ich freue mich auf deine Antwort.* — I look forward to your answer.
- *Bis bald!* — See you soon!

## Filling in a form

Forms (*Formulare*, lesson 15) ask for the same facts again and again. Learn the field
names and you can fill in almost any form, from a course registration to a hotel check-in.

| field | meaning | example answer |
|---|---|---|
| Vorname | first name | Elif |
| Familienname / Nachname | surname | Kaya |
| Geburtsdatum | date of birth | 14.03.1995 |
| Geburtsort | place of birth | Izmir |
| Staatsangehörigkeit | nationality | türkisch |
| Familienstand | marital status | ledig / verheiratet / geschieden |
| Geschlecht | sex, gender | männlich / weiblich / divers |
| Adresse (Straße, Hausnummer, PLZ, Ort) | address | Bergstraße 12, 50667 Köln |
| Telefonnummer, E-Mail | | |
| Datum, Unterschrift | date, signature | |

Points to watch:

- Dates are written day, month, year with full stops: *14.03.1995* is 14 March 1995.
- *Staatsangehörigkeit* is usually answered with an adjective: *deutsch*, *amerikanisch*,
  *türkisch*. *PLZ* is short for *Postleitzahl*.
- *Ledig* means single (never married), *verheiratet* married, *geschieden* divorced (lesson
  6). *Divers* is the third official option for gender.
- The *Unterschrift* is your signature, written by hand; *unterschreiben* is to sign.

## Dialogue: help with a course registration

Elif wants to register for a German course and asks at the language school office.

> Elif: Guten Tag. Ich möchte mich für den Kurs am Abend anmelden.
>
> Frau Roth: Gern. Hier ist das Formular. Haben Sie eine Frage?
>
> Elif: Ja. Was ist der Familienstand?
>
> Frau Roth: Sind Sie verheiratet oder ledig?
>
> Elif: Ich bin nicht verheiratet, sondern ledig.
>
> Frau Roth: Gut, dann kreuzen Sie hier „ledig“ an. Und unten ist die Unterschrift.
>
> Elif: Wann ist die Prüfung? Ich muss sie unbedingt bestehen, denn ich brauche das Zertifikat.
>
> Frau Roth: Im Juni. Aber keine Sorge, Sie haben genug Zeit!

Translation:

> Elif: Hello. I'd like to sign up for the evening course.
>
> Frau Roth: Of course. Here's the form. Do you have a question?
>
> Elif: Yes. What is "Familienstand"?
>
> Frau Roth: Are you married or single?
>
> Elif: I'm not married, I'm single.
>
> Frau Roth: Good, then tick "ledig" here. And the signature goes at the bottom.
>
> Elif: When is the exam? I really have to pass it, because I need the certificate.
>
> Frau Roth: In June. But don't worry, you have enough time!

Notes: *sich anmelden* means "to register, to sign up"; *unbedingt* is "absolutely, really";
*keine Sorge* is "don't worry"; *ankreuzen* (to tick, to put a cross) comes in lesson 24.

## Pronunciation

- *Staatsangehörigkeit* is long but regular: STAATS-an-ge-hö-rig-keit. Stress the first
  part.
- *Unterschrift*: stress on the first syllable, UN-ter-schrift.
- *geehrte* has two separate e sounds: ge-EHR-te.
- *Grüße* has a long *ü* and *ß*: say "ee" with rounded lips.

## Common mistakes

- Putting the verb at the end after *denn*. Not *…, denn ich krank bin*, but *…, denn ich
  bin krank*.
- Normal order after *deshalb*. Not *Deshalb ich komme nicht*, but *Deshalb komme ich
  nicht*.
- Using *sondern* without a negative, or *aber* to correct a negative: *Ich trinke keinen
  Tee, sondern Kaffee.*
- Mixing *du* and *Sie* in one message, or writing *Lieber Anna*. Match the ending to the
  person: *Liebe Anna, Lieber Tom*.
- A capital letter after the opening: *Liebe Julia, Vielen Dank* is wrong; write *vielen
  Dank*.
- Writing dates the American way. 03.04. is the third of April, not March fourth.

## Key points

- *Und, oder, aber, denn, sondern* stand in position zero: the verb stays in position 2.
- *Denn* gives a reason; *sondern* corrects a negative (*nicht/kein … sondern*).
- *Deshalb* is in position 1, so the verb follows it directly.
- Informal messages: *Liebe/Lieber …,* → *Viele Grüße*. Formal: *Sehr geehrte/geehrter
  …,* → *Mit freundlichen Grüßen*.
- Forms: *Vorname, Familienname, Geburtsdatum, Geburtsort, Staatsangehörigkeit,
  Familienstand, Geschlecht, Unterschrift*.
