## What this lesson is for

So far you've used verbs with *ich*, *du* and *Sie*, plus *er/sie ist*. This lesson completes
the picture: all the people a verb can go with, the endings for every one of them, the full
forms of *sein*, and the two rules of German word order that you'll use in every sentence
from now on: the verb comes second in a statement, and first in a yes/no question. Once you
have these, you can talk about what people do, and ask about it.

## Personal pronouns

A **personal pronoun** is a short word that stands for a person or thing: I, you, she, we…

| | Singular | | Plural | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st person | *ich* | I | *wir* | we |
| 2nd person | *du* | you (informal, one person) | *ihr* | you (informal, two or more) |
| 3rd person | *er* / *sie* / *es* | he / she / it | *sie* | they |
| formal | *Sie* | you (formal, one or more) | *Sie* | you (formal) |

Three things to notice:

- German has a separate plural "you": *ihr* is what you say to two or more friends, like
  "you guys" or "y'all". Formal *Sie* is the same for one person or many.
- *sie* has three jobs. Lowercase *sie* means "she" or "they"; capital *Sie* means formal
  "you". The verb ending tells "she" apart from "they": *sie wohnt* (she lives), *sie wohnen*
  (they live).
- *er*, *sie* and *es* also mean "it", depending on the noun's gender (Lesson 5). For people,
  *er* is he and *sie* is she.

## Regular verb endings

To use a verb in a sentence you **conjugate** it: you take its **stem** (the infinitive minus
*-en*) and add the **ending** that matches the subject. *machen* (to do, to make) has the stem
*mach-*:

| | Ending | machen | wohnen | spielen (to play) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ich | -e | *mache* | *wohne* | *spiele* |
| du | -st | *machst* | *wohnst* | *spielst* |
| er / sie / es | -t | *macht* | *wohnt* | *spielt* |
| wir | -en | *machen* | *wohnen* | *spielen* |
| ihr | -t | *macht* | *wohnt* | *spielt* |
| sie / Sie | -en | *machen* | *wohnen* | *spielen* |

Memory hook: say the endings as a chant, *e, st, t, en, t, en*. Two pairs look the same: the
*wir* and *sie/Sie* forms are both the infinitive, and the *er* and *ihr* forms both end in
*-t*. The pronoun tells you which one you mean.

The **present tense** covers all of English "I play", "I'm playing" and "I do play". There's no
"-ing" form in German: *Ich lerne Deutsch* is both "I learn German" and "I'm learning German".
With a time word it can also mean the future: *Heute spielen wir Tennis.* (We're playing tennis
today.)

## Stems ending in -t or -d

If the stem ends in *-t* or *-d*, German adds an *e* before *-st* and *-t*, because *arbeitt*
or *antwortst* can't be pronounced clearly:

| | arbeiten (to work) | antworten (to answer) |
|---|---|---|
| ich | *arbeite* | *antworte* |
| du | *arbeitest* | *antwortest* |
| er / sie / es | *arbeitet* | *antwortet* |
| wir | *arbeiten* | *antworten* |
| ihr | *arbeitet* | *antwortet* |
| sie / Sie | *arbeiten* | *antworten* |

*Er arbeitet hier.* — He works here.
*Antwortest du?* — Are you answering?

## Stems ending in -s, -ß or -z

If the stem ends in an *s* sound (*-s*, *-ß*, *-z*), the *du* form adds only *-t*, so it looks
like the *er* form: *du heißt*, *er heißt*. The same happens with *tanzen* (to dance): *du
tanzt*.

## sein in full

| | sein (to be) |
|---|---|
| ich | *bin* |
| du | *bist* |
| er / sie / es | *ist* |
| wir | *sind* |
| ihr | *seid* |
| sie / Sie | *sind* |

Don't confuse *ihr seid* (you are) with *seit* (since), which sounds the same. *ist* is easy to
remember: it's English "is" with a *t*.

*Wir sind hier.* — We're here.
*Seid ihr aus Berlin?* — Are you (all) from Berlin?
*Sie sind jetzt in Wien.* — They're in Vienna now. / You're in Vienna now.

## A few verbs to know

*gehen* (to go, on foot), *kommen* (to come), *hören* (to hear, to listen to), *fragen* (to
ask), *sagen* (to say), *lernen* (to learn), *studieren* (to study at university), *leben*
(to live, to be alive) and *verstehen* (to understand) are all regular in the present tense.

*lernen* vs *studieren*: *lernen* is learning anything, including revising for a test;
*studieren* is studying a subject at a university. *Ich studiere Biologie* (I'm a biology
student), but *Ich lerne Deutsch* (I'm learning German).

*wohnen* vs *leben*: *wohnen* is where your home is (*Ich wohne in der Goethestraße*), *leben*
is living in a wider sense (*Wir leben in Deutschland. Er lebt allein.* — He lives alone).

## Rule 1: the verb comes second

In a German **statement**, the conjugated verb is always the second element. The first element
can be the subject, but it can also be a time word, a place or an object. When something else
comes first, the subject moves to just after the verb:

| 1 | 2 (verb) | rest |
|---|---|---|
| *Ich* | *arbeite* | *heute in Köln.* |
| *Heute* | *arbeite* | *ich in Köln.* |
| *In Köln* | *arbeite* | *ich heute.* |

All three mean "I'm working in Cologne today". This is the **verb-second rule**, and it's the
biggest difference from English word order. English says "Today I work"; German must say
*Heute arbeite ich*. Starting with a time word is very common and sounds natural:

*Jetzt lernen wir.* — Now we're studying.
*Oft hört sie Musik.* — She often listens to music.
*Hier wohnt Familie Klein.* — The Klein family lives here.

Adverbs such as *oft* (often), *immer* (always) and *gern* usually come right after the verb:
*Ich spiele oft Tennis. Er arbeitet immer.* *gern* is how German says "like doing": *Ich lerne
gern Deutsch.* (I like learning German.) *Wir spielen gern.* (We like playing.)

## Rule 2: questions

For a **yes/no question**, put the verb first and the subject second:

*Du wohnst hier.* → *Wohnst du hier?* — Do you live here?
*Er arbeitet heute.* → *Arbeitet er heute?* — Is he working today?
*Ihr versteht das.* → *Versteht ihr das?* — Do you (all) understand that?

There's no "do" in German questions. Just swap the verb to the front.

For a W-question, the question word comes first and the verb second, exactly as in Lesson 2.
Three new question words:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| *was* | what | *Was machst du heute?* — What are you doing today? |
| *warum* | why | *Warum lernst du Deutsch?* — Why are you learning German? |
| *wann* | when | *Wann arbeitet Lara?* — When does Lara work? |

*wann* only asks questions about time. It looks like English "when" but isn't used for
"when I was young"; that's a different word you'll meet later.

*hier* (here), *dort* (there), *jetzt* (now) and *heute* (today) are useful in answers:
*Wo ist Max? — Dort.* *Wann lernst du? — Jetzt.*

## Dialogue: a new flatmate

> Lukas: Hallo! Bist du Sara? Ich bin Lukas. Ich wohne hier.
>
> Sara: Hallo, Lukas! Ja, ich bin Sara. Was machst du? Studierst du?
>
> Lukas: Nein, ich arbeite. Ich bin Koch. Und du?
>
> Sara: Ich studiere Musik. Ich spiele Klavier.
>
> Lukas: Toll! Spielst du oft?
>
> Sara: Ja, immer. Heute spiele ich auch. Warum fragst du?
>
> Lukas: Ich höre gern Musik. Und Jonas und Mira, sie spielen Gitarre.
>
> Sara: Wann sind Jonas und Mira hier?
>
> Lukas: Jetzt sind sie dort, im Park. Gehen wir?

Hi! Are you Sara? I'm Lukas. I live here. — Hi, Lukas! Yes, I'm Sara. What do you do? Are you a
student? — No, I work. I'm a cook. And you? — I study music. I play the piano. — Great! Do you
play often? — Yes, all the time. I'm playing today too. Why do you ask? — I like listening to
music. And Jonas and Mira, they play the guitar. — When are Jonas and Mira here? — Right now
they're over there, in the park. Shall we go?

(*der Koch* is a cook, *das Klavier* a piano, *die Gitarre* a guitar, *toll* means "great", and
*im Park* "in the park". Notice: no article before a job: *Ich bin Koch*.)

## Pronunciation

- *arbeiten*: "AR-by-ten"; *ei* as in "eye".
- *verstehen*: stress on *STE*; *st* = "sht" because it starts the root.
- *studieren*: "shtoo-DEE-ren"; *-ieren* verbs are stressed on *ie*.
- *warum*: stress on *RUM*: "va-ROOM".
- *ihr seid*: "eer zite"; same sound as *seit*.
- *jetzt*: *j* = y, *tzt* is one quick "tst": "yetst".

## Common mistakes

- English word order after a time word: *Heute ich arbeite.* Correct: *Heute arbeite ich.*
- Adding a helper verb the way English adds "do": *Tust du hier wohnen?* Just put the verb
  first: *Wohnst du hier?*
- Using *-st* with *ihr*: *ihr wohnst*. The *ihr* ending is *-t*: *ihr wohnt*.
- Forgetting the extra *e*: *er arbeitt*, *du antwortst*. Correct: *er arbeitet*, *du
  antwortest*.
- *ihr sind*, *wir seid*. Correct: *wir sind*, *ihr seid*.
- Translating "I am learning" word for word: *Ich bin lernen.* German uses one verb: *Ich lerne.*
- Using *studieren* for school homework. Say *Ich lerne* for revising.

## Key points

- Pronouns: *ich, du, er/sie/es, wir, ihr, sie/Sie*.
- Endings: *-e, -st, -t, -en, -t, -en*; stems in *-t/-d* add *e* (*arbeitest*); stems in
  *-s/-ß/-z* take only *-t* with *du* (*heißt*).
- *sein*: *bin, bist, ist, sind, seid, sind*.
- One German present form covers "I play", "I'm playing" and "I do play".
- Statements: the verb is second; if something else comes first, the subject follows the verb.
- Yes/no questions: the verb comes first. W-questions: question word, then verb.
- *gern* after the verb means "like doing".
