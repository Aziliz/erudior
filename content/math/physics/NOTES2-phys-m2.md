## For a reviewer
- Stopping table uses t_r = 1.5 s and 7.0 m/s², labeled illustrative. "US highway designers assume
  2.5 s and 3.4 m/s²" is the AASHTO stopping-sight-distance basis (11.2 ft/s²); signal timing "about
  1 s and 3 m/s²" is the ITE yellow-interval formula (10 ft/s²). The dilemma-zone model (clear the
  intersection before red) is labeled as a physics model, not traffic law.
- Example 1 of 06h1 has the police car reach 50 m/s (180 km/h); this is the point of Example 2,
  which adds a top speed.
- History: Stevin and de Groot (1586, about 30 ft, Delft, one ball about ten times heavier); Viviani
  as the source of the Pisa story; Apollo 15 (David Scott, 1971). Aristotle's view is labeled a
  simplified summary.
- g from about 9.78 (equator) to 9.83 m/s² (poles); g_n = 9.80665 m/s² exactly. Rolling ball
  a = (5/7) g sin θ matches lesson 17 (a = g sin θ/(1 + c), c = 2/5).
- Shot-put remark ("well below 45°") is qualitative.
