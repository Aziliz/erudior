# Biology (science-biology): scope research (2026-10-23)

## Scope
One high school credit (grades 9–11), covering what US standards ask of a first-year
biology course, with honors-level depth in later lessons.

- NGSS / state standards built on it (HS-LS1 to HS-LS4): structure and function, cell
  division and differentiation, homeostasis and feedback, photosynthesis and respiration as
  matter and energy flow, ecosystems (carrying capacity, energy pyramids, carbon cycle,
  biodiversity and human impact), inheritance and variation (DNA → proteins, meiosis,
  mutation, environment), natural selection and evolution (common ancestry evidence, the
  four factors, adaptation, extinction and speciation). Source: NJ Model Science Curriculum
  9–12 scope and sequence, https://www.nj.gov/education/standards/science/accessible_science9to12.shtml
- Texas TEKS Biology (19 TAC §112.42, adopted 2020) adds explicitly: biomolecules and
  function, prokaryote vs eukaryote, viruses vs cells, cell cycle and cancer, DNA
  replication, gene expression, mutations, PCR / gel electrophoresis / genetic engineering,
  monohybrid and dihybrid crosses with incomplete dominance, codominance, sex linkage and
  multiple alleles, fossil/biogeography/homology evidence, gradualism vs punctuated
  patterns, drift / gene flow / mutation / recombination, enzymes, animal systems
  (regulation, nutrient absorption, reproduction, defense), plant structures (transport,
  reproduction, response), carbon and nitrogen cycles. Source:
  https://regulations.justia.com/states/texas/title-19/part-2/chapter-112/subchapter-c/section-112-42
- Investigation skills (TEKS strands 1–4, NGSS practices): experimental design,
  variables and controls, data analysis, models, hypothesis vs theory vs law.

## Not in scope
Wet labs (a home learner can't be required to have equipment; lessons describe the classic
experiments and use their data instead). Dissection. AP Biology depth beyond what an honors
first-year course covers.
