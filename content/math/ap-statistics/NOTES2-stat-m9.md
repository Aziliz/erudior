**Honors lessons** (3: two directions for chi-square, one for regression)
- `lessons/39h1-chi-square-theory.md` (stat-m9-h-chi-theory, "Where the chi-square test comes
  from", 45 min, ~1,800 words): χ²_k as a sum of k squared standard normals (mean k, variance 2k,
  3.841 = 1.96²); proof that two-category GOF χ² = z² exactly; Pearson's k − 1 result (stated, not
  proved, and labeled as such); proof that E(χ²) = k − 1 exactly under H0; df = k − 1 − m for
  estimated parameters (Fisher 1922/1924), and (r − 1)(c − 1) derived from it; Chernoff–Lehmann
  caution (labeled simplified); why expected counts ≥ 5, with Cochran's more lenient rule mentioned
  as "often attributed". Examples: 65 of 250 clips (χ² = 5.625 = 2.372²); E(χ²) for the die test;
  binomial fit to defects per box of 4 with p̂ estimated (χ² = 8.79, df 1, p = 0.0030; also df 2,
  p = 0.012). Plot of χ² densities (1, 3, 6, 10 df).
- `lessons/39h2-chi-square-follow-up.md` (stat-m9-h-chi-followup, "After the chi-square test:
  residuals, effect size and exact tests", 45 min, ~1,800 words): adjusted residuals (Haberman
  1973) with the multiple-cell caution; Cramér's V (1946) and φ, χ² ∝ n, Cohen's conventions
  labeled as conventions; Fisher's exact test via the hypergeometric, lady tasting tea (1935).
  Examples: all nine adjusted residuals for the transit survey; V = 0.247 (unchanged when counts
  ×10) and a 51% vs 49%, n = 20,000 survey (p = 0.005, φ = 0.02); brake pads 1/10 vs 6/10
  (one-sided Fisher p = 0.0286, two-sided 0.057, invalid χ² p = 0.019). Mermaid "complete
  analysis" flowchart; hypergeometric bar plot.
- `lessons/39h3-regression-intervals.md` (stat-m9-h-reg-intervals, "Predicting with a regression
  line: confidence and prediction intervals", 45 min, ~1,500 words): SE of the mean response
  (derived from ŷ = ȳ + b(x* − x̄); uncorrelatedness of ȳ and b stated without proof), prediction
  SE, confidence and prediction bands, limit ±1.96σ, why Normal and Equal SD matter more for
  prediction, extrapolation, and t = r√(n − 2)/√(1 − r²) derived from b/SE_b. Examples: rail wear
  n = 12 at x* = 30 (CI 9.34–11.66, PI 8.18–12.82) and at x̄; the core lesson's five-point
  training data at x* = 3; correlation tests (r = 0.8, n = 5 matches lesson 39's t = 2.31;
  r = 0.3, n = 50, p = 0.034). Plot of both bands.
