## For a reviewer to double-check
- Probability plot correlation cutoffs (0.919, 0.951, 0.977, 0.987 for n = 10, 20, 50, 100) are
  from my own simulation (200,000 normal samples each, $(i - 0.5)/n$ positions); the lesson says
  so, and says Filliben's 1975 table gives nearly the same values (his table uses different
  plotting positions; from memory his n = 10, 5% value is about 0.918, which I could not
  look up here, so please confirm).
- Shapiro–Wilk (1965) is described only as "built on a closely related idea."
- Capability conventions: "many manufacturers ask for $C_{pk} \ge 1.33$; some require 1.67 or more
  for new or safety-critical parts," explicitly called industry conventions. Six Sigma: Motorola,
  1980s; 3.4 per million from the 1.5σ shift, labeled a debated convention. Shewhart control
  charts at Bell Telephone Laboratories in the 1920s.
- Example 4 of 10h2 (9 defects found vs 1.3 expected) is labeled illustrative.
- The labeled QQ-parts figure says "at z = 0: the center" rather than "the mean", because the
  outlier pulls the mean (51.1) above where the line crosses (50).
