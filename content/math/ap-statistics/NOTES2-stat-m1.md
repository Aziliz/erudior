## Added

**Honors lessons** (3; the module has six core lessons and three distinct directions worth teaching)
- `lessons/06h1-density-histograms.md` (stat-m1-h-density, "Density histograms, bin widths and
  smooth curves", 45 min, ~2,000 words incl. figure JSON): unequal bins and the density scale
  (area = proportion), bin-width rules (Sturges 1926, Scott 1979, Freedman–Diaconis 1981),
  starting-point and boundary effects, kernel density estimates (Rosenblatt 1956, Parzen 1962),
  bandwidth, Silverman's rule of thumb, boundary problem. Examples: density of an unequal age bin
  and an area estimate for ages 30–40; three bin-width rules for n = 200; KDE value at a point.
- `lessons/06h2-robust-statistics.md` (stat-m1-h-robust, "Robust statistics: resistance made
  precise", 45 min, ~1,700 words): mean minimizes squared distance (proof by the identity
  Σ(x−c)² = Σ(x−x̄)² + n(x̄−c)²), median minimizes absolute distance, midrange minimizes the
  largest distance; breakdown points; trimmed and winsorized means; MAD and 1.4826·MAD, IQR/1.349;
  masking; modified z-score (Iglewicz and Hoaglin 1993). Examples: identity check; a 9999
  data-entry error; MAD of the core lesson's delivery times; three outlier rules applied to 30.
- `lessons/06h3-grouped-combined-data.md` (stat-m1-h-combining, "Summaries from grouped and
  combined data", 45 min, ~1,900 words): exact summaries from frequency tables; grouped-data
  estimates (midpoint SD, median and quartiles by linear interpolation, Sheppard's correction
  mentioned and labeled as not used); combining group means and SDs, within + between
  decomposition; medians can't be combined; running-mean update and Welford's algorithm (1962).

## For the reviewer to double-check
- Every number in the honors examples and every computed question was recomputed in Python
  (scipy for the normal kernel and 0.6745 / 1.4826 / 1.349 constants).
- Honors 2 breakdown table: finite-sample values are stated as "about" (the median's exact
  finite-sample breakdown depends on n); "one value, 1/n" for mean/SD/range.
- Honors 1 says R's `hist` uses Sturges' rule by default (true as of R 4.x: `breaks = "Sturges"`).
- Honors 3: grouped quartiles by interpolation (n/4, 3n/4 positions) are labeled as possibly
  differing from the course's median-of-halves quartiles of raw data.
- q05 (star rating) follows lesson 01's own classification of a 1–5 rating as ordinal; the
  explanation acknowledges such ratings are often averaged.
- q20 order: mode 3 < median 4 < trimmed mean 4.4 < mean 6 (strict, so one order only).
- Possible overlap: honors 1's kernel density estimate touches density curves (stat-m2) and
  honors 2's MAD/modified z-score touches outliers and z-scores (stat-m2). I kept both tied to
  module 1's displays and resistance; the stat-m2 and stat-h1 writers may want to cross-reference.
