## For a reviewer
- Statements given without proof and labeled as such: the general graph formula for K, the Theorema
  Egregium, uniqueness of exterior potential flow (it needs the far-field condition and zero
  circulation, which the lesson says), and the global Gauss–Bonnet theorem (a sketch that assumes a
  geodesic triangulation).
- Simplifications that the lessons label: the uniform-density Earth (the real Earth's g rises slightly
  with depth through the mantle; a realistic profile shortens the tunnel trip), sunlight as one plane
  wave, Earnshaw's point-charge/static idealization, and potential flow without viscosity.
- Numbers recomputed in Python: tunnel period 5063 s (R = 6.371e6 m, g = 9.81); Moon one-way 54.2 min;
  c = 2.99792458e8 from the CODATA 2022 μ₀ and ε₀; sunlight E₀ ≈ 1013 V/m and B₀ ≈ 3.38 μT
  (I = 1361 W/m²); Bouguer slab 1.13e-3 m/s² for 1 km at 2700 kg/m³; shell potential integral
  (sympy); ball Poisson check; torus K = cos v/(b(a + b cos v)) and its totals 0 / ±4π (sympy);
  sphere and xy-saddle graph curvatures; a spherical triangle with 0.5° excess has area ≈ 354,000 km².
- History, checked against standard accounts: Newton's *Principia* (1687) shell theorem; Poisson 1813;
  Maxwell 1862 and 1865; Hertz 1887–88; Poynting 1884; Earnshaw 1842; d'Alembert's paradox 1752;
  Euler's curvature theorem (1760s); Gauss 1827 (Theorema Egregium, geodesic triangles); Bonnet 1848;
  Euler's polyhedron formula (1750s). L93 doesn't name who first proved the global Euler-characteristic
  form, which is deliberate.
