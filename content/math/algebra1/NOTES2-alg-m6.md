# Algebra 1 module alg-m6 (Quadratic equations and functions), round 2: writer notes

## Added

**Honors lessons (3)**, `honors: true`, `module: alg-m6`. The module has six core lessons and two
topics clearly worth more time (Vieta, quadratic inequalities), plus a history and golden-ratio lesson
that ties completing the square to its origins.
- `lessons/27h1-sum-product-roots.md` (alg-m6-h-vieta, "Sums and products of roots (Vieta's
  formulas)", 45 min, about 1,900 words of prose): derivation from $(x - r)(x - s)$ and from the
  quadratic formula, when the formulas apply (real roots; a double root counts twice), building
  $x^2 - Sx + P = 0$, when a sum and product are possible ($S^2 - 4P \ge 0$, linked to Lesson 26),
  symmetric expressions table, distance between roots $\sqrt D/|a|$, axis at half the sum, unknown
  coefficients, signs of roots, new equation from old roots. 7 worked examples, a plot, a Mermaid chart.
- `lessons/27h2-quadratic-inequalities.md` (alg-m6-h-quad-inequalities, "Quadratic inequalities",
  45 min, about 1,550 words): why the roots split the line, graph method with an a > 0 / a < 0 table,
  sign chart (SVG), procedure (Mermaid), D < 0 and D = 0 cases, irrational endpoints, the
  "$x > \pm 3$" error and the link to $|x|$, projectile and revenue applications. 6 worked
  examples, 2 plots, 2 number lines.
- `lessons/27h3-quadratics-history.md` (alg-m6-h-history, "Quadratics through history: from Babylon
  to the golden ratio", 45 min, about 1,900 words): BM 13901 #1 ($x^2 + x = \tfrac34$, base 60),
  Euclid II.4 and extreme and mean ratio, Brahmagupta's rule (628), al-Khwarizmi's six types,
  $x^2 + 10x = 39$ with his four-rectangle figure, $x^2 + 21 = 10x$ and his "impossible" case as a
  discriminant test, Viète 1591 and Descartes 1637 (Mermaid timeline), golden ratio, golden
  rectangle (SVG), golden cut of a segment, Fibonacci ratios, and a "fact and fiction" section
  labeling the art/beauty claims as unsupported. 5 worked examples.

## For a reviewer
- 27h3 history: dates are given as "about" where sources differ (Old Babylonian ~1800 BCE,
  al-Khwarizmi ~820). The cut-and-paste reading of BM 13901 (Høyrup) and the "geometric algebra"
  reading of Euclid Book II are labeled as interpretations. al-Khwarizmi's four-rectangle proof of
  $x^2 + 10x = 39$ and his remark that $x^2 + c = bx$ is impossible when $(b/2)^2 < c$ are from his
  text (Rosen translation, via MacTutor). The question h30 problems are "in the style of"
  al-Khwarizmi; "five squares equal eighty" is not claimed to be his.
- 27h1 says the formulas need real roots in this course and mentions that they also hold for
  complex roots (Algebra 2). The signs-of-roots chart assumes $D \ge 0$ before using the product.
- Question q11 / q26 distractors deliberately pair a correct picture name with a wrong discriminant
  sign ("Two real roots (D < 0)", "Tangent line (D > 0)").
- q22 accepts 1.5 (and 3/2 by value); h35 accepts 16.2 and 16.18.
- 27h2 Example 6 reuses Lesson 26's theater revenue; money is written with `\$`.
