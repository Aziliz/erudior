## For the reviewer

- 26h1 names the exchange lemma after Ernst Steinitz, without a date, and calls the dimension formula "sometimes called Grassmann's formula". Both names are standard but were only lightly checked.
- 26h2 proves rank–nullity for linear maps "for finite-dimensional W" (said so in the text); the theorem is stated for finite-dimensional V. It uses "the matrix of a linear transformation" for the general V → W case. The existing glossary term "B-matrix" (lesson 29) covers the operator case, and 26h2 points ahead to it.
- 26h3 treats only real roots in detail; complex roots are mentioned in one sentence ("a real basis can be written with powers of |r| times cosines and sines"). The Casorati test is stated only for two solutions of the same recurrence, where it is an equivalence. Fibonacci gets one sentence (irrational roots, closed formula) without the formula itself, to avoid overlap with eigenvalue material.
- 26h3 calls r² + ar + b = 0 the "auxiliary equation" and notes that many books call it the characteristic equation; the glossary alias is "characteristic equation of a recurrence", so it doesn't clash with the existing "characteristic equation".
- In the label question for the four subspaces (q21), the ovals carry their dimensions (r, n − r, r, m − r) so that Col A and Nul Aᵀ can be told apart; the picture doesn't claim any perpendicularity (that comes in la-m9).
- q05 says "the matrices whose two diagonal entries add to 0" rather than "trace 0", because trace isn't defined until lesson 28.
