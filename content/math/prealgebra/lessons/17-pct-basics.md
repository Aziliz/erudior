## Out of one hundred

Percents are everywhere: sales tax, interest rates, test scores, battery levels, election results. A percent is just a ratio with a fixed second number, 100, which makes different amounts easy to compare. This lesson shows how percents relate to the fractions and decimals of the last two modules, and how to convert among all three. The next lessons use these conversions to solve percent problems.

## What a percent is

**Percent** means "per hundred" (from the Latin *per centum*). The symbol % stands for "out of 100":

$$37\% = \frac{37}{100} = 0.37$$

On a 10-by-10 grid of 100 squares, 37% is 37 shaded squares. 100% is the whole grid, and 0% is none of it. Percents can be greater than 100% (more than one whole: 150% = 1.5) and can be fractional (0.5% = half of one percent = 0.005).

## Percent to decimal and back

Because a percent is "over 100," converting to a decimal means dividing by 100, which moves the decimal point two places *left*:

- $45\% = 0.45$
- $7\% = 0.07$ (not 0.7)
- $125\% = 1.25$
- $0.8\% = 0.008$
- $12.5\% = 0.125$

To go the other way, decimal to percent, multiply by 100: move the point two places *right* and add the % sign.

- $0.62 = 62\%$
- $0.9 = 90\%$
- $2.3 = 230\%$
- $0.004 = 0.4\%$

A way to remember the direction: a percent number is always the *bigger-looking* one (45 vs 0.45), because it counts hundredths.

## Percent to fraction

Write the percent over 100 and simplify.

- $40\% = \dfrac{40}{100} = \dfrac25$
- $175\% = \dfrac{175}{100} = \dfrac74 = 1\dfrac34$

If the percent has a decimal, clear it by multiplying top and bottom by 10 or 100:

$$12.5\% = \frac{12.5}{100} = \frac{125}{1000} = \frac18$$

If the percent has a fraction, divide by 100 by multiplying by $\tfrac{1}{100}$:

$$33\tfrac13\% = \frac{100}{3} \times \frac{1}{100} = \frac13$$

## Fraction to percent

Two methods.

*Method 1: convert to a decimal first, then to a percent.* $\tfrac38 = 3 \div 8 = 0.375 = 37.5\%$.

*Method 2: find an equivalent fraction over 100.* $\tfrac{7}{20} = \tfrac{35}{100} = 35\%$. This is fast when the denominator divides 100 (2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50).

### Example 1

Write $\dfrac{5}{6}$ as a percent.

$5 \div 6 = 0.8333\ldots$, so $\tfrac56 = 83.333\ldots\% = 83\tfrac13\%$. Rounded to the nearest tenth of a percent, 83.3%.

## The conversion triangle

| Fraction | Decimal | Percent |
|---|---|---|
| $\tfrac{1}{100}$ | 0.01 | 1% |
| $\tfrac{1}{20}$ | 0.05 | 5% |
| $\tfrac{1}{10}$ | 0.1 | 10% |
| $\tfrac18$ | 0.125 | 12.5% |
| $\tfrac15$ | 0.2 | 20% |
| $\tfrac14$ | 0.25 | 25% |
| $\tfrac13$ | $0.\overline{3}$ | $33\tfrac13\%$ |
| $\tfrac25$ | 0.4 | 40% |
| $\tfrac12$ | 0.5 | 50% |
| $\tfrac23$ | $0.\overline{6}$ | $66\tfrac23\%$ |
| $\tfrac34$ | 0.75 | 75% |
| 1 | 1 | 100% |

Knowing this table by heart makes mental percent calculations quick: 25% of something is a quarter of it, 20% is a fifth, 10% is a tenth.

### Example 2

Fill in the missing forms: (a) 0.06, (b) $\tfrac{9}{25}$, (c) 240%.

- (a) $0.06 = 6\% = \tfrac{6}{100} = \tfrac{3}{50}$.
- (b) $\tfrac{9}{25} = \tfrac{36}{100} = 0.36 = 36\%$.
- (c) $240\% = 2.4 = \tfrac{240}{100} = \tfrac{12}{5} = 2\tfrac25$.

## Comparing with percents

Percents put different-sized groups on the same scale.

### Example 3

On one quiz Dev scored 17 out of 20. On another he scored 21 out of 25. Which score was better?

$\tfrac{17}{20} = \tfrac{85}{100} = 85\%$ and $\tfrac{21}{25} = \tfrac{84}{100} = 84\%$. The first quiz was slightly better, even though he got more questions right on the second.

### Example 4

A phone battery shows 0.35 of its charge remaining. What percent is used?

Remaining: $0.35 = 35\%$. Used: $100\% - 35\% = 65\%$. Percents of a whole always add to 100%.

## Very large and very small percents

Percents over 100% describe more than the whole. If a town's population is 140% of what it was ten years ago, it is 1.4 times as large: it has grown by 40%. If a stock is worth 300% of its purchase price, it has tripled.

Percents under 1% are common with rates such as interest and taxes. A fee of 0.25% is a quarter of one percent, or 0.0025 as a decimal. On a \$10,000 purchase that is $0.0025 \times 10{,}000 = 25$ dollars. Writing the decimal carefully, two places to the left of the percent's own decimal point, keeps you from being off by a factor of 10 or 100.

### Example 5

Write 0.6% and 260% as decimals and as fractions in simplest form.

- $0.6\% = 0.006 = \tfrac{6}{1000} = \tfrac{3}{500}$.
- $260\% = 2.6 = \tfrac{260}{100} = \tfrac{13}{5} = 2\tfrac35$.

## Common mistakes

- *Moving the point the wrong way* or only one place: $7\% = 0.07$, not 0.7 or 7.0.
- *Treating a percent sign as decoration.* 0.5% is half of one percent, which is 0.005, not 0.5.
- *Forgetting to simplify* the fraction: $60\% = \tfrac35$, not just $\tfrac{60}{100}$.
- *Rounding repeating decimals too early:* $\tfrac23 = 66\tfrac23\%$, about 66.7%, not 66%.
- *Thinking percents can't exceed 100%.* A price that doubles is 200% of the original.

## Key points

- Percent means per hundred: $p\% = \tfrac{p}{100}$.
- Percent → decimal: divide by 100 (point two places left). Decimal → percent: multiply by 100 (point two places right).
- Percent → fraction: over 100, then simplify. Fraction → percent: divide to get a decimal, then multiply by 100.
- Memorize the common equivalents (halves, quarters, fifths, eighths, thirds, tenths).
