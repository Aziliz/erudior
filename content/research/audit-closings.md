## Confirmed facts a reviewer might doubt

- **Broker's closing duty (Rule 6.19 A–E):** the broker with a brokerage relationship is responsible for the closing; the closing statement must be signed by the broker; the broker attends or is reasonably available; a firm-designated substitute shares joint responsibility; there is a good-funds bar on disbursement. Confirmed from the 4 CCR 725-1 ch. 6 text (Cornell LII). The fetched 2026 CREM Ch. 2 PDF text was cut off before Rule 6.10, so Rule 6.19 was read from LII, not from the CREM PDF.
- **C.R.S. 12-10-217(1)(h), (1)(j):** confirmed from CREM Ch. 1 (2026).
- **Property-management license exemption:** C.R.S. 12-10-201(6)(b)(XII) covers apartments and (XIII) covers condominium units. Both apply only to a regularly salaried on-site manager doing customary duties. The owner exemption is (6)(b)(VII). Confirmed from CREM Ch. 1.
- **Trust rules (CREM Ch. 2):** Rule 5.7 sets the deposit deadlines: 5 business days for property-management funds and 3 for other funds. Rule 5.5 requires separate rent and security-deposit accounts. Rule 5.15 requires owner reports within 30 days after month end unless the agreement says otherwise. Rule 5.8 covers owner-held deposits (tenant notice) and the transfer when management changes (60 days, with a 30-day disclosure). CP-15 (CREM Ch. 3) also says no deductions for normal wear and tear and requires a written statement of exact reasons.
- **Closing Instructions (2026 form):** signed by the buyer, seller and closing company only, with no broker signature block. §4.3 says party instructions that conflict with the contract need an Agreement to Amend/Extend. §6 says good funds come before documents are released. §7 says the closing company disburses commissions. §15 covers FIRPTA and Colorado withholding. §18 gives brokers copies.
- **CBS1 2026:** §8.1.1 makes "seller selects and pays for the owner's policy" the default when neither box is checked. §8.1.3 makes OEC Will/Will Not and lets the parties allocate the premium. §12.2 has a box for whether Closing Instructions are executed with the contract. §15.3 has the seller request the status letter at least 14 days before closing and pay its fee. §16.1 prorates to the Closing Date. The tax basis is the calendar year before closing, using the most recent mill levy and assessed valuation. Rents are prorated on amounts actually received or accrued. Security deposits are transferred and the tenants notified. §16.1.3 lists water/sewer, propane and interest on a continuing loan. §16.1.4 makes prorations final. §16.2 credits prepaid assessments to the seller but not reserves. §2.7.3 covers the well form. §10.8 covers the Source of Water acknowledgment and the all-caps warning about nonrenewable ground water. No contract section assigns the documentary fee.
- **Documentary fee:** 1¢ per $100 or major fraction, none at $500 or less. It is imposed on the person offering the deed for recording and collected by the clerk and recorder (C.R.S. 39-13-102). All fee math in s4 and g3 was rechecked ($51.20, $48.50, $45, $38, $52, $43.67, $39.50).
- **TD-1000:** filed with the deed and not recorded. The clerk sends it to the assessor and access is limited. After 30 days' notice the assessor may charge a penalty of $25 or 0.025% of price, whichever is greater (C.R.S. 39-14-102).
- **Good funds:** wires and certified, cashier's or teller's checks count. Up to $500 may be advanced for incidentals (C.R.S. 38-35-125).
- **Race-notice:** C.R.S. 38-35-109 states it is a race-notice statute.
- **Taxes:** they become due Jan 1 of the following year (39-10-102). They can be paid in full by the last day of April or in halves by the last day of February and June 15 (39-10-104.5). DPT confirms the formula, the odd-year reappraisal, the Jan 1 classification date and the appeal path (assessor → county board of equalization → BAA, district court or arbitration).
- **Nonresident withholding (39-22-604.5):** the lesser of 2% of the price or net proceeds. Exempt at $100,000 or less, with the three affirmations, and for foreclosure or deed in lieu to a bank or corporate beneficiary.
- **Special district / metro district disclosures:** 38-35.7-101 (seller's duty, bold text, buyer can sue for damages) and 38-35.7-111 (district website on the Seller's Property Disclosure, districts organized on or after Jan 1, 2000, sales from Jan 1, 2024).
- **Wells:** 38-30-102(3) says the buyer completes the form. The closing service provider submits it within 60 days after closing. Without a closing provider, the buyer files and pays the fee. Exempt-well use limits follow CSU Extension 6.700.
- **Water rights conveyance:** 38-30-102(2), with the ditch-stock exception. Ditch shares are personal property under C.R.S. 7-42-104(4) (per a Colorado Lawyer article). Case law separates the shares from the water right they represent; the lesson's simplification is acceptable.
- **Landlord-tenant:** the two-month deposit cap (38-12-102.5) and habitability timelines (38-12-503: 24-hour response, 24/96-hour remedial action, 96-hour mold containment, hotel or comparable unit with rent still owed) are confirmed. So are the 13-40-104 notice periods (10/5/3 days and the exempt-agreement definition) and the 38-12-510 remedies (actual damages plus the greater of 3× monthly rent or $5,000, plus attorney fees).
- **For-cause eviction (HB24-1098):** confirmed via leg.colorado.gov and the Colorado Judicial FED clinic slides (June 2025). It does not cover tenants with under 12 months. The listed no-fault grounds are right, including sale and refusal of a reasonable new lease. No-fault requires 90 days' notice. Exemptions include short-term rentals, owner-occupied single-family homes, duplexes and triplexes, and employer housing.
- **Fair housing:** the protected-class list in 24-34-502(1)(a)(I) is confirmed. Source of income has separate provisions, the 3-or-fewer-units exemption and the every-applicant credit-check rule. A charge must be filed within 1 year (24-34-504). A private suit is due within 2 years (24-34-505.6). CCRD gives source of income an effective date of 2021 and veteran/military status 2022.
- **Foreclosure:** the sale falls 110–125 days after the NED (215–230 for agricultural property) (38-38-108). The notice of intent to cure is due 15 calendar days before the sale, with payment by noon the day before (38-38-104). Only junior lienors redeem, filing notice within 8 business days (38-38-302). The owner has no post-sale redemption right. The combined notice is published for 5 consecutive weeks (38-38-103).
- **CFPA:** definitions (6-1-1103) cover the broker, attorney and lender exclusions, the short-sale-addendum exclusion and the 30-day "residence in foreclosure" test. A consultant may not take compensation until full performance and may not take a lien, an interest or a power of attorney (6-1-1107). The homeowner may cancel a consultant contract at any time (6-1-1105). The equity-purchase cancellation window is the earlier of midnight on the 3rd business day or noon the day before the sale (6-1-1113). Documents must be returned within 10 days (6-1-1117). The DRE CFPA worksheet confirms the trigger steps and the leaseback/repurchase/assumption → attorney rule. The DRE advisory confirms the broker must not translate the Homeowner Warning Notice.
- **g3 worksheet:** the Commission worksheet (SS61-9-08) has Seller/Buyer/Broker debit/credit pairs, and the listed line items match. All three worked examples (A, B, C) were recomputed and every pair balances. g3-012, 013, 016, 018, 019, 020 and 021 were recomputed and are correct, including their distractor explanations.
- **f4-001..011:** each answer matches the lesson it cites: co-forms-commission-approved (strike-through; 4-year attorney-clause retention), co-cbs1-part1 (New Loan Terms Deadline), co-counterproposals-amend-notices (counteroffer rejects the offer; Agreement to Revive; backup offer; go to the employing broker first), co-listing-disclosures-addenda-financing (transaction-broker default) and g4-hoa-ccioa (fine policy; foreclosure threshold; July 1, 1992).
